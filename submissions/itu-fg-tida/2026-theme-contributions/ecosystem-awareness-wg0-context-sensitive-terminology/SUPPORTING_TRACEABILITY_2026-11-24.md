## Documented antecedents of the terminology contribution

These entries consolidate a terminology line already used in requirements, interfaces and case discussions. Theme 16 supplied the capacity/determinacy and bounded-oversight context; Theme 9 separated an authored bounded search from an assertion of universal absence; Cyril's Theme 6 response preserved action-side indeterminacy, an attested absence of evaluation and evidence-side appraisal rejection as different conditions. Their meanings must not be collapsed into a universal INDETERMINATE state or attributed to EA alone.

Iv�n's [1 September Theme 18 contribution](https://github.com/FG-TIDA/themes/issues/18#issuecomment-5501700348) already distinguished justified determination, residual indeterminacy and context-sensitive risk, as well as epistemic determination and an authorized operational determination. This is a direct antecedent of the present vocabulary.

The original UC20 dossier at 6ce0f55a458bd38ea86e5f54debdc1d3dfbd44a3 and UC21 at aec08fb5b0fac2ee399372370e9ee2a82a2d5779 precede this terminology paper. UC20 contains 26 HAP requirements and 66 test designs, with no execution claimed in that publication. The October-November T28 discussion with Olena and Nelson distinguishes a historical finding, evidence currently sufficient for a decision, and the decision made under authority. It also distinguishes monitored change from a newly discovered condition outside the represented boundary.

UC21 S5, the CDA revisions, the B1/B2 source-quality corrections and Theme 21's evaluator discussion provide further operational uses and counterexamples. Observation-limited indeterminacy in Theme 21 does not by itself denote the EA residual outside the represented decision universe. Justin Flores's November handoff preserves Iv�n's receiving-side continuation but does not establish completed later validation or ongoing co-development by Justin.

The nine definition entries developed in v0.2 are retained; this v0.3 revision strengthens their antecedents and traceability.
