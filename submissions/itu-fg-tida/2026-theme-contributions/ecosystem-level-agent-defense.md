# Intervention sufficiency for ecosystem-level agent defence

> **Public discussion entry:** [FG-TIDA theme issue #13](https://github.com/FG-TIDA/themes/issues/13#issuecomment-5462867033)
>
> **Posted:** 29 August 2026 by `dakleyer`
>
> **Status:** Public GitHub discussion contribution; not a formal ITU input document or adopted text.

Building on the operational-oversight discussion in #16, it may be useful to distinguish detection sufficiency from intervention sufficiency at ecosystem level.

An ecosystem may detect suspicious behaviour, publish an alert or reduce an agent’s reputation while still lacking an authorised and reachable intervention path capable of containing the effects before they propagate.

A shared defensive signal may therefore need to communicate, at minimum:

- the observed condition and the confidence and freshness of the evidence;
- the affected or potentially affected scope;
- the containment action currently available and its effective reach;
- the authority under which that action may be taken;
- the response deadline; and
- the fallback when neither automated containment nor timely human review is available.

This would allow ecosystem participants to determine whether the shared signal can still change the outcome, rather than merely document or recognise it. The bounded standardisation question would be: which signal and intervention properties are minimally necessary for heterogeneous participants to coordinate an effective response, without imposing a common central controller or architecture?
