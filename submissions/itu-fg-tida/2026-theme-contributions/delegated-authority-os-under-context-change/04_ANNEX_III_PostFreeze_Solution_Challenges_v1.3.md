# Annex III — Challenges Exposed by the Case

- **Version:** 1.3
- **Parent case:** [TIDA — Delegated Authority OS under Context Change v0.6](01_TIDA_Delegated_Authority_OS_under_Context_Change_v0.6.md)
- **Status:** Public working post-freeze challenge framework; not an adopted FG-TIDA deliverable.

These are post-freeze solution challenges, not additional design challenges of the case itself. A theme, workstream or architecture does not need to solve the whole case. It should be able to test the challenge dimensions that fall within the scope it claims to cover against the same stable facts. The value of the case is precisely that it has enough depth to expose unresolved seams even when individual components appear to work.

| Challenges 1–7 | Challenges 8–14 |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Authority provenance & current applicability. Can the system establish who conferred authority, on whom, for what purpose and scope, and whether that authority still binds at commitment and action time? This includes origin, standing, expiry, revocation, composition and evidence. | 8. Bounded subdelegation & non-amplification. When an agent delegates to one or more sub-agents, can purpose, scope, time, hard limits, relevant preferences and revocation conditions survive without expansion? Delegation must not manufacture authority that the original principal never granted. |
| 2. Preference fidelity & reviewable decision basis. Can an agent show that its decision still represents the principal's declared preferences, hard limits and permitted trade-offs? The challenge is explainability at the decision basis level—not disclosure of every internal model step. | 9. Multi-principal composition, non-substitution & conflict. Can independently valid citizen, employer, provider, operator and public authorities compose without one silently replacing another? The case tests concurrence, conflict, priority, hold and escalation without assuming one universal hierarchy. |
| 3. Regime, context, escalation & bounded escape path. Can a continuously operating system distinguish normal escalation from an exceptional escape path? Normal escalation requests confirmation, revalidation or a change of authority while the current rules remain in force. A governed escape path temporarily suspends only specified rules or actions when continuing under them could cause harm after an exceptional frame change. The system must identify who or what may detect and declare the emergency, the evidence and thresholds required, the affected scope and segments, what leaves the ordinary frame, what remains valid, and the conditions for review and return. For example, autonomous vehicles should not continue ordinary allocation or routing rules through an emergency, but unaffected services should remain governed. Emergency detection and response must be authenticated and bounded so a false signal, injected instruction or compromised component cannot silently expand permissions or trigger a system-wide suspension. | 10. Commitment state, material change & normal escalation. Can the system distinguish recommendation, negotiation, reservation, binding commitment and execution, and detect when a material change of cost, route, time, context, policy or authority requires normal escalation—confirmation, revalidation, cancellation or a change of authority—while the current frame remains safe? If confirmation cannot be obtained within the useful time window, or if the ordinary frame itself has become unsafe, the case passes to Challenge 3's bounded escape path; silence or delay must not be converted into permission. |
| 4. Human-inclusive oversight authority & capacity. In a system that includes humans and agents, can escalation reach a human or human-held role that is formally authorized, available, informed, competent and able to intervene within the useful time window? Human oversight is an internal system capacity: named roles, required information, availability and response windows, plus means to pause, redirect or reverse action—not an external fallback represented only by a nominal role. | 11. Policy, objective & preference integrity across domains. Can each policy, objective and preference retain its owner or source, version, scope, priority and conditions across public/private systems, organizations or jurisdictions? For the same actor, a new or lower-priority preference must not silently overwrite an earlier hard limit; conflicts should preserve both inputs, apply declared priority and validity rules and record the determination. Across actors, one actor's policy, objective or preference must not be substituted for another's merely because both concern the same action. |
| 5. Operational indeterminacy & containment. Some indeterminacy is inevitable, especially when agents subdelegate or compose incomplete, conflicting or stale authority facts, preferences and delegations. Can the system manage it rather than silently convert it into permission? It must bound uncertainty, preserve evidence and links to canonical authority sources, contain propagation and define hold, rollback or escalation paths before downstream effects leave control parameters. If effective rollback to the applicable authority source is no longer possible, the system should enter a governed safe state rather than continue by default. | 12. Accountability, challenge & repair. After an action, can a principal or competent authority reconstruct which identity, role, grant, policy, preference, evidence and contextual determination applied, challenge the result and repair future state without rewriting historical records? |
| 6. Interoperable, privacy-preserving trust determination. Can independent personal agents, public systems, providers and relying parties verify enough authority and status in real time without exposing full agendas, preferences or unnecessary personal data? The challenge is technological and interoperable, but implementation-neutral. | 13. Authority history vs intervention history. Can the original authority record and a later human/technical intervention remain distinct historical objects while producing one unambiguous current operative determination for the same action? An intervention may confirm, restrict, suspend or replace authority without erasing provenance. |
| 7. Identity & representation link. Can every relying party distinguish the principal, organization or role, acting human/agent, relevant agent instance or substitute, and the current representation relationship? Correct attribution cannot be inferred merely from similar output or behavior. | 14. Evidence-to-decision assessment. Can each transition state what must be demonstrated, what evidence is required, whether that evidence is sufficient/insufficient/inconclusive, and which decision it can support? This creates a reusable assessment layer without turning the assessment method into a mandatory runtime architecture. |

Why the case has depth. A solution may pass one challenge and fail another; two themes may each work locally yet fail at their interface. The frozen case therefore acts as a shared stress test: it does not prove that a theme satisfies the whole FG-TIDA mandate, but it makes visible whether the requirements and architectures being proposed can survive a realistic multi-actor agentic situation.

Definitive-version choice. FG-TIDA should decide whether the definitive version will turn each challenge into a one-page validation unit with specific requirements, test and stress-test conditions, required evidence and clear validation criteria, so that the challenges are not left as open questions; or deliberately keep this annex at the present general level as a shared orientation and a basis for validation against real cases. Either approach is valid, but the intended level of specification should be explicit.
