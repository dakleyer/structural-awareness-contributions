Frozen internal validation package. It is not an adopted FG-TIDA or ITU-T deliverable. The Parent Case Study remains public pre-freeze working material. The legacy identifiers UC-EA-01…04 are retained for continuity, but the four documents are Architecture-Validation Profiles, not FG-TIDA use-case submissions. They do not modify Parent Case facts. Public representation, if filed, is one situational use-case record derived from the mobility instantiation; the four profiles remain the deeper falsification/test apparatus behind that record.

&nbsp;

Historical v0.2 preserved the four-profile structure and added controlled branches/variables for ecosystem sensitivity/exposure, consequence/reversibility, tolerated residual, finite observation/determination/human capacity and response horizon. These additions test the risk/resource qualification of W(d,t); they do not create a fifth validation profile or modify Parent Case facts.

v0.5 frozen reconciliation preserves the four-profile architecture and APQ traceability, but resolves the artifact collision with the FG-TIDA `use-cases` repository. It adds no fifth validation profile and no new top-level function. EHD is fixed as the general interoperable handoff contract; the four-field Theme #13 determinacy envelope is a #13 profile of EHD. External interoperability is tested in Nelson's Stage 1 fixture before any corpus reopening.

# 2. Traceability model

Internal validation chain: Case Study → Challenge / ToR requirement → Architecture-Validation Profile → test or stress test → evidence → determination.

External publication chain: Parent Case Study → one concrete situational FG-TIDA Use Case → related Theme links / requirements → optional testbed profiles. The public Use Case and the internal validation profile are deliberately different artifact types.

`CH-S#` = DAOS Challenge S#.

`IF-S#` = Functional Interface S#.

Native Challenge and Functional Interface artifacts retain their existing `S#` identifiers.

&nbsp;

The Case Study fixes the world and stable facts. A Challenge identifies a concrete problem, requirement, interface or structural question exposed by the Case Study. An Architecture-Validation Profile selects a decision surface or operational path through those facts and evaluates the architecture against one or more Challenges. Tests and stress tests generate observations and evidence; assessment determines what that evidence is sufficient to establish. Separately, an FG-TIDA Use Case is a public situation record answering who acts, what happens, what decision is required, what fails today and why current handling falls short.

# 3. Validation Profile family and public-use-case projection

UC-EA-01 — Architecture-Validation Profile — Action-time Operating-Frame Requalification under Context Change

Primary Challenge: S3 Regime & context qualification.

Secondary: S10 Commitment state & material change; S14 Evidence-to-decision assessment; S5 Operational indeterminacy & containment.

EA functions: F1, F2, F5, F6, F7, F9.

Decision required: May the prior T1 determination still be relied upon at T2 after the context change, and if not, what requalification and operating posture are justified? v0.2 also asks how the answer changes when the same context change has different mission sensitivity/consequence, reversibility, observation capacity or response horizon.

&nbsp;

UC-EA-02 — Architecture-Validation Profile — Bounded Determination under Incomplete, Conflicting or Partially Scoped Evidence

Primary Challenge: S5 Operational indeterminacy & containment.

Secondary: S14 Evidence-to-decision assessment; S6 Interoperable, privacy-preserving trust determination.

EA functions: F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9.

Decision required: Is the available evidence sufficient to close, should the observation window be expanded, narrowed/stopped or redirected, or must the result remain indeterminate? v0.2 explicitly tests whether further awareness has enough marginal decision value to justify its compute/latency/privacy/human-capacity cost.

&nbsp;

APQ branch set: UC-EA-02 also tests decision-relative acquisition-pathway discrimination when the same missing evidence can be obtained through pathways with different scope, provenance, source-independence evidence, latency, privacy/cost burden and response-window compatibility. Partial participation and adverse/divergent pathway incentives are applied as cross-cutting stress profiles rather than as a fifth validation profile. Interoperability with a non-EA producer/consumer is tested externally in the Stage 1 fixture and is deliberately not introduced as a new internal Branch L.

&nbsp;

UC-EA-03 — Architecture-Validation Profile — Human Oversight under Bounded Effective Capacity and Non-curative Approval

Primary Challenge: S4 Human oversight authority & capacity.

Secondary: S13 Authority history vs intervention history; S14 Evidence-to-decision assessment; S5 Operational indeterminacy & containment.

EA functions: F1, F3, F5, F6, F7, F9.

Decision required: Does the available human oversight path have sufficient current authority, information, time and intervention capacity to alter the operative determination, and what epistemic effect does the human decision actually have? The profile now evaluates that question against Theme #16's consolidated lifecycle — trigger qualification → normal/exceptional intervention path → escalation → applicable human authority → permitted intervention → evidence/decision assessment → execution-or-containment outcome → state/authority revalidation → return/suspension/rollback. `HELD`, `INDETERMINATE`, bounded mandates and admission decisions are optional implementation mappings, not mandatory universal stages.

&nbsp;

UC-EA-04 — Architecture-Validation Profile — Scope-indexed Composition of Locally Valid Determinations

Primary Challenge: S9 Multi-principal composition, non-substitution & conflict.

Secondary: S11 Policy integrity across domains & jurisdictions; S14 Evidence-to-decision assessment; S5 Operational indeterminacy & containment.

EA functions: F2*, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8.

`*` F2 is exercised where APQ/acquisition-pathway selection is part of the branch.

Decision required: Do locally valid determinations compose into a sufficiently qualified system-level determination for the current action without silent substitution, cross-domain compensation or epistemic laundering? v0.2 also asks whether observation effort is allocated to the domains where ecosystem sensitivity/consequence makes it material, rather than allowing excessive caution/search in one domain to compensate under-observation in another.

&nbsp;

APQ composition branch: UC-EA-04 also tests whether nominally distinct acquisition pathways that share an upstream evidence source are incorrectly treated as independent corroboration, and whether a less attractive but materially independent path can have greater marginal epistemic value for the unresolved domain. #13 boundary: the incident lifecycle may construct/refine the operational affected-scope or blast-radius graph; F5 constructs only the decision-relative epistemic dependency/inherited-indeterminacy graph needed to determine what those relations establish. The graphs may share evidence without merging ownership.

# 5. Architecture-validation and freeze rule

The four Architecture-Validation Profiles are intended to falsify or refine the Ecosystem Awareness architecture F1–F9 and interfaces O1–O6 / S1–S13. Architecture changes require a test showing that a responsibility derived from the EA principles has no current owner. A failed interoperability test is first treated as an interface/profile finding; it does not automatically justify F10, S14 or another top-level family.

&nbsp;

A named sub-capability inside an existing F-function does not create a new top-level function when the responsibility is already owned by that F-function. Such a sub-capability may be named when a validation branch requires the behavior to be cited and tested independently. F2.APQ is treated in this way and remains part of F2.

&nbsp;

Every validation profile records both peer similarity and difference: what the peer already does; what EA reuses rather than replaces; what additional EA behavior is being tested; whether the peer can reproduce that behavior without adding an EA-equivalent epistemic layer; and the observed outcome under the same Parent Case facts.

&nbsp;

For v0.2 comparisons, the test vector must also control or explicitly vary: ecosystem sensitivity/exposure, consequence severity, reversibility, tolerated residual, observation/determination budget, human capacity budget and response horizon. Resource use and missed-risk exposure are outcome variables, so a configuration does not win merely by observing more.

# 6. External validation and non-duplication

FG-TIDA Use Case #4 — Federated ecosystem defense across independently governed organizations — is the preferred external Stage 1 interoperability and incident-response fixture for EA. It supplies signal lifecycle, affected-scope/blast-radius, local authority and response-window behavior that the EA profiles consume rather than duplicate. EA should not create a parallel testbed.

&nbsp;

FG-TIDA use-cases PR #2 — Regulated financial services; primary theme Runtime Enforcement — is complementary for policy/conformance, explicit INDETERMINATE, verifier/appraisal semantics and test vectors. EA consumes those outputs with their source-native semantics rather than translating them into EA/#16 states.

&nbsp;

Theme #16 supplies the human-oversight lifecycle and human-capacity state. UC-EA-03 tests how EA consumes trigger/path/capacity/authority/decision/outcome/revalidation state; it does not replace the oversight mechanism or require one particular state machine. Human attention remains a finite resource of the extended human-agent system.

&nbsp;

Theme #17 supplies a production rights/identity case that can later validate identity, provenance, attestation and record-facing EA interfaces.

&nbsp;

The four validation profiles continue to cover F1–F9. F2.APQ is exercised primarily through UC-EA-02 and UC-EA-04 and therefore does not justify a fifth profile.

&nbsp;

Interoperability reconciliation. EHD is the general handoff contract. The Theme #13 determinacy envelope remains a specialized EHD profile: `closure` maps to operational result/closure; `determinacy_margin` is a decision/scope-specific qualifier; `capacity_binding` is a conditional capacity qualifier; and `inherited_indeterminacy` is a dependency/residual qualifier. This relation clarifies rather than supersedes Nelson Use Case #4 Requirement 20. No change to that requirement is needed for the current #13 profile.

&nbsp;

Public-use-case strategy. One external situation should be filed only after the #13 interface comment reconciles this profile/general-contract relation. Working public title: “Reassessing a delegated mobility commitment after a critical context change.” The issue should lead with actors, action, decision required, current failure and residual gap; it may link Annex II for upward/downward/horizontal extensibility but should not expose the full internal Challenge/peer/novelty apparatus by default.
