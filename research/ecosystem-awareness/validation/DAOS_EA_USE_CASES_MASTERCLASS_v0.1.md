## 2. The derivation chain — why four profiles arise from one case

**Internal chain:** Parent Case Study and stable facts → exposed Challenge CH‑S# → EA architecture-validation profile → controlled branch/test vector → observed evidence → bounded assessment of F1–F9 and interfaces.

**External chain:** Parent Case Study → one concrete situational mobility Use Case, if and when filed through the relevant public process → links to relevant Themes/requirements → optional deeper testbed profiles. Internal validation and public situation records answer different questions.

The parent package deliberately separates [case design and freeze logic](../../../submissions/itu-fg-tida/2026-theme-contributions/delegated-authority-os-under-context-change/01_Case_Design_and_PreFreeze_Working_Structure.md), Annex I facts, [Annex II extensibility](../../../submissions/itu-fg-tida/2026-theme-contributions/delegated-authority-os-under-context-change/03_ANNEX_II_Case_Extensibility.md), [Annex III solution challenges](../../../submissions/itu-fg-tida/2026-theme-contributions/delegated-authority-os-under-context-change/04_ANNEX_III_Challenges_Exposed_by_the_Case.md), [Annex IV ToR traceability](../../../submissions/itu-fg-tida/2026-theme-contributions/delegated-authority-os-under-context-change/05_ANNEX_IV_FG-TIDA_ToR_Mapping_and_Traceability.md) and [Annex V adjacent standards](../../../submissions/itu-fg-tida/2026-theme-contributions/delegated-authority-os-under-context-change/06_ANNEX_V_Adjacent_Standards_and_Research_Relevance.md). A test profile selects a path through those facts; it does **not** add a new case fact to make EA succeed.

**Identifier rule:** CH‑S3 means DAOS solution Challenge S3 in Annex III/IV. IF‑S3 would mean a Functional Interface S3 in EA's separate interface taxonomy. They are not the same object. Older EA working text sometimes abbreviates challenge references as C3/C5; this guide uses **CH‑S#** to keep challenge IDs distinct from interface IDs and from the case's commitment C1.

## 3. One minimum scene, four different determinations

The parent Annex I is intentionally intelligible before it is technical: a citizen wants to meet a time-bounded appointment; alternatives may involve transport, sharing, cost, autonomy and public constraints. The planning/Pareto layer supplies candidate routes but is **not** the TIDA trust-and-identity challenge. The challenge begins when a human or agent must commit and later act under bounded public and private authority.

| Case object / time | Meaning | What remains distinguishable |
|---|---|---|
| T0: G1 | Citizen principal's grant to a personal agent: hard limits, tradeable preferences, scope and validity | Principal, acting agent, instance, representation and retained authority. Preference is not permission. |
| T0: P1 | Authorized municipal role's policy/mandate, including Qnormal/Qcritical, scope, origin/version and E1 source | Public authority and policy applicability do not rewrite private preferences. The policy objective alone is not a grant. |
| T1: D1 and C1 | Agent's selection/negotiation and mobility commitment under the then-current G1/P1 | A decision and a binding commitment are different states; historical validity at T1 is not a reusable T2 permit. |
| T2: E1 reports Qcritical crossed | Approved observation challenges the ordinary route/access frame | Signal source, time, threshold and applicability require assessment; no mere alert supplies a new authority. |
| T2: A1, possibly H1 | Proposed action/execution after change; H1 is a separately authorized exceptional intervention if applicable | G1/P1 provenance, D1/C1 commitment and H1 intervention are separate records. Proceed, reroute, restrict, hold or escalate depends on current evidence and authority. |

This shared T0→T1→T2 sequence is *not* four different fictional worlds. UC‑EA‑01 asks whether the supporting **operating frame** can still be relied upon; UC‑EA‑02 asks whether the evidence and acquisition path can sufficiently **determine** the current action; UC‑EA‑03 asks whether human intervention is **effective and epistemically curative or not**; UC‑EA‑04 asks whether local results **compose** across non-substitutable domains. A good result in one profile does not imply a pass in another.

The parent remains public **pre-freeze working material**. Its design note proposes D1 intelligibility, D2 minimality with bounded extensibility, D3 ToR alignment, plus a declared governing regime for authority-standing questions. A later parent freeze should fix identities, grants/mandates, the change event, T0–T2 evidence and expected branch determinations. Testing must then hold those facts fixed across architectures; a material change to a frozen fact creates a versioned variant rather than a silent “better” outcome.

## 4. Extension is controlled reuse, not an enlarged mobility claim

[Annex II](../../../submissions/itu-fg-tida/2026-theme-contributions/delegated-authority-os-under-context-change/03_ANNEX_II_Case_Extensibility.md) asks whether the same structural **meaning** survives changes in scale and service domain. It does not pretend one case covers every agentic system.

| Axis | Controlled example | What may change | What must not change | Result if the invariant fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Upward** | Add transport providers, employers, infrastructure and public authority roles; possibly nested objectives/KPIs | More principals, agents, grants, jurisdictions, capacity layers and objective owners | A local KPI cannot manufacture mandate; priority/hard constraints stay owned, scoped, versioned and traceable | Declare an extension boundary or a new case, not a universal hierarchy. |
| **Downward** | Private factory, possibly a single production cell and maintenance/safety role | Fewer actors; machine/material/energy/time capacity instead of roads and vehicles | Production agent acts only within delegated envelope; management, maintenance, safety and quality remain distinct bounded authority domains | Do not relabel factory safety as “municipal policy”; version the variant. |
| **Horizontal** | Appointments, school rooms, hospital beds/staff, compute allocation or logistics windows | Resource, service and policy vocabulary | Principal, role, agent, preference, policy, grant, commitment and decision retain their distinctions | Stop reuse if these objects have no meaningful counterparts. |

At factory T0 an agent may schedule an order; T1 fault/material loss may invalidate the plan; T2 asks who may revise or hold under the current roles. This is structurally analogous, **not** an added fact in the mobility scene. A public mobility Use Case should point to Annex II as bounded-reuse evidence, not advertise hospital/factory findings it has not tested. A governing legal regime belongs to each authority-standing variant; no cross-jurisdictional operative answer is assumed from the common case skeleton.

## 6. The four profiles as an engineering test suite

| Profile | Primary / secondary CH‑S# | EA functions actually exercised | Core perturbation | What would support the EA hypothesis; what would weaken it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **01 — frame** | S3 / S10,S14,S5 | F1 F2 F5 F6 F7 F9 | Same T2 change under different consequence, reversibility, W(d,t), capacity and response horizon | A current determination distinguishes T1 from T2, requalifies only affected assumptions and avoids both stale continuation and needless blanket escalation at comparable burden. If a strong peer does the same more simply, differential claim weakens. |
| **02 — evidence** | S5 / S14,S6 | F2 F3 F4 F5 F6 F7 F8 F9 | Incomplete/unknown/correlated evidence; resolvable out-of-window gap versus structural residual; alternative acquisition pathways | Bounded retrieval and EHD handoff preserve scope/residual without binary closure or endless search. If source-lineage-aware value-of-information peers reproduce the decision/resource frontier, novelty shrinks. |
| **03 — human** | S4 / S13,S14,S5 | F1 F3 F5 F6 F7 F9 | Available versus nominal/overloaded reviewer; biased representation; approval, execution and later change | Approval changes only the permitted domain and does not cure contrary evidence; review effort preserves useful response capacity. A peer lifecycle with the same separation and lower load weakens incremental EA value. |
| **04 — composition** | S9 / S11,S14,S5 | F2* F3 F4 F5 F6 F7 F8 | Locally coherent but differently scoped outputs; duplicate lineage, sectioning, wrong-domain caution and asymmetric effort | F5 preserves domain-specific E(d), detects cross-domain compensation/closure laundering, targets the failing relation and keeps the result in F8. A strong composition peer matching this without EA-equivalent semantics weakens distinctiveness. |

*F2 is exercised in UC‑EA‑04 when APQ/acquisition-pathway selection is part of the branch. F2.APQ is a named sub-capability **within F2**, not a new F10 or fifth use case.*

### 6.1 Profile 01: a trigger is not a fresh decision

A correct Qcritical alert or policy recheck does not by itself establish whether the old T1 context/window still supports A1. The profile's A–I branches include a normal-frame control, bounded response, incomplete qualification, Type‑1 over-escalation, Type‑2 stale closure, regime-transition possibility, high-sensitivity narrow-window failure, low-sensitivity over-requalification and a shrinking response window. Record W(d,t)'s selection basis, source freshness, changed domain, time/calls/reviewer effort, whether the extra observation changed the decision and whether a response remained feasible. **Potential benefit:** timely targeted correction without converting every uncertainty into global migration.

### 6.2 Profile 02: knowing what can be known is part of control

Branches A–K contrast sufficient bounded evidence, resolvable gaps, unknown source windows, correlated corroboration, endless search, false closure, structural residual, excessive observation, hidden exposure, equal-budget risk adaptation and decision-relative pathway selection. A second provider or transport is not a second independent *source* if both depend on E1. A lower-rated route may have higher marginal decision value if it addresses the unresolved domain before A1's deadline; source independence must be demonstrated or marked UNKNOWN. EHD is the general handoff; the Theme #13 determinacy envelope is a specialized profile, not a competing universal schema. **Potential benefit:** more valuable evidence per unit of scarce capacity and fewer forced-certainty outcomes, subject to strong peer comparison.

### 6.3 Profile 03: a human can authorize without repairing the world model

Branches A–I distinguish effective, nominal, overloaded, biased and delayed human review, a later material change, unresolved execution and cumulative escalation-capacity depletion. The source Theme #16 lifecycle remains implementation-neutral: trigger/path qualification → applicable human authority → permitted intervention → evidence/decision assessment → execution or containment → authority/state revalidation. EA asks whether that path has current time, information and capacity, and what the human's action legitimately changes. A valid H1 cannot rewrite E1's contrary observation or turn a biased representation into truth merely because the reviewer clicked approval. **Potential benefit:** more selective human attention and a better-qualified return to operation, not replacement of human oversight.

### 6.4 Profile 04: valid pieces need not make a valid whole

Branches A–K contrast healthy heterogeneous composition with compensation fallacy, role sectioning without independent corroboration, correlated consensus, closure laundering, creativity/caution in the wrong domain, high-token effort, asymmetric awareness allocation, equal-budget/unequal-sensitivity decisions and nominally diverse but source-dependent pathways. **Operational blast radius** (which services may be affected) and **epistemic dependency** (what evidence supports this decision) may reuse relation evidence but are not the same graph or ownership. A planner, policy checker and human reviewer can all be locally correct while A1 remains unsupported because their claims answer different questions. **Potential benefit:** fewer false system-level closures and a more actionable affected-domain trace, not a blanket claim that peers lack voting or provenance.

## 7. How to run a fair test instead of telling four plausible stories

1. **Freeze the vector:** case version; G1/P1/E1/D1/C1/A1/H1 conditions; governing regime; available source material; decision deadline; authority owners; expected source-native facts and permitted response. Deliberately varied sensitivity/capacity belongs in named branches, not a hidden rewrite of the base.
2. **Use strong peers:** the [reviewed EA benchmark](../ARCHITECTURE_BENCHMARK_v0.5_REVIEWED_WORKING.md) requires a realistic B0 and a **strong B1** with scoped propositions, lineage, UQ, risk-aware acquisition, HITL and exceptions before testing EA's incremental layer. Do not compare EA with a peer deliberately missing controls it normally has.
3. **Pre-register failure and cost:** stale-frame reuse, false systemic closure, unsupported action, missed response window, unnecessary hold, duplicated source counted as independent, reviewer overload and source-native result collapse. Count tool/retrieval calls, compute/tokens, latency, privacy/disclosure, human minutes and remaining useful response margin.
4. **Observe evidence separately from assessment:** a detector report, attestation result, authority record, human decision, permit, command receipt and measured outcome answer different questions. CH‑S14 asks what the evidence is sufficient to establish; F9 cannot treat a receipt as measured service effect.
5. **Record falsification:** if B1 reproduces the same decisions, residual trace and cost with no EA-equivalent layer, the differentiation claim contracts. If EA improves false closure but consumes too much latency/attention to act, the benefit claim also fails. Positive tests remain bounded to the declared case version and peer configuration.

A profile's assessment should distinguish **SUPPORTED, FAILED and UNRESOLVED** evidence relative to its declared question rather than promote one branch into universal architecture validation. Detailed acceptance criteria and measured-evidence lists are in each continuous profile. No pass/fail result is supplied by this guide.
