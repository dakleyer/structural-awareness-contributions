3\. Deep derivation chain

&nbsp;

The deep chain developed before the compact functional architecture was:

&nbsp;

Objective Envelope / mission conditions → reachable response capability → Good Enough Early Warning requirement → Semantic Window requirement → Ecosystem Awareness determination → Minimum Control Architecture / sufficient configuration → Normal / Containment / Migration posture → feedback and revalidation.

&nbsp;

The present architecture does not require those stages to exist as separate runtime components. F1 absorbs mission, consequence, reversibility, tolerated residual and response-horizon qualification; F2 owns bounded working-window qualification; F6 owns systemic assessment/posture; F7 directs requalification; and F9 closes the loop. The historical chain remains important because it explains why observation is derived from what must be preserved and from what responses remain possible, rather than from available data outward.

&nbsp;

4\. Objective Envelope and response-relative awareness

&nbsp;

The early research began from the Objective Envelope: the conditions that must remain sufficiently true for the mission to remain viable. Awareness requirements were then derived backwards from what changes could violate that envelope and what responses remained reachable.

&nbsp;

This means the useful amount of awareness was never “as much context as possible.” It was the amount and type of ecosystem knowledge required early enough to preserve a viable response option at acceptable cost.

&nbsp;

5\. Good Enough Early Warning

&nbsp;

Good Enough Early Warning (GEEW) was not a claim that every risk needs a universal predictor. It was a sufficiency relation: a warning is useful only if it arrives early enough, with enough qualified information, for an available response to alter the outcome. The original work used the actionable warning margin Δt \= t\_l − t\_d as one explanatory form, where late determination can be epistemically correct yet operationally useless if the response window has already closed.

&nbsp;

The current architecture absorbs this through F1 response/warning horizon, F2 minimum-sufficient window selection, F6 response-window/actionability state, F7 bounded requalification and F9 outcome feedback.

&nbsp;

6\. Represented observable field versus active Semantic Window

&nbsp;

Article 03 distinguished technically/accessibly observable state from the subset admitted to active interpretation. This distinction is preserved here as explanatory lineage: a system can have information available, cached, discoverable or technically observable without treating all of it as active decision context.

&nbsp;

The later W(d,t) formalism preserves the active-window concept while Ω remains open and cannot be identified with any finite observable field. Any represented observable field is itself bounded and incomplete relative to the open ecosystem.

&nbsp;

7\. Semantic Window quality vector

&nbsp;

The historical window-quality vector included breadth, depth, temporal horizon, resolution, source diversity, provenance depth, update frequency, explicit uncertainty representation and cost.

&nbsp;

The compact current architecture distributes these dimensions across F2, F4/F5 and the interface model. Their historical grouping is retained here so later tests do not silently reduce “window size” to breadth alone.

&nbsp;

8\. Minimum Control Architecture as configuration space

&nbsp;

Minimum Control Architecture (MCA) was developed as a constrained sufficiency/configuration-space interpretation rather than a fixed minimal stack. Different configurations may trade observation, warning, containment, migration, signalling, redundancy, human capacity and feedback while remaining sufficient under stated assumptions.

&nbsp;

Local optima, feasible sufficiency regions and Pareto-efficient configurations are research/design-space interpretations, not current FG-TIDA normative functions and not a claim that one universal optimizer exists. MCA therefore remains a deep design-space lens rather than a reason to add another top-level EA function.

&nbsp;

9\. Level-1 and Level-2 adaptation

&nbsp;

The early research used two adaptation levels.

&nbsp;

Level 1 — local adaptation: a participant adjusts its own sufficient configuration under its own mission, observations, constraints and resources.

&nbsp;

Level 2 — ecosystem-informed adaptation: information originating outside the participant’s local observation can alter its local window, evidence strategy, response readiness or control configuration without imposing a central controller or common objective.

&nbsp;

The current F2/F4/F5/F7/F9 architecture operationalizes this relationship without requiring the Level-1/Level-2 labels as new planes.

&nbsp;

10\. Ecosystem signalling without required cooperation

&nbsp;

Ecosystem signalling was defined without required cooperation. Relevant information may arise from voluntary disclosure, regulation, commercial incentives, passive observation, third-party assessment, public behavior, environmental traces or adversarial leakage. The receiver remains independently governed.

&nbsp;

A signal changes the evidence available to the receiver; it does not create authority or global truth. Signalling can therefore have both defensive value and positive adaptation/information-efficiency value by reducing local epistemic isolation, reusing discoveries made elsewhere and avoiding redundant exploration.

&nbsp;

The source and receiver need not share objectives. Participation does not establish cooperation, aligned incentives, source independence or truthfulness. Declared properties remain declarations unless supported by stronger evidence.

&nbsp;

11\. Partial participation and competing objectives

&nbsp;

Article 05 treated partial participation and competing objectives as validation profiles rather than edge cases. Some ecosystem actors may not publish structured state, may expose only partial information, or may actively prefer a different outcome. A valid EA architecture must remain useful under those conditions without inventing missing qualifiers or fabricating one global objective.

&nbsp;

These assumptions later become especially important for acquisition-pathway qualification: a useful pathway may be passive/non-cooperative, and a self-described pathway may have incentives to overstate its properties.

&nbsp;

12\. Learning better boundaries of attention

&nbsp;

The early Semantic Window work emphasized that learning should improve not only predictions but also the boundary of attention itself. Repeatedly low-value variables/sources may be removed from active interpretation; repeatedly missed precursors may justify widening, refreshing or redirecting W(d,t).

&nbsp;

The current F9 feedback loop preserves this responsibility through evidence about wasted search/escalation, capacity depletion, missed material changes, stale-frame surprises and hidden exposure.
