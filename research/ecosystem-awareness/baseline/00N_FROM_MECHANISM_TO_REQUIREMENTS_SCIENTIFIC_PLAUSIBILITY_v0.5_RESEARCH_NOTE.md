## From compressed agent outputs to qualified context: scientific plausibility and requirements alignment

**A network of agents can keep exchanging locally adequate answers while losing the context needed to know whether those answers still work together.** EA investigates whether a small, qualified part of that context can survive in metadata—and whether composing it can reveal a reason to reassess before the opportunity to respond is lost. **A local blind spot need not be a collective blind spot:** another participant may already have a relevant assessment, or a characterized capability to obtain one. Making that correspondence visible offers a direct reason to investigate the architecture.

**Research note · v0.5 · 2 October 2026 · local draft for review.**

**Read first:** [A/B/C/D Semantics and Mathematical Plausibility of Scoped Requalification, v0.6](./00M_ABCD_AND_MATHEMATICAL_PLAUSIBILITY_v0.6_RESEARCH_NOTE.md). That note asks whether the proposed operations have defensible mathematical representations. This companion asks whether those operations could serve the functions EA is required to provide. It develops a scientific plausibility argument, not a demonstration of operational compliance or engineering viability.

Here, **EA** is shorthand for the broader EA/EP architectural proposal. Qualification, positioning, authority and execution keep their respective responsibilities. The A/B/C/D meanings are those of the preceding note; older shorthand elsewhere in the corpus is not an alternative definition for this argument.

The central claim is that **selected review questions can be answered, or meaningfully left unresolved, through compatible metadata without reconstructing all source data**. The main remaining question is whether useful profiles of that mechanism meet the required accuracy, response and cost conditions together. Throughout, a *profile* means a declared use case with specified inputs, interpretation rules, scope and limits.

## 1. The architectural concern—and the opportunity it creates

### 1.1 A useful answer can conceal the conditions that make it useful

An agent may examine many possibilities and return one recommendation. A second agent may combine several recommendations and return one plan. A third may turn that plan into an operational instruction. Each handoff can be useful for its immediate purpose while leaving the receiver with less knowledge of the conditions on which it depends.

The concern is especially relevant to agentic systems that communicate selected answers from probabilistic processes. Different evidence, alternatives and degrees of support can lead to the same delivered answer. A receiver that gets only that answer cannot unambiguously distinguish those underlying situations. This issue also occurs in deterministic computation: a many-to-one transformation can discard distinctions regardless of how its producer reasons.

Using the preceding note's vocabulary, **A is the delivered functional result**. When an interface carries A alone, it need not carry the established basis and assessable remainder associated with B, the witnessed exploration frontier of C, or the effective determination limits of D. This is a particular architectural exposure, not a claim that every existing agent interface transmits only A. Nor is every discarded input or unchosen possibility automatically B.

Compression is useful and often necessary. The question is **which distinctions the receiving task can afford to lose**. An answer can be sufficient for the original task and insufficient for a different question: *do the conditions under which we may rely on this answer still hold?* Information bottleneck research formalizes the choice of a compact representation relative to a target of interest [R1].

Repeated handoffs can make this problem consequential: a qualified local result may be reused as an unqualified premise for many dependent decisions. Agreement among later agents can then reflect shared compression or shared evidence rather than independent confirmation. The number of agents or handoffs alone does not measure the loss, and no general disadvantage at equal computing power follows from it. Tool use and new observations can also introduce fresh information. The concern is the disappearance of **material qualification along particular dependency paths**.

### 1.2 Preserve a second kind of information

EA proposes that selected metadata about foundations, coverage, dependencies, exploration routes and determination limits remain available for qualified composition. It does not seek to recreate every discarded alternative, collect all source data or recompute every participant's task.

Metadata must preserve a distinction at its source, or obtain it through another justified route. It cannot be generated reliably from an A that has already erased that distinction. “Lost” here means unavailable through the handoff; source records may still permit a later, potentially costly, inquiry.

The opportunity comes from the different perspectives of participants. **The same phenomenon need not occupy the same A/B/C/D role for every actor.** One actor may have an unexplored C frontier where another already has a characterized B reserve; a specialist may have delivered an assessment as its A. Another actor may still face a D barrier concerning the relevant effect. The labels describe each process's relation to a specified question, not fixed classes of phenomena.

The canonical vocabulary proposed in the preceding note is: A, the result of active exploitation; B, the established basis and characterized exploitation reserve; C, the exploration frontier; and D, residual uncertainty beyond effective evaluation. **Evaluation** applies an established frame to a characterized question or option; **exploration** seeks the grounds for evaluating an as-yet uncharacterized avenue. C is not a reserve rejected after a cost–benefit calculation. An unused option can remain B when its evaluation basis is already available.

This difference in positions gives composition something useful to investigate. An eligible qualification from one actor may supply missing grounds for a specified part of another's frontier. Two locally adequate results may also rely on incompatible versions or overlapping evidence. Several exploration reports may identify a shared direction worth examining, without establishing what lies there. The opportunity is to relate these positions while preserving their different grounds.

**The potential gain comes from preserving complementary distinctions and relating them correctly.** Loss does not create information. Distribution can, however, make different partial views available; metadata can retain aspects of those views that the operative answers omit. The comparison of interest is with an architecture that lacks those qualifications, not with an ideal observer already possessing all the underlying information.

An A-only comparison isolates this mechanism. A claim of added value requires a stronger comparison with the qualifications, monitoring and source access already available to a competent alternative, under comparable resources.

“Composition of blind spots” is a useful description of this proposal when it means composing *grounded descriptions of the boundaries of local knowledge*. It does not mean observing the hidden contents of those blind spots. In particular:

- B metadata can expose the grounds, known limits and assessment conditions of a result.
- C metadata can expose an evidenced exploration direction without supplying a population, search cost, expected benefit or probability that the producer cannot calculate.
- D metadata can expose a partly witnessed evaluation barrier and report changes in associated indicators without enumerating the open residual or measuring its hidden risk. Monitoring that residual is compatible with being unable to evaluate its relevant effect.

More C reports about one street do not establish more cats. A meaningful composition may instead establish that several declared frontiers overlap there, or that a decision depends on a location for which coverage is not established. Even that conclusion needs compatible scope, attribution and a declared view of the participating actors. Missing reports do not prove an ecosystem-wide absence of coverage.

The distinction is between information *about a boundary* and information *inside that boundary*. Describing a C frontier precisely does not supply the variables needed to assess its contents. Likewise, establishing that a D barrier matters does not quantify the hidden effect. An observed indicator is A of a monitoring function; it need not resolve the D of the process affected by the underlying influence. An uncontrollable but defensibly assessed risk can instead be A or B, depending on the function. The role remains relative to the producer, question, capability and time.

### 1.3 What a successful composition would deliver

The mechanism inherited from the preceding note has four steps: interpret the functional roles of declarations; establish legitimate scope correspondences; compose task-relevant metadata; and issue a justified requalification or direction of review.

A useful result could say that an assessment basis has expired, that a dependency is no longer qualified, that a particular frontier has become assessable, or that a window merits widening, narrowing or redirecting. Direction is property-specific: improved assessability does not automatically mean lower risk, and more coverage does not automatically mean a better decision.

MSCA—the **Minimum Sufficient Control Architecture**—can anchor the control dimensions and objective for which this review matters. Participants need not share one MSCA, objective or world model. A justified correspondence between relevant parts is sufficient for a particular comparison. The result can support a qualified position over those dimensions without becoming a detailed operational command.

Composition may be participant-local or distributed. Selective messages, queries or a shared store can supply the eligible evidence; the argument concerns what their content means and preserves. Disclosure choices remain with participants under their applicable commitments, so actual signal availability is a condition of coverage.

This yields the proposal examined here:

> Compatible metadata from different participants may expose material changes in the basis for collective reliance that their operative outputs alone do not reveal. If those distinctions survive composition and reach the appropriate recipient in time, they may support bounded requalification without reconstructing the discarded source information.

### 1.4 A local blind spot may have a route to closure elsewhere

Consider several participants concerned with the same property of a defined plot of land. A field operator has an established inspection method, access and a defensible effort estimate, but did not perform the inspection because its local expected benefit did not justify the work. For this operator, the unused assessment belongs to its characterized B reserve. One participant has that property in C: it could investigate, but has not characterized how. Another faces D: the effect matters to its activity, but it has no effective evaluation route under its current conditions. “Matters” need not mean that its probability or impact has been quantified.

By mapping their declarations to a compatible scope and relevant MSCA dimensions, EA could expose that **a known assessment option matches a gap affecting other participants**. Those needs can become grounds for the operator to reconsider its earlier local evaluation. If several receiving decisions can use the same qualified result, one inspection could avoid several separate searches or make an assessment accessible where no effective route existed. This is a plausible opportunity, not a measured saving: compatible use, access, total effort and the participants' different objectives still matter. Repeated reports alone do not establish independent demand or additive value.

Three achievements must remain distinguishable:

- **Map the gap and the reserve:** establish a qualified correspondence. The hidden property is still unknown.
- **Make an evaluation route available:** establish that the capability can actually be used for the receiving question. A specified C aspect may become B; a D barrier may be reduced if its effective conditions change.
- **Close the specified gap:** obtain and validate sufficient evidence for that question. Finding a willing evaluator is not yet an evaluation result. Closure of one gap does not eliminate the open residual.

Sometimes the eligible metadata already contains the missing grounds, so composition itself can close a bounded qualification gap, as in the preceding note's inspection-effort example. When the relevant measurement has not been made, the participants must still perform it and interpret its result through the appropriate route. EA's direct contribution is the scoped correspondence and resulting review or positioning signal. Commitments, authorization, incentives and execution determine whether the opportunity is taken up; EA does not acquire the source's data or commission the work merely by exposing it.

A shared assessment is not cheaper merely because it is performed once. A reuse claim requires sufficient compatibility of question, scope, time and method for every receiving decision, together with legitimate access. Its total burden includes discovery, adaptation, privacy protection, coordination, waiting and renewed qualification, compared with a competent existing coordination route. Reuse across several decisions is one opportunity; enabling a single previously inaccessible but important assessment is another. This is the T4/H6 question of total burden and decision-relevant yield, not an assumption of economic superiority.

The possible benefit is therefore concrete: **what was not worth evaluating for one participant may become worth evaluating when its relevance to others becomes visible**. A diminishing exploitation reserve can likewise motivate exploration, but so can a newly recognized need. Neither trigger requires pretending that the whole C frontier has a calculable cost–benefit function.

## 2. Scientific grounds for harmful and useful composition

The sources contribute different kinds of support. **Formal precedents** in information theory and assumption monitoring concern retained distinctions and conditional validity (§§2.1–2.4). **Dynamical precedents** concern early warning under specific system assumptions (§2.5). **Organizational and cognitive precedents**, including experiments with human groups, concern using distributed knowledge and capabilities (§2.6). Each supports a different part of the argument; their combination is not a theorem about EA.

### 2.1 Compression must be assessed against the receiving question

The data-processing inequality applies when a summary is generated from source observations without additional target information: conditioned on those observations, its generation is independent of the target. Under that assumption, summarization cannot increase information about the target. Loss can be strict, but need not be. A suitably chosen summary may preserve exactly the distinctions a particular question requires [R1].

EA's proposed use follows from distinguishing two questions: the producer's operative task and the receiver's review of the conditions for reliance. Optimizing a summary for the first does not establish sufficiency for the second. The preceding note's factorization condition makes that issue explicit without requiring probabilities: identical summaries must not conceal different answers to the declared review question if exact sufficiency is claimed.

This supports the concern behind the architecture. It does not show that every compressed handoff is harmful or that EA selects the right summary.

### 2.2 Distributed summaries can preserve jointly useful information

Estella Aguerri and Zaidi study separate encoders whose compressed observations jointly retain target information, characterizing information–rate trade-offs for specified discrete and Gaussian models [R2]. The receiver need not obtain all original observations. Their source assumptions include conditional independence given the target; these cannot simply be transferred to agents with shared sources, imitation or feedback.

This supplies a close precedent: **useful distributed compression is an established mathematical possibility**. Its asymptotic coding results do not establish delay or total cost for a finite, changing agentic exchange. Whether EA's eligible metadata realizes a useful trade-off remains a separate question.

### 2.3 The relation between messages can carry the useful distinction

Work on partial information decomposition distinguishes redundant, unique and synergistic information about a target [R3]. It provides a language for the possibility that a relation among sources is informative even when each source, examined alone, is insufficient. It does not make diversity synonymous with useful information or provide a universally agreed measure for every application.

For example, two actors may each report readiness under a different specification version. Their metadata can expose that relation although their operative answers agree. Section 3.6 develops this example through a requirement route. It illustrates relational usefulness; a quantitative claim of statistical synergy would additionally need a probability model and a specified measure.

For a probabilistic profile, let \(J\) be the information already available to the comparison system—including operative outputs and existing qualifications—\(\mathbf M\) the eligible additional metadata, \(Z\) their composed result, and \(R\) an independently specified review target. One question is whether:

\[
I(R;Z\mid J)>0.
\]

In words: does the composition retain information the comparison system lacks about that target? Restricting \(J\) to operative outputs gives the simpler A-only comparison. This is an analysis of information availability, not a prescription that EA ingest every input available to the comparator.

The expression requires a justified joint probability model for the bounded question. It assigns no distribution to the open contents of C or D. If \(Z\) is computed solely from the metadata, its conditional information cannot exceed that of those metadata given the same \(J\). Positive gain can be arbitrarily small or leave the appropriate action unchanged; it is not a criterion for requirement fulfillment. The receiving decision and its materiality threshold must be specified independently, including whether the distinction changes the justified posture, admissible response set or need for requalification. Changing an action alone is not evidence of better information or a better decision. Where a probability model is unavailable, the preceding note's compatible-answer sets and conservative approximations describe distinguishability without inventing one.

### 2.4 Changes in the conditions for reliance are legitimate monitoring targets

Assume-guarantee reasoning separates a component's guarantee from the environment assumptions under which it holds. Sokolsky and colleagues study runtime monitoring of those assumptions and the verification needed when an interface transforms what the monitor observes [R4]. Their analysis also identifies detection limits.

This is the closest conceptual link to EA's context requirements. A component can continue producing familiar outputs while the grounds for relying on its guarantee have changed. Detecting a violated assumption means that the previous conditional justification no longer suffices; it does not prove that the output is wrong. An unobservable or insufficiently established assumption must likewise remain unresolved.

EA generalizes the research question to qualified relations among partial declarations. The transfer requires declared assumptions, trustworthy interpretations and enough observable metadata. Runtime monitoring theory does not automatically supply those mappings.

### 2.5 Some distributed changes can warn of later dynamical transitions

Generalized modeling provides a more specific precedent for anticipation. Lade and Gross use partial structural knowledge and observations to estimate changes in local stability without specifying every process function [R5]. The method depends on an appropriate dynamical model, relevant observables and assumptions such as the separation of slow parameter change from faster system behavior.

Research on spatial early warning also studies aggregating observations across network locations rather than relying only on long time series. MacLaren, Aihara and Masuda show that performance depends on the network and tipping scenario [R6]. This supports investigating distributed perspectives, while their measurements of system state must not be equated with arbitrary declarations about blind spots.

These studies make anticipatory use scientifically credible **for profiles that connect the available metadata to a justified precursor**. They do not establish such a connection for EA in general. The literature's distinction among bifurcation-, noise- and rate-induced tipping further prevents treating all regime changes as one warning problem [R7].

EA can therefore pursue two related contributions: recognize that a basis for reliance is no longer established, and, in suitably modeled cases, recognize a precursor while useful response time remains. The first does not require a prediction of a physical transition. The second needs additional temporal and dynamical evidence.

In particular, detecting a changed basis before harmful consequences is different from predicting that basis change in advance. Neither claim follows merely from observing more metadata. These cited traditions provide separate tools; their assumptions must be made compatible in a chosen profile before their results can support one combined argument.

### 2.6 Distributed knowledge and the reason to use an unexploited reserve

March examines the allocation of resources between exploring new possibilities and exploiting established knowledge, including differences in when and where costs and benefits occur [R8]. This supports considering how a locally unattractive option might be reconsidered when its relevance elsewhere becomes visible. It does not supply an EA allocation rule. The present B/C boundary is narrower than that literature's broad terminology: a trial with a characterized evaluation frame belongs on the B side here, even if another discipline calls it exploration.

Wegner's transactive memory framework describes how people can use knowledge distributed across a group by retaining information about where relevant knowledge resides and communicating to retrieve it [R9]. This is a close precedent for the proposition that a local blind spot need not be shared by everyone. EA's proposed connection between a gap and an **unperformed but characterized assessment** extends the question from locating existing knowledge to locating a capability that could produce it. That extension, its incentives and its benefit remain to be established for EA.

Human-group experiments provide a more concrete comparison. Making expertise locations mutually known improved use of unshared clues and choice accuracy in one decision task [R10]. In a later computer-supported study, maps of collaborators' knowledge improved information sharing and processing without being sufficient to improve group problem-solving performance [R11]. These results support distinguishing a better information route from a better final outcome.

These precedents concern human and organizational coordination. Applying them to autonomous agents requires establishing disclosure, trustworthy capability declarations, compatible scope, effective access and a useful MSCA mapping; willingness to use the resulting indication cannot be assumed. They motivate a bounded question: can qualified metadata make an otherwise overlooked route to assessment visible at a worthwhile cost?

## 3. Why this mechanism could serve the existing requirements

The controlling baseline is [Canonical Requirements: S1–S14, T1–T4, H1–H6 and KPIs](./00_CANONICAL_REQUIREMENTS_CHALLENGES_SUFFICIENCY_HYPOTHESES_KPIS.md), frozen on 17 September 2026. The identifiers below provide traceability; their meanings are stated alongside them. This companion changes none of those requirements and awards no conformance status.

### 3.1 The relevant change is in the basis for reliance

Ordinary changes in A do not necessarily mean a regime change. For this argument, the relevant **context change** is a material change in the conditions that qualify a result, its composition with others, or its use in a receiving decision. Relevant conditions can include coverage, dependencies, source validity, operating assumptions, authority applicability and available response capacity.

Such a change can require routine revalidation within existing rules. A change of operating regime or a governed exceptional path requires further grounds. The canonical regime requirement explicitly distinguishes these cases. Loss of an assessment certificate is neither proof of a physical bifurcation nor permission to replace the rules.

B metadata may carry the affected assessment conditions. C and D metadata may reveal exploration routes or effective limits material to reviewing them. These components do not form a universal temporal sequence. A useful warning need not wait for an operative result to become visibly wrong, but its usefulness depends on whether a material distinction is available before the receiving decision becomes irreversible.

A changed report is not necessarily a changed world: disclosure policy, source participation or interpretation rules may have changed. That can still alter what the receiver is entitled to rely on, but the indication must say whether it concerns the environment, the evidence available about it, or unresolved uncertainty between those explanations.

### 3.2 Three conditions connect mathematical plausibility to functional plausibility

The four operations of the preceding note connect to the requirements as follows: role interpretation preserves what is established and what remains open; scope correspondence prevents unrelated claims from being substituted; aggregation retains the distinctions needed by a receiving review; and requalification identifies the affected basis and a justified position. A failure in one operation cannot be repaired merely by successful operation of another.

Three links must then hold for the **same declared decision scope**:

1. **An observable distinction matters.** A decision-relevant condition is stated independently of the available messages, and compatible, current metadata distinguishes at least some situations requiring different qualifications. Merely counting declarations or assigning A/B/C/D labels does not achieve this.
2. **Composition preserves the distinction and its grounds.** Scope, provenance, dependency, uncertainty and validity must survive the transformation into a review result. Duplicate or correlated evidence must not acquire unwarranted corroborative weight.
3. **A qualified result can be used while it matters.** The result identifies the affected basis, a receiving responsibility and an admissible review or repositioning direction. Where response is required, an authorized actor must still have the capacity and time to respond.

The first two links have close precedents in Sections 2.1–2.4. The third joins those precedents to an operational condition. For example, an actionable-warning claim needs a justified bound of the form:

\[
t_{\mathrm{available}}+\tau_{\mathrm{qualification}}+\tau_{\mathrm{response}}
<t_{\mathrm{last\ useful\ response}}.
\]

Here the first time is when informative evidence becomes available to the declared acquisition path; qualification includes acquisition, composition and delivery, and response includes authorization and intervention. The deadline is the latest useful completion of that response. The serial expression is an illustrative sufficient timing condition; concurrent paths require their actual elapsed-time bound. It does not follow from information theory. The qualification must also remain applicable to the state on which the response acts: being timely does not cure a change between review and use.

### 3.3 Alignment with the four sufficiency conditions

| Existing condition | Plausible contribution of the mechanism | What remains necessary |
|---|---|---|
| **T1 — Material awareness within a declared boundary** | Scope-aligned metadata can expose an expired assumption, incompatible basis or lost assessment capability that operative outputs conceal. Distributed compression and assumption monitoring support this route; dynamical warning supports selected anticipatory profiles. | A material review target, detectable change family, justified interpretation and stated blind spots. No coverage claim for indistinguishable states. |
| **T2 — Qualified determination and owner-preserving handoff** | Composition can preserve grounds and unresolved alternatives, then support an explicit posture: continue under stated conditions, requalify, contain/hold, escalate or preserve UNKNOWN. The strongest direct support is for bounded parts of T1 and T2. T4 has a credible route but an unresolved economy and timing question. T3 remains an essential coupled obligation whose satisfaction cannot be inferred from the informational mechanism. A full requirement route may need all four simultaneously.

### 3.5 What this adds to the existing hypotheses

The architecture's own hypotheses already express the bridge. **H3, compositional residual expansion**, is the concern that critical use of lossy representations can hide qualifications or produce false confidence. **H4, bounded preservation**, is the proposed opportunity to retain enough context without disclosing full internal state. The scientific precedents make these paired hypotheses well-motivated; they do not establish their comparative performance at equal resources.

**H1, boundary-aware local closure**, preserves insufficient determination within the current observation window U rather than forcing a binary answer. **H2, residual-scope explicitness**, separates confidence conditional on U from the open residual beyond it. A scoped unresolved output or a sound conservative answer set can serve these different aims. Operational support must also avoid unbounded HOLD and unjustified system-wide confidence.

**H5, dynamic ecosystem pressure**, connects freshness and dependency churn to the continued validity of a position. **H6, calibrated window selection**, asks whether adapting breadth and effort improves the risk/resource trade-off. The monitoring and dynamical precedents motivate examining these relations. Neither says that widening is always beneficial or that more agents automatically improve determination.

No additional hypothesis family is needed for this note. Its contribution is a scientific rationale for investigating the existing six.

### 3.6 A bounded example through the requirement route

Consider two actors delivering “ready.” The receiving joint operation has an independently established requirement that both qualifications refer to the same version of one shared specification. Each actor's B includes the version supporting its readiness. The declarations concern the same operation and relevant time horizon, and their attribution and interpretation are established.

| Available qualification, with both operative outputs still “ready” | Answer to the specific version condition |
|---|---|
| Both current qualifiers name version v1. | This condition is supported. Other readiness conditions remain separate. |
| One names v1 and the other v2. | This condition is not met. Local readiness does not establish joint readiness. |
| A required qualifier is missing or stale. | This condition is not established. Missing qualification is not proof of a mismatch. |
| The qualifiers concern different subjects or incomparable time frames. | Comparison is not justified; scope correspondence must be resolved first. |

This small construction needs neither a probability distribution nor the source data behind readiness. Version metadata preserves the distinction needed for this review question. The same-version rule is essential: systems permitting cross-version compatibility require a different review. The result of the review is A of that reviewing function, although its inputs are B relative to the source functions.

If the position changes from the first row to the second, **S9/S11** require preservation of the distinct grounds and **S3/S10** address the changed basis. **S5/S14** require a known mismatch to remain distinct from an unestablished judgment. This is a concrete candidate contribution to T1 and T2, not a fulfilled end-to-end route.

The version mismatch is established; detecting it does not automatically turn B into C. Under the declared same-version rule, the current joint qualification fails that condition. A separate question may then concern a permissible adaptation: if the adaptation is assessable with known variables and a valid method, its unperformed assessment can remain in B. If a formerly established assessment basis has ceased to apply but a grounded exploration route remains, that specified aspect can move from B to C. Missing grounds for either a route or a barrier leave its component role unestablished. The earlier position is retained historically.

EA/EP could qualify a position that requires stricter review of the version-dependent scope. Whether to pause, obtain an adaptation, change permissions or proceed through an established compatibility path belongs to the applicable authority and control functions. An authorized participant may perform a local reassessment autonomously within its existing mandate; a need to change that mandate must remain explicit. T3 applies to any response, and T4 requires the complete path to remain timely and proportionate.

The canonical routes require **T1–T4 for S3, S5 and S14**; **T1, T2 and T4 for S10**; and **T2 and T4 for S9 and S11**. The hypothesis routes are **H1, H5 and H6 for S3/S10**; **H1, H2 and H5 for S5**; **H2, H3 and H4 for S9/S11**; and the applicable H1–H6 components for S14.

For this example, the **explicit-indeterminate rate** distinguishes a missing qualifier from a confirmed mismatch. Material-break precision/recall and false-continuation rate concern the change branch; owner/version/scope/dependency preservation and residual-scope preservation concern the handoff. Correlated-evidence error and false-convergence rate apply to designated S9/S11 branches with repeated or dependent evidence, rather than being mandatory measurements in every simple version comparison. Where a non-null response is proposed, authority-field completeness and authorized-response compliance join the relevant deadline and response-margin measures.

**Qualification-loss rate** measures required qualifiers lost, flattened or contradicted in a handoff without declared requalification. Correctly withdrawing an expired qualification is not itself such a loss. These measures illustrate the inherited, branch-specific routes; they are not a replacement KPI list, and no measurements are claimed here.

If both actors omit the version distinction and no other qualified source supplies it, “ready” cannot recover it. If the indication arrives too late, it cannot provide advance protection. If a competent comparison system already carries and interprets adequate version qualification, the example supplies no special advantage for EA.

### 3.7 Where C and D contribute without becoming quantified uncertainty

The version example mainly concerns B. A different bounded case clarifies what composing C and D can contribute.

Suppose a declared joint decision requires a qualified assessment of dependency G from its assigned evidence providers. That assessment was previously supplied. In the current position, one provider reports a C frontier relevant to G: it has grounds for beginning an exploration but lacks the variables to assess its extent, cost or yield. Another reports a D limit concerning G: with its present access and method it has no effective route to evaluate the material effect, although it may still monitor related indicators. Their operative answers remain valid within their narrower scopes.

If the reports can be mapped to the same dependency and current decision, and the declared evidence view contains no still-applicable assessment satisfying that requirement, composition can establish a **gap in the supplied basis for joint reliance**. It does not establish a dangerous state of G, its probability, or that no actor anywhere knows more. The C route may identify where a bounded inquiry could begin; the D report identifies why the other provider cannot presently close the gap. A repeated C/D label without these grounds adds no such conclusion.

The gap-to-reserve example in §1.4 adds a possible route toward closing this supplied-basis gap: expose a compatible capability, preserve what still needs evaluation, and revise the qualification only as the required grounds become available. Mapping the route is a direct informational contribution; completing it remains conditional on access, authority and actual evidence.

This bears on S5/S9/S11/S14 and, when it changes a previously supported position, S3/S10. The appropriate result is a scoped requalification under the existing materiality and response rules, not an automatic global stop. A reporting-policy change could explain the loss of supplied qualification even if G itself has not changed.

Suppose another participant already has a characterized B basis for evaluating one aspect of G. If its eligible qualifiers supply missing variables and a valid assessment method for the receiving process, that specified part of the receiver's C may become B—as illustrated by the bounded inspection-cost example in the preceding note. The same scope can therefore begin as C for one actor and B for another. This transition relies on usable assessment grounds, not on averaging component labels or counting reports.

The source qualification must retain its provenance, scope, time and method. It does not automatically become the receiver's result, and a route excluding source A cannot admit it by relabeling it metadata. Likewise, another actor's knowledge does not automatically remove a participant's D barrier; an effective route to evaluation must become available within the relevant frame. A new monitoring observation can justify reviewing that barrier while leaving the unresolved effect in D.

## 4. Assessment of the claim

The argument identifies distinctions that operative outputs can conceal, mathematical precedents for preserving target-relevant information, and monitoring precedents for reviewing conditional validity. The examples connect those ideas to specific requirement routes without reconstructing hidden source data. Scope alignment and metadata interpretation remain supplied conditions; this note does not derive them from message similarity.

The appropriate claim is **conditional functional plausibility**: suitable profiles could support material context awareness and qualified repositioning. The direct case is strongest for preserving distinctions and recognizing a changed basis. Anticipatory dynamical warning is a further, model-dependent possibility. No result here establishes autonomous collective convergence, optimal positioning, adversarial resilience or whole-system self-healing.

Full-system fulfillment remains open because input quality, semantic correspondence, useful aggregation, timing, authority and economic viability must hold together. The proposal would narrow or fail for a profile if the metadata cannot distinguish material cases, composition loses the useful relation, manipulation defeats qualification, or added burden consumes the response window. Its differential value would also disappear if a competent alternative achieved the same result at equal or lower burden. Subsequent assessment should use the existing scope-specific KPIs under matched evidence, authority and resources; an improvement in one scope cannot compensate for an unresolved material failure in another.

This companion supplies grounds for such an investigation. It supplies neither an automatic TRL assignment nor a claim that the complete architecture has already been validated.
