# Research Neighbours and Experimental Precedents for Ecosystem Awareness

**Optional addendum · v0.1 · 2 October 2026 · local research draft.** Companion to [00M v0.6](./00M_ABCD_AND_MATHEMATICAL_PLAUSIBILITY_v0.6_RESEARCH_NOTE.md) and [00N v0.5](./00N_FROM_MECHANISM_TO_REQUIREMENTS_SCIENTIFIC_PLAUSIBILITY_v0.5_RESEARCH_NOTE.md). It identifies useful precedents and bounded research comparisons. It reports no EA experiment and adds no canonical requirements or hypotheses.

## 1. The closest intellectual neighbours

The useful question is specific: can participants locate and use decision-relevant distinctions or assessment capabilities that their operative answers alone do not expose?

| Neighbour | Connection to the proposal | Transfer boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Task-relative abstraction and distributed compression | A summary may preserve distinctions sufficient for a specified question without preserving every source datum. See 00M §4 and 00N §§2.1–2.3. | The relevant question and sufficient representation must be justified; compactness alone establishes neither relevance nor privacy. |
| Epistemic logic of distributed knowledge | Separates information implicitly available across a group from what members know after communication. [E1] | EA's partial, qualified disclosure is not full information pooling; declarations need not be true, complete or mutually accessible. |
| Transactive memory and knowledge awareness | Locating existing knowledge, and making that location usable, is distinct from giving everyone the same information. See 00N R9–R11. | An unperformed but characterized B evaluation is a capability, not an already stored answer. |
| Exploration–exploitation in organizational learning | Motivates revisiting how effort and benefits are distributed. See 00N R8. | The proposed B/C distinction is local to EA; the broader literature does not supply its canonical classifier or a common utility function. |
| Assumption monitoring and dynamical warning | Distinguish loss of a basis for reliance from prediction of a modeled transition. See 00N §§2.4–2.5. | A missing qualification is not a physical transition; an arbitrary C/D declaration is not a dynamical precursor. |

Ågotnes and Wáng explicitly distinguish distributed knowledge from the result of actually sharing information [E1]. That supports keeping *available somewhere*, *reachable through a qualified route* and *established for this recipient* separate. It does not justify placing EA in a universal logical ordering between “everyone knows” and full distributed knowledge. Equal messages also do not establish knowledge or independent corroboration.

These connections support an intellectual position that preserves scoped evidence, different perspectives and responsibility. They do not entail a political doctrine or a requirement for decentralization: the proposed semantics can be used with centralized or distributed execution. A claim that the complete synthesis is historically novel would need a separate prior-art review.

## 2. Experimental references: positive results and useful limits

### 2.1 Locating expertise: a positive reference

Stasser, Stewart and Wittenbaum (1995) gave three-person groups different subsets of clues in a mystery task. Making members aware of who held additional information improved discussion of unshared clues and identification of the correct suspect. Telling members only about their own additional information did not provide the same benefit. [E2]

**What to borrow:** compare private capability awareness with a mutually interpretable map of relevant capability. **What not to infer:** the clues already existed; this did not test producing a new assessment from an unused B reserve, open C/D, or minimum-cost metadata.

### 2.2 Knowledge maps: better information use did not establish better performance

Engelmann and Hesse (2011) compared 20 spatially distributed triads given maps of collaborators' knowledge with 20 without those maps. The maps improved how early unshared information entered discussion and how it was used and processed. They were not sufficient to improve collaborative problem-solving performance. [E3]

**What to borrow:** measure locating and using information separately from the quality of the resulting decision. This is particularly close to EA's proposed mapping benefit. The maps also exposed richer knowledge structures and underlying information; they were not an EA metadata-only interface.

### 2.3 Shared training: a positive, less isolated comparison

Liang, Moreland and Argote (1995) compared training together with individual training before a later group radio-assembly task. Jointly trained groups recalled more of the procedure and produced better-quality radios; the authors' analysis associated the advantage with transactive memory. [E4]

**What to borrow:** inspect effective capability use as well as declaration accuracy. **Limit:** shared training and prior group experience differ between conditions; the experiment does not isolate the effect of a capability map alone.

### 2.4 Available information can remain unused

Stasser and Titus (1985) studied groups whose individually biased candidate descriptions collectively contained a better basis for choice. Discussion tended to sustain the initial preference rather than recover the best-supported candidate. [E5]

**What to borrow:** include a case in which useful information exists across participants but remains unintegrated. A successful EA comparison must improve on competent coordination, not merely outperform an intentionally silent group. The study does not show that every consensus is wrong.

The summaries above use the studies' reported findings, not a replication or independent reanalysis. Publisher abstracts were checked for E2–E4; the original article was inspected for E5. No effect size or engineering savings are inferred from them.

## 3. Two bounded comparisons worth investigating later

**Existing knowledge.** Start from a distributed-information task with a correct scoped answer. Compare an established expertise directory or information-sharing method with the same route plus EA's proposed scope, dependency, validity and unresolved-state qualifications. Keep the underlying facts and eligible access matched. This asks whether EA contributes beyond knowing whom to ask. If the conventional route already preserves the needed distinctions at lower burden, that profile supplies no differential benefit for EA.

**Unperformed but characterized evaluation.** Give a provider a valid B assessment option it has not activated, and other participants a relevant C frontier or D barrier. Compare how a competent coordination method and a qualified EA mapping expose the opportunity. Record mapping, effective route availability and evidence-based gap closure separately. The source's test result must remain unavailable until the assessment is actually performed. This examines an extension beyond locating existing knowledge; it is a proposed comparison, not a finding of the cited experiments.

These comparisons can address bounded parts of T1/T2 and the T4/H6 burden question. Any proposed non-null response also needs the applicable authority and response-safety conditions. Their purpose is to refine the current plausibility question, not to certify all requirements.

## 4. Economy without a fictitious collective score

For a reuse-only comparison with a defined recipient set, compatible scopes and the same required outcomes, one possible cost question is:

\[
C_{\mathrm{shared,total}} < C_{\mathrm{baseline,total}}.
\]

The shared total includes finding the capability, acquiring evidence, adaptation, authorization, privacy, communication, waiting and review. The baseline is an actual competent alternative, which may already reuse results; it is not necessarily a sum of isolated evaluations. Both sides must meet the required accuracy, freshness, legitimate-use and response conditions for each recipient. Costs must be expressed in justified comparable units or retained as separate resource dimensions.

This is a conditional cost comparison, not a theorem of benefit. One newly available critical assessment may matter even without reuse or lower cost. Conversely, collective value does not imply a provider has an incentive or obligation to perform it. Open C/D contents need no invented probabilities or utilities for a mapping opportunity to be identified.

## 5. Scope of the broader research programme

Questions about invisible work, capability removal, automation and organizational rationalization can be valuable extensions. They require their own causal definitions and evidence. A regression interaction alone does not establish that automation caused a loss, and observing a beneficial deviation does not establish that the deviation was indispensable. Those questions should not replace EA's present claim about qualified metadata, scope correspondence and requalification.

The present addendum therefore prioritizes expertise location, knowledge maps and information-use failures. It neither audits an entire Structural Awareness programme nor endorses every bibliographic or ideological claim made about it.
