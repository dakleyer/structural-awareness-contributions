> **Result.** Relative to the foundational language explicitly extracted from 01/02 below, P1–P6 form a covering set of operational normal forms. Every primitive failure term expressible using the four epistemic positions A/B/C/D, the Type-0/1/2 taxonomy and the already-declared search, reception, closure, temporal and composition operators reduces to at least one P1–P6 violation. A genuinely independent P7 would therefore have to introduce a new primitive operator, qualifier or failure class not present in the declared foundation.

---

## 1. Why a syntactic proof is needed

02A establishes semantic derivation: the meaning of each P is conserved from identifiable upstream invariants.

That is not yet the strongest possible statement. Two statements can preserve meaning while still leaving the reader unsure whether the six operational principles are merely one convenient re-description among many.

02B asks a sharper question:

> If the Foundation is written as a small formal language, what are the operational normal forms generated by that language?

The purpose is not to replace 01 or 02. It is to make the derivation inspectable at the level of syntax.

## 2. Foundation language LF

### 2.1 Epistemic position

For a material decision domain \(d\) at time \(t\), let:

\[
E(d,t)\in\{A,B,C,D\}
\]

where the symbols retain the 01/02 meanings:

- **A** — sufficiently determined state for the active mission/scope;
- **B** — defined in-window indeterminacy;
- **C** — recognized potentially knowable out-of-window state;
- **D** — structural residual that cannot be presumed exhaustively discoverable.

These positions are qualifications, not a closed partition of the world.

### 2.2 Failure/condition class

Let:

\[
\tau\in\{T0,T1,T2\}
\]

with the current foundational meanings:

- **T0** — structural non-determination despite correct management;
- **T1** — uncertainty acknowledged but not bounded into legitimate closure;
- **T2** — uncertainty suppressed or promoted into unjustified certainty.

T0 is a condition, not a management failure. Its management error occurs when it is misclassified into T1 or T2.

### 2.3 Operational operators

The Foundation already reasons over a small set of transformations. 02B names them without adding new semantics:

\[
\mu\in\{\mathrm{LOCAL},\mathrm{SEARCH},\mathrm{RECEIVE},
\mathrm{ACT},\mathrm{SHIFT},\mathrm{COMPOSE}\}.
\]

- **LOCAL** — use/interpret a qualified state inside the current decision frame;
- **SEARCH** — perform further observation, retrieval, compute, review or escalation;
- **RECEIVE** — consume a judgement/claim from another participant;
- **ACT** — convert a qualified state into operational closure, permission or execution;
- **SHIFT** — carry a previously qualified judgement across time/context change;
- **COMPOSE** — combine several local/received states into one larger decision.

These names merely expose operators already present in I0/I1/I2, O0/O1/O2, E0/E1/E2, regime-transition material and the General Law of Epistemic Composition.

### 2.4 Material qualifiers

A judgement may carry material qualifiers:

\[
Q=\{
scope,\ source,\ provenance,\ freshness,\ dependency,\ authority
\}.
\]

The Foundation repeatedly requires these qualifiers to remain decision-relative through handoff, temporal change and composition.

### 2.5 Well-formed judgement

A primitive judgement is represented as:

\[
J=\langle subject,proposition,decision,d,t,E,Q\rangle.
\]

This is a formal reading aid for the existing subject–proposition–decision and \(E(d,t)\) semantics. It is not a mandatory runtime schema.

## 3. Primitive failure terms

A primitive foundational failure term is:

\[
F=\langle\tau,\mu,J\rangle
\]

possibly with one material qualifier from \(Q\) identified as the locus of the failure.

The syntax therefore asks three questions:

1. **What epistemic position is being represented?**
2. **Which Type-0/1/2 condition/failure is occurring?**
3. **Under which operator does the qualification fail?**

No P1–P6 label is used to construct this language.

---

## 4. Reduction rules

The following reduction rules are extracted from the controls already present in 01/02.

### R0 — classification / residual preservation

If a state cannot be sufficiently determined, its actual qualification must remain distinguishable: A is not silently substituted for B/C/D; structural residual is not erased; received qualification is not silently strengthened.

This is the root A/B/C/D preservation rule.

### R1 — Type-1 boundedness

For SEARCH over unresolved B/C state, the determination path must have a finite decision-relevant stopping/fallback relation under available capacity and useful response time.

This is I1/O1 and received E1-I/E1-O in common form.

### R2 — Type-2 local non-promotion

ACT must not convert known unresolved, conflicting, missing or stale material state into certainty-equivalent closure merely because action is required.

This is I2/E2-I at the local closure boundary.

### R3 — qualifier preservation under received authority

When RECEIVE/COMPOSE carries an authority/representation claim, its scope, provenance, current applicability and non-amplification boundary must remain sufficient for the receiving action.

This is the authority-typed case of received-signal qualification plus the Foundation's explicit authority-boundary rule.

### R4 — temporal requalification

SHIFT across a material change invalidates unqualified reuse of the earlier judgement. The affected basis must be requalified before ACT.

This is the time-indexed form of the dynamically requalified \(E(d,t)\) model.

### R5 — compositional non-substitution

COMPOSE must preserve material scope, source dependence, compatibility and unresolved coupling. Local validity, repeated signals or controls in another domain cannot be promoted into ecosystem-wide support by aggregation alone.

This is O2/E2-O plus the General Law of Epistemic Composition.

## 5. Normal forms P1–P6

Apply R0–R5 to the primitive operators.

| Foundational syntax | Operational normal form | Current principle |
|---|---|---|
| classification/residual preservation under LOCAL/RECEIVE | qualification must state what is sufficiently established and what residual remains | **P1** |
| T1 under SEARCH | unresolved determination must be finitely bounded and viable | **P2** |
| T2 under ACT at the local decision boundary | unresolved material state must not become permission/certainty | **P3** |
| received/combined authority qualifier under RECEIVE/COMPOSE | current decision-sufficient non-amplifying authority must survive reliance | **P4** |
| material basis under SHIFT → ACT | material change requires action-time requalification | **P5** |
| source/domain/dependency qualification under COMPOSE | local states must not silently substitute for ecosystem truth | **P6** |

The table is not the proof; §§6–7 establish coverage and the condition for a new principle.

## 6. Foundational normal-form theorem

### Theorem

For every primitive failure term \(F\) well formed in \(L_F\), if \(F\) violates the root epistemic discipline of 01/02, then \(F\) violates at least one member of \(\{P1,\ldots,P6\}\).

### Proof by operator/failure case

Take any well-formed primitive failure term \(F=\langle\tau,\mu,J\rangle\).

1. If the failure is a misclassification or unjustified strengthening of A/B/C/D before a distinct closure/transition operator is material, R0 applies and the failure is a **P1** violation.
2. If \(\tau=T1\) and \(\mu=SEARCH\), R1 applies and the failure is a **P2** violation.
3. If \(\tau=T2\) and \(\mu=ACT\) at the local decision boundary, R2 applies and the failure is a **P3** violation.
4. If the failed qualifier is authority/representation and reliance crosses RECEIVE/COMPOSE, R3 applies and the failure is a **P4** violation.
5. If a previously qualified judgement is reused after material change under SHIFT→ACT, R4 applies and the failure is a **P5** violation.
6. If several local/received judgements are combined and the failure is scope/dependence/compatibility substitution under COMPOSE, R5 applies and the failure is a **P6** violation.

The operators can overlap. A concrete failure may violate more than one P. The theorem requires coverage, not a disjoint partition.

Therefore every primitive foundational failure term is covered by at least one P1–P6 normal form. ∎

## 7. Principle-closure corollary

Let \(P_7\) be a proposed additional operational principle.

If \(P_7\) uses only:

- A/B/C/D;
- Type 0/1/2;
- the operators LOCAL, SEARCH, RECEIVE, ACT, SHIFT, COMPOSE; and
- the existing material qualifiers in \(Q\),

then every primitive failure prohibited by \(P_7\) is already covered by one or more P1–P6 normal forms.

Therefore \(P_7\) can be:

1. a specialization of an existing P;
2. a conjunction of existing P obligations; or
3. an alternative formulation of the same covered failure surface,

but it is not a new primitive principle class.

For \(P_7\) to be independent at the same abstraction level, it must exhibit at least one new primitive:

\[
\mu^\* \notin
\{\mathrm{LOCAL},\mathrm{SEARCH},\mathrm{RECEIVE},\mathrm{ACT},\mathrm{SHIFT},\mathrm{COMPOSE}\},
\]

or a new qualifier/failure class not reducible to the current \(L_F\).

That is the falsifier for the six-principle closure claim.

## 8. Relationship to Type 0/1/2

The reduction also clarifies why there are six operational principles rather than three.

Type 0/1/2 are **failure/condition classes**, while P1–P6 are **operational normal forms after the failure classes are lifted through the dynamic/distributed operators**.

- Type 0 chiefly creates the need to preserve correct epistemic classification and residual: **P1**.
- Type 1 creates the bounded-determination obligation: **P2**.
- Type 2 at local closure creates **P3**.
- Type 2 under authority-bearing reception/delegation creates **P4**.
- Type 2 through stale temporal reuse creates **P5**.
- Type 2 through aggregation/cross-domain substitution creates **P6**.

So P4–P6 are not new failure types. They are operator-specific manifestations of the same foundational epistemic discipline.

---

## 9. Relationship to A/B/C/D

A/B/C/D provide the state vocabulary on which every normal form operates.

- **P1** preserves the distinction directly.
- **P2** governs justified movement from B/C toward A without unbounded determination.
- **P3** prevents B/C/D-qualified material state from being relabelled A by forced closure.
- **P4** prevents an authority claim received from one bounded state/scope from becoming a stronger A-like permission at another scope.
- **P5** prevents an A-qualified judgement at \(t_0\) from remaining A by assumption after a material change at \(t_1\).
- **P6** prevents several local A-qualified judgements from being composed into an unjustified ecosystem-level A.

This is the syntactic bridge from the four-pole epistemology to the six operational principles.

---

## 10. Boundary

This proves **relative syntactic closure**, not metaphysical finality.

A future theory may legitimately add:

- a new primitive operator;
- a new kind of decision-relevant qualifier;
- a new failure class not reducible to Type 0/1/2; or
- a materially different ecosystem object not representable by the current decision-frame language.

If that happens, the theorem should fail and P1–P6 should be extended or refactored.

Within the declared 01/02 language, however, a seventh primitive principle has no uncovered syntactic failure surface.
