&nbsp;

APQ benchmark consequence. Acquisition-pathway qualification does not earn differentiation merely by selecting a secure, fast, reputable or low-cost source. The test is whether decision-relative acquisition, under the current scope/open-residual/non-fungibility state, changes the supported decision or preserves a viable response better than a strong conventional selector.

&nbsp;

F3 Local Epistemic State Qualification

Common antecedents: confidence/UQ, guardrails, evaluator/critic patterns, explicit indeterminate verdicts.

Differential element: evaluates the local output against the four-pole position and six in/out-window controls without rerunning the whole domain task.

Assessment: building blocks established; combined semantics not found as a first-class peer function.

&nbsp;

F4 External Epistemic Signal Qualification

Common antecedents: attestation verification, provenance, uncertainty communication, reputation/evidence appraisal.

Differential element: treats “source says u=0.3” as a scoped external epistemic claim and preserves unknown qualifiers/window/residual instead of inheriting the value as local certainty.

Assessment: strong analogues, but generic cross-source epistemic qualification not found as one function.

&nbsp;

F4 remains downstream of APQ: a conventional or EA pathway selector may choose a source, but F4 still qualifies the actual received result. The benchmark should not credit EA merely for selecting a secure/fast/reputable channel.

&nbsp;

F5 Scope-Indexed Epistemic Composition & Coupling Assessment

Common antecedents: evidence fusion, dependency graphs, uncertainty propagation, orchestration aggregation.

Differential element: epistemic position is non-fungible across domains; correction in d2 does not compensate failure in d1 unless the dependency is materially established. Detects closure laundering, cross-domain compensation, source duplication and stable agentic epistemic fragmentation.

Assessment: strongest candidate for distinct architectural contribution.

&nbsp;

F6 Systemic Epistemic & Operating-Frame Assessment

Common antecedents: risk posture, incident severity, trust evaluation, runtime monitoring.

Differential element: keeps the management verdict (Sound/Type1/Type2/Mixed by domain) separate from structural Type-0 markers, and keeps both orthogonal to operating posture (Normal/Containment/Migration). A correctly represented Type-0 condition can therefore coexist with Sound management and Normal operation. v0.2 additionally records when the current observation burden is materially mismatched to mission sensitivity/capacity without turning that mismatch into a new failure class.

Assessment: no equivalent first-class separation found in reviewed peers.

&nbsp;

F7 Requalification & Corrective Directive Generation

Common antecedents: retries, guardrails, escalation, retrieval, human review, containment, routing.

Differential element: correction is selected by epistemic failure and domain, not generically “more verification.” It may explicitly request less search, preserve indeterminate, change W(d,t), seek independent evidence or move toward migration. In v0.2 the same targeting rule can stop/narrow low-value awareness effort or increase/redirect observation when the current frame is too narrow for the domain's sensitivity.

Assessment: mechanisms common; targeting logic potentially distinctive.

&nbsp;

F8 Epistemic Statement & Envelope Generation

Common antecedents: A2A extensions, attestation results, conformance records, provenance envelopes, confidence signalling.

Differential element: a transport-neutral handoff that preserves proposition/scope/window qualification/uncertainty semantics/inherited indeterminacy/residual/capacity sufficiently for downstream epistemic composition.

Assessment: fields have precedents individually; combined EHD semantics not found as a current general agent interoperability primitive.

&nbsp;

F9 Outcome Feedback & Revalidation

Common antecedents: traces, evals, persistence, checkpointing, control feedback, return-to-operation.

Differential element: observed outcome can selectively re-enter F1/F2/F3/F4/F5/F6 according to which assumption failed, rather than simply rerun the workflow. v0.2 also uses outcomes to learn whether the previous observation burden was too high (wasted search/escalation/capacity depletion) or too low (missed change/stale-frame surprise/hidden exposure).

Assessment: feedback loops established; epistemic re-entry semantics are the distinguishing element.

7\. What appears genuinely different

The audit does not support claiming that any one of the following is new by itself:

\- uncertainty quantification;

\- uncertainty propagation;

\- confidence signalling;

\- context engineering;

\- risk-based control selection;

\- resource-bounded search and context budgeting;

\- adaptive monitoring/observation;

\- value-of-information or cost-sensitive evidence acquisition;

\- human oversight;

\- provenance;

\- attestation;

\- observability;

\- multi-agent orchestration;

\- containment;

\- epistemic-agent framing.

&nbsp;

The candidate differential combination is narrower:

&nbsp;

A. Four-position epistemic state by domain

The architecture distinguishes sufficiently determined in-window state, defined in-window indeterminacy, recognized potentially knowable outside-window state, and structural residual that cannot be presumed eliminable.

&nbsp;

B. Twelve control surfaces

The same Type 0/1/2 logic is evaluated internally and on externally received epistemic claims, rather than assuming that correct local UQ automatically survives composition.

&nbsp;

C. General Law of Epistemic Composition

Epistemic states are scope-bound and non-fungible. Different biases or controls in different domains do not average into an epistemically balanced system.

&nbsp;

Acquisition-pathway diversity is one application of non-fungibility: different routes do not create independent epistemic value when they carry the same upstream evidence, and a lower-rated route may have greater marginal decision value when it contributes genuinely independent evidence to the unresolved domain.

&nbsp;

D. Agentic epistemic decoupling

Local functional coherence can produce stable global epistemic fragmentation because role-specialized agents have no intrinsic obligation to harmonize epistemic positions across domains.

&nbsp;

E. Risk/capacity-indexed double-loop requalification

The system first qualifies the decision-relevant window from mission sensitivity/exposure, consequence/reversibility, tolerated residual, finite observation/determination capacity and response capability, then continuously re-evaluates local/external epistemic emissions and can re-enter window/context qualification when the frame loses validity or when the awareness burden itself becomes materially too high or too low.

&nbsp;

F. Orthogonal epistemic condition and operating posture

Type 0/1/2 are not themselves Normal/Containment/Migration states. Correctly managed residual uncertainty can coexist with normal operation; Type 1/2 can trigger targeted correction without automatically implying migration.

&nbsp;

G. Transport-neutral epistemic handoff

The EHD does not demand shared internal reasoning or a common UQ algorithm. It requires enough scope/window/uncertainty/residual/dependency semantics to prevent downstream epistemic collapse.

&nbsp;

No reviewed peer was found that combines A–G as one runtime functional/interface architecture. v0.4 does not add a new H-item to inflate the novelty list: the risk/capacity calibration remains a refinement of E and of F1/F2, and APQ remains an application of the General Law plus that requalification logic. Both must earn differentiation empirically.

&nbsp;

This is a novelty hypothesis, not yet a novelty proof. The next validation step must test whether these combined distinctions solve concrete failure cases better than peer architectures that use ordinary orchestration, UQ, HITL, guardrails and provenance separately.

&nbsp;

8\. Freeze recommendation and use-case sequence

The architecture should now enter provisional freeze, not final freeze.

&nbsp;

Recommended sequence:

1\. preserve F1–F9 and O1–O6 / S1–S13 as the fixed functional/interface set in Functional Architecture v0.4, with v0.1 retained as the pre-integration baseline;

2\. design a small set of adversarial/diagnostic use cases specifically chosen to exercise Type 0/1/2 in-window, Type 0/1/2 out-of-window, external epistemic-signal qualification and cross-domain composition;

3\. run each case against EA and against at least one plausible peer composition using conventional orchestration/UQ/HITL/guardrails;

4\. change the architecture only if a use case exposes an uncovered responsibility or an unnecessary function;

5\. after those cases, freeze the functional/interface architecture and move to minimum interoperability semantics/conformance.

&nbsp;

The cases therefore come after the first coherent interface architecture exists, but before final interface freeze. They are architecture-validation cases, not merely examples added after standardization.

10\. Use-case comparison protocol — similarities and differences must remain explicit

For every architecture-validation Use Case, the benchmark must document both overlap and differentiation. The comparison is not allowed to present an existing mechanism as an EA invention merely because EA uses it.

&nbsp;

Each Use Case comparison should record:

\- peer baseline architecture or paradigm used for comparison;

\- shared functions/mechanisms: what both architectures already do;

\- shared interfaces/artifacts: what both consume or emit in materially similar form;

\- peer-specific strengths that EA reuses rather than replaces;

\- EA-specific added behavior being tested;

\- responsibilities present in EA but absent as a first-class peer function;

\- responsibilities present in the peer but intentionally outside EA ownership;

\- whether the peer can be configured to reproduce the EA behavior without adding a new epistemic layer;

\- for APQ branches, whether a strong conventional selector using cost/latency/QoS/trust/reputation and explicit source-lineage/correlation information where available can reproduce the same scoped pathway choice and non-collapse behavior;

\- observed outcome under the same frozen case facts;

\- ecosystem sensitivity/exposure and consequence/reversibility assumptions held constant or deliberately varied;

\- observation/determination budgets and actual awareness-resource consumption;

\- whether additional observation changed the decision or only consumed capacity;

\- resulting novelty status: already existing, partial overlap, composition-level difference, or unsupported claim.

&nbsp;

The use-case benchmark must therefore answer two questions independently:

1\. Similarity — how much of EA is assembled from established agentic, security, uncertainty, orchestration and interoperability mechanisms?

2\. Difference — what additional epistemic behavior appears only when those mechanisms are composed through the EA principles, functions and interfaces?

&nbsp;

A positive result for EA is not that a peer lacks HITL, retrieval, provenance, attestation, UQ, guardrails, observability or containment. Those mechanisms are expected to exist. The relevant result is whether the peer composition preserves and requalifies epistemic position by domain, including window sufficiency, external epistemic claims, structural residual and non-fungible cross-domain composition, without adding an EA-equivalent layer. For v0.2 it must also show that adaptive W(d,t) produces a better risk/resource frontier than plausible fixed-broad and fixed-narrow alternatives under comparable facts and resources; otherwise the risk/capacity refinement is unsupported.

&nbsp;

This comparison protocol applies to UC-EA-01 onward and should be carried forward into every case/stress-test record.
