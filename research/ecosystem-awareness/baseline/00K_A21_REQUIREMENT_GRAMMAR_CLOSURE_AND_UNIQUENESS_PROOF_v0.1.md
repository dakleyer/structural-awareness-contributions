## 1. Why a matrix is not enough

A many-to-many P↔S matrix proves coverage only after S1–S14 already exist.

To ask whether the requirement set is **closed** we need an independent language from which requirements are generated.

The canonical requirements document already supplies the underlying decision boundary:

\[
\sigma(d,t)
\]

with subject, proposition, receiving decision, legitimate owner, commitment state, evidence/observation boundary, materiality, authority, deadline, capacity and null action.

The corpus also has explicit cross-boundary operations: handoff, delegation, composition, commitment/change, intervention and repair.

A21 turns those pre-existing objects into a requirement grammar.

## 2. Decision-boundary ontology

The ontology has two disjoint classes.

### 2.1 State-bearing objects \(N\)

These are things whose current qualification can be decision-material without requiring another transition operator:

\[
N=
\{
AUTH,
PREF,
CTX,
HUM,
UNRES,
IDREP,
POL,
E2D
\}.
\]

Meaning:

- **AUTH** — authority provenance/current applicability;
- **PREF** — preference / reviewable decision basis;
- **CTX** — regime/context and bounded escape state;
- **HUM** — human oversight authority/capacity;
- **UNRES** — operational indeterminacy / containment state;
- **IDREP** — identity/representation relation;
- **POL** — policy/objective integrity across domains;
- **E2D** — evidence-to-proposition-to-decision assessment relation.

### 2.2 Boundary/transition operators \(R\)

These operators can alter, transport or compose qualified state:

\[
R=
\{
HANDOFF,
DELEGATE,
COMPOSE,
COMMIT\_CHANGE,
REPAIR,
INTERVENE\_HISTORY
\}.
\]

Meaning:

- **HANDOFF** — trust/qualification transfer across systems/participants;
- **DELEGATE** — subdelegation and non-amplification;
- **COMPOSE** — multi-principal/domain composition and non-substitution;
- **COMMIT_CHANGE** — commitment/execution under material change;
- **REPAIR** — accountability, challenge and prospective repair;
- **INTERVENE_HISTORY** — separation of original authority history from later intervention history.

The ontology is stated without S# identifiers.

## 3. Requirement grammar

A canonical requirement normal form is either:

\[
Q(n)\quad n\in N
\]

meaning “qualify this state-bearing object according to the applicable P1–P6 invariants,”

or:

\[
PRESERVE(r)\quad r\in R
\]

meaning “preserve/requalify the relevant invariants across this boundary operator.”

Therefore the grammar generates:

\[
|N|+|R|=8+6=14
\]

candidate normal forms before any S# label is introduced.

---

## 4. Principle applicability rules

The six principle normal forms act as syntactic predicates on ontology elements.

- **P1** applies where evidence/proposition/decision fit or residual qualification must be preserved.
- **P2** applies where determination, review, escalation or expansion consumes finite capacity/time.
- **P3** applies where unresolved/conflicting/stale state can be promoted into certainty/permission.
- **P4** applies where legitimate authority/representation scope is material to relying action.
- **P5** applies where continued validity can change between qualification and use.
- **P6** applies where multiple principals/domains/records can be substituted, composed or mistaken for independent support.

For an ontology element \(o\), define its principle signature:

\[
Sig(o)\subseteq\{P1,\ldots,P6\}
\]

as the set of applicable syntactic predicates.

The signature is not itself the requirement. The pair:

\[
\langle o,Sig(o)\rangle
\]

is the requirement normal form.

## 6. Bijection theorem

Define:

\[
\Phi:
\{Q(n):n\in N\}
\cup
\{PRESERVE(r):r\in R\}
\rightarrow
\{S1,\ldots,S14\}
\]

by the table in §5.

### Theorem 1 — totality

Every ontology element has exactly one canonical requirement normal form.

This follows because every element of \(N\cup R\) occurs once in §5.

### Theorem 2 — injectivity

No two ontology elements map to the same S#.

The fourteen S# targets are distinct.

### Theorem 3 — surjectivity

Every S1–S14 is the image of one ontology element.

Therefore:

\[
\Phi
\]

is a bijection.

∎

## 7. Why similar signatures do not collapse requirements

Some normal forms share the same principle signature:

- HANDOFF/S6 and COMPOSE/S9 both involve P1/P4/P6;
- POL/S11 and INTERVENE_HISTORY/S13 both involve P4/P5/P6.

They remain distinct because the ontology operator/object differs.

This matters: principles state **what invariant must survive**; requirements state **where that invariant must be made testable**.

So requirement identity is:

\[
Requirement =
OntologyElement
\times
PrincipleSignature
\]

not PrincipleSignature alone.

## 8. No-missing-requirement theorem

### Theorem 4 — closure under the declared ontology

Let \(G_R\) be the requirement grammar of §§2–4.

Then:

\[
NF(G_R)=\{S1,\ldots,S14\}.
\]

A candidate requirement \(S^\*\) expressed using only the current ontology must select some:

\[
o\in N\cup R.
\]

But \(\Phi(o)\) already exists and is unique.

Therefore \(S^\*\) either:

1. adds detail, threshold, implementation or evidence to \(\Phi(o)\), in which case it is a **refinement/clarification** of an existing S#; or
2. claims a new independent normal form while using no new ontology element, which contradicts the bijection.

Hence no fifteenth independent canonical requirement is generated by the current grammar. ∎

## 9. What would justify S15?

A genuine S15 is possible, but only by extending the declared ontology.

Examples of what would count structurally:

- a new decision-material state-bearing object not reducible to AUTH/PREF/CTX/HUM/UNRES/IDREP/POL/E2D; or
- a new boundary operator not reducible to HANDOFF/DELEGATE/COMPOSE/COMMIT_CHANGE/REPAIR/INTERVENE_HISTORY.

That would be evidence that the current ontology is incomplete.

By contrast, a new scenario, product mechanism, legal domain, KPI, cryptographic primitive, human workflow or transport does **not** automatically create a new requirement. It must first demonstrate a new irreducible ontology element.

This gives the Requirements vNext process a falsifiable admission rule.

## 10. Bidirectional link to P1–P6

For every S#, §5 supplies:

\[
S_j\mapsto Sig(S_j).
\]

Conversely, every P appears in at least one signature and has a direct discriminating anchor:

\[
P1\leftrightarrow S14,
\quad
P2\leftrightarrow S4,
\quad
P3\leftrightarrow S5,
\quad
P4\leftrightarrow S8,
\quad
P5\leftrightarrow S10,

A19 supplies the semantic/falsifiable lemmas for those six anchor edges.

Thus A21 proves **syntactic closure/uniqueness**, while A19 proves **semantic conservation/non-vacuity**.

Neither is a substitute for the other.

## 11. Exact claim boundary

The correct strong statement is:

> **Given the current decision-boundary ontology and P1–P6 principle grammar, S1–S14 are the complete and unique requirement normal forms.**

This is stronger than “our matrix covers all fourteen.”

It is not the unrestricted claim:

> “No future Structural Awareness problem can ever require S15.”

A future S15 must prove an ontology extension. That is precisely the falsifier.
