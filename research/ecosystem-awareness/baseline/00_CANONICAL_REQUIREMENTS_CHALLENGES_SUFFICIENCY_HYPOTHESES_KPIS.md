The DAOS source case uses `T0 → T1 → T2`: a basis is established, a commitment relies on it, and a material condition changes or becomes insufficiently established. In this document, **T1–T4 are condition identifiers**, not those case times.

**Bidirectional provenance and reference-scenario application.** DAOS is one concrete source of the challenge taxonomy. ### 1.1 Common language for every challenge, hypothesis and KPI

The test unit is a **material subject–proposition–decision boundary**: the entity or action concerned, the claim being assessed, and the receiving decision that will rely on it. In the EA [dictionary](./01_FOUNDATIONAL_THEORY_v0.5_INTEGRATED.md#readers-dictionary--scope-qualified-non-normative), these are the [subject, proposition and decision domain](./01_FOUNDATIONAL_THEORY_v0.5_INTEGRATED.md#readers-dictionary--scope-qualified-non-normative); **scope** is the coverage to which the resulting statement applies. A scope is therefore not merely a technical field or a business label. It answers: *which subject, which claim, which decision, under which owner, time, evidence and operating conditions?*

For readability, this document calls that boundary `σ(d,t)`. It is a reader notation, not a new runtime schema. Every S#, T#, H# and KPI must name the same `σ(d,t)`, or explicitly state the material dependency that links one scope to another.

- [**W(d,t)**](./00_CANONICAL_ARCHITECTURE_TOPOLOGY.md#1-canonical-topology) is the active observation and semantic window selected for decision `d` at time `t`. It is bounded and repeatedly qualified; it is not maximum context collection.
- **Non-fungibility:** a correct control, approval, search result or extra evidence in `d2` does not compensate for a material knowledge fault in `d1` unless a represented dependency and fresh evidence show that it actually requalifies `d1`. See the [ecosystem-composition principle](./02_EPISTEMIC_SAFETY_PRINCIPLES_CONTROL_MATRIX_v0.4.part02.md#ecosystem-composition-principle--no-cross-domain-epistemic-cancellation).
- **Non-monotonicity:** more tokens, signals, agents, telemetry or context are not presumed to produce more determination. They can add latency, correlation, conflict, privacy cost or human-capacity depletion. Expansion is justified only when it changes the qualified decision or preserves a viable response window.

- **Failure-trajectory rule:** Type 1 and Type 2 remain the only two management-failure classes, but a run may move between them. Timeout, default, defensive approval or reviewer pressure can force an unresolved Type-1 path into Type-2 closure; later contradiction of a compressed Type-2 closure can reopen as Type-1 HOLD/search when its discarded basis is unrecoverable. False convergence, divergence, oscillation and cascade are measured trajectories or consequences, not additional types. Defensive `UNKNOWN`, qualifier saturation and injected doubt are classified by whether they create unbounded delay/containment (Type 1) or suppress/force unsupported closure (Type 2).

This prevents a new vocabulary at each stage: S# identifies the problem surface for `σ(d,t)`; T# states the condition the solution must show; H# states why the EA principles predict that condition can be met; and the KPI records supporting or falsifying evidence for that same scope.

## 2. Challenges S1–S14

The following are the complete solution challenges exposed by the DAOS case. A candidate need not solve every challenge. It must declare which challenge dimensions it addresses, what it consumes from other owners, and where it stops. A locally correct component does not establish a whole-system result by itself.

| ID | Challenge and full requirement | EA relation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **S1** | **Authority provenance and current applicability.** Establish who conferred authority, on whom, for what purpose and scope, and whether it remains binding at commitment and action time; include origin, standing, expiry, revocation, composition and evidence. | Upstream authority fact. EA consumes its qualification and must not issue or invent the grant. |
| **S2** | **Preference fidelity and reviewable decision basis.** Show that the decision still represents the principal’s declared preferences, hard limits and permitted trade-offs, at the decision-basis level without requiring disclosure of every internal model step. | Upstream decision-basis fact. EA does not own preference interpretation. |
| **S3** | **Regime, context, escalation and bounded escape path.** Distinguish normal escalation—confirmation, revalidation or change of authority while current rules remain in force—from an exceptional, governed escape path after a material frame change. Declare trigger, evidence, threshold, affected scope, what remains valid, review and return conditions. A false or injected signal must not expand permission or suspend the whole system. Repeated transitions require an evidence-change rule, expiry and hysteresis or an equivalent anti-oscillation control. | Direct EA requalification surface; authority and enforcement remain external. |
| **S4** | **Human-inclusive oversight authority and capacity.** Reach a human or human-held role that is authorized, available, informed, competent and able to intervene within the useful response time, with real means to pause, redirect or reverse action. A nominal role is not effective oversight. | Direct EA capacity and qualification surface; EA does not redesign the oversight lifecycle. |
| **S5** | **Operational indeterminacy and containment.** Manage incomplete, conflicting or stale authority, preference and delegation facts without converting them into permission. Preserve evidence and canonical links; contain propagation; define hold, rollback or escalation before effects leave control parameters; enter a governed safe state if rollback is not possible. An `UNKNOWN` or a large qualifier set must not become an automatic system-wide veto: materiality, duplication, expiry, incentive and affected scope remain explicit, and a timeout/default that forces closure is treated as a response requiring qualification. | Direct EA residual and qualification surface; containment execution is authorized downstream. |
| **S6** | **Interoperable, privacy-preserving trust determination.** Independent agents, public systems, providers and relying parties must verify enough authority and status in real time without exposing full agendas, preferences or unnecessary personal data. | EA uses a bounded epistemic handoff; it does not own identity, trust transport or privacy authorization. |
| **S7** | **Identity and representation link.** Distinguish the principal, organization or role, acting human/agent, relevant agent instance or substitute, and their current representation relationship. Similar output or behaviour is not proof of correct attribution. | External identity/representation function; EA preserves its qualification and does not infer authority from output similarity. |
| **S8** | **Bounded subdelegation and non-amplification.** Preserve purpose, scope, time, hard limits, relevant preferences and revocation conditions through agent chains. Subdelegation must not manufacture authority absent from the original principal. | External delegation requirement; EA preserves inherited uncertainty and qualification limits. |
| **S9** | **Multi-principal composition, non-substitution and conflict.** Compose independently valid citizen, employer, provider, operator and public-authority determinations without one silently replacing another. Preserve concurrence, conflict, priority, hold and escalation without assuming a universal hierarchy. Detect both incompatible divergence and unsupported convergence caused by correlated evidence, imitation or shared compressed closure. | Direct EA composition surface; EA does not set a universal authority hierarchy. |
| **S10** | **Commitment state, material change and normal escalation.** Distinguish recommendation, negotiation, reservation, binding commitment and execution; detect when a material change of cost, route, time, context, policy or authority requires confirmation, revalidation, cancellation or change of authority. Silence or delay is not permission. | Direct EA frame-requalification surface. |
| **S11** | **Policy, objective and preference integrity across domains.** Preserve owner/source, version, scope, priority and conditions across systems, organizations and jurisdictions. Do not let a new or lower-priority preference overwrite a hard limit; preserve conflicts and record the determination. Represent material cross-domain couplings; a relation such as regulation × product or supplier × operations must not disappear merely because no single specialist owns both sides. | EA consumes source-native policy state and tests non-substitution in composition. |
| **S12** | **Accountability, challenge and repair.** Reconstruct the identity, role, grant, policy, preference, evidence and contextual determination that applied; allow challenge and repair of future state without rewriting the historical record. | External record/accountability owner; EA may use the observed outcome for requalification and learning. |
| **S13** | **Authority history versus intervention history.** Keep the original authority record and a later human or technical intervention as distinct historical objects while producing one unambiguous current operative determination. An intervention may confirm, restrict, suspend or replace authority without erasing provenance. | EA qualifies the current result; it does not overwrite authority provenance. |
| **S14** | **Evidence-to-decision assessment.** State at each transition what must be demonstrated, what evidence is required, whether it is sufficient, insufficient or inconclusive, and which decision it supports. Record any Type-1↔Type-2 transition and the arbitration rule—material dependency, legitimate veto, timeout/default, owner, expiry, re-entry and evidence-versus-authority role—that determines what advances. This is a reusable assessment layer, not a mandatory runtime architecture. | Direct EA assessment surface across all profiles. |

### 2.1 Decision frame and test branches

Before assessment, declare the decision, legitimate owner, commitment state, null action, T0 basis, T2 change or insufficiency, evidence boundary, residual, materiality threshold, authority, response deadline, capacity and full cost ledger.

The minimum test branches are valid continuity; observed material change; insufficiently established condition; adversarial or poisoned evidence; capacity/deadline constraint; cross-domain handoff; false convergence from correlated or imitated closure; defensive `UNKNOWN`; qualifier saturation; and repeated posture reversal. A command receipt, elapsed time, confidence score or human approval is not an outcome unless the declared observable effect is recorded.

## 3. Sufficiently-good conditions T1–T4

The following four conditions adapt the structure of the “Sufficiently Good” requirements in *Minimalistic Regime-Aware Early Warning Systems* to the changed-decision-basis problem. They do not transfer its detector theorem, action guarantee or validation result to EA, DAOS or another system.

| Condition | Requirement | Source requirements retained in full | Insufficient evidence or behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **T1 — Material awareness within a declared boundary.** | Distinguish a material break in the applicable decision basis from ordinary variation and state which breaks are observable and distinguishable under the supplied evidence conditions. | **Material-break awareness:** declare decision, relevant basis, change family, materiality/detectability threshold, observation boundary and useful horizon. **Declared coverage boundary:** declare coverage domain, threshold, calibration/assumptions and relative-completeness limitation. Where composition matters, declare the cross-domain interaction and shared-dependency branches capable of producing false convergence, divergence or cascade. | Claiming to “adapt to change,” to detect every change, or to see the whole ecosystem without a declared boundary; treating every deviation as material; inferring validity merely because several dependent actors converge. |
| **T2 — Qualified determination and owner-preserving handoff.** | Produce an explicit, reviewable posture—continue under stated conditions, requalify, contain/hold, escalate or preserve UNKNOWN—and carry the information needed by the legitimate receiving owner. | **Qualified operating posture:** declare posture vocabulary, transition conditions, affected scope, owner, authority and expiry/review condition. **Owner-preserving handoff:** carry material scope, provenance, freshness, dependencies, unresolved state, capacity and authority limits; declare receiving responsibility, missing-field treatment and re-entry/requalification path. Qualifiers are selected by decision materiality, deduplicated/versioned and expired rather than accumulated without bound. | A confidence score, alert or trace with no reliance consequence; a message that collapses UNKNOWN to PASS; an immaterial, duplicated, stale or defensive UNKNOWN that automatically blocks the whole system; assuming authority over another system’s controls. |
| **T3 — Bounded and authorized response.** | Couple a posture only to an authorized response whose failure, reversibility, externalities and residual downside are declared. Where the strongest class is claimed, show each non-neutral response is no worse than the null action for every covered admissible state. | **Bounded-downside response:** declare null action, allowed responses, response deadline, authority, reversibility, externalities and residual risk. Timeout, default, forced approval and automatic containment are responses, not neutral absence of decision. Declare transition/re-entry and hysteresis or equivalent anti-oscillation control. **Strong action-safety / PNI:** declare stakeholders, utility model, horizon, admissible states and action library, and establish `U(A(p), ω) ≥ U(A_null, ω)` for every covered `ω`. | Treating an alert, approval or agent intention as permission; allowing injected doubt to create unbounded containment; silently closing a Type-1 queue through default; labelling expected-utility, low-friction or reversible action “PNI” without the per-state condition. |
| **T4 — Timely, viable and minimum-sufficient requalification.** | Reach a justified posture while a legitimate response remains possible, with finite and proportionate evidence, computation, coordination and human-review effort. Extra layers may be added only with their semantic, latency and conflict effects visible. | **Viable decision support:** declare cost/resource ledger, capacity limits, marginal decision value and baseline. **Non-monotone composition:** state retained capability, contradiction treatment and incremental burden/failure modes, including qualifier and posture-transition burden. **Timely qualification:** declare time budget, response margin, compute/communication/review capacity and fallback. **Minimum sufficient intervention:** declare stop/contain rules, observation-expansion criterion, response objective and evidence that added effort is decision-relevant; use a bounded authorised experiment when action can obtain material evidence safely. | Unlimited search, escalation or review presented as safety; repeated posture reversal without new material evidence; qualifier volume that consumes the response window; a retrospective answer after action is no longer changeable; assuming more telemetry, agents or controls are automatically better. |

These conditions are jointly constraining. A timely alert without an authorized response is insufficient; a permitted response without material-break qualification is insufficient; and a rich control plane that loses scope, authority or unresolved state is insufficient.

## 4. Foundational hypotheses H1–H6

These six hypotheses are the research hypotheses of the EA foundation. They are not established results and are not identical to the later EA differential. A system following the EA foundation should make them testable under a declared scope and resource envelope.

| Hypothesis | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| **H1 — Boundary-aware local closure** | Systems that explicitly represent insufficient determination inside U and finite determination resources will avoid some deadlock and false-certainty failures caused by forcing every local closure into a binary determined state. |
| **H2 — Residual-scope explicitness** | Separating confidence conditional on U from the open residual `R_U` beyond that bounded representation will reduce unjustified local-to-global confidence inflation in downstream decisions. |
| **H3 — Compositional residual expansion** | Under comparable evidence and computational resources, systems whose critical downstream decisions depend solely on lossy compressed representations will exhibit greater hidden residual and/or false confidence than systems with better context preservation or independent-source access. |
| **H4 — Bounded preservation** | A bounded determinacy/context envelope can preserve enough decision-relevant information to reduce architecture-induced residual without requiring disclosure of full internal state. |
| **H5 — Dynamic ecosystem pressure** | For a fixed observation and verification budget, increasing participant/dependency churn and shortening ecosystem-state validity will increase the operational importance of residual-state preservation and requalification even when local algorithms remain unchanged. |
| **H6 — Risk-capacity calibrated window selection** | Systems that adapt observation-window breadth, freshness and determination effort to mission sensitivity, consequence severity, available capacity and response horizon will achieve a better risk/resource frontier than systems using fixed broad or fixed narrow observation under otherwise comparable conditions. |

### 4.1 Coordination trajectories do not create H7

The H1–H6 wording above remains the complete hypothesis set. The new trajectory branches refine how it is tested:

- H1 covers legitimate bounded closure and whether Type 1 is forced into Type 2, or a prior Type 2 reopens as an unrecoverable Type-1 loop.
- H2 and H3 cover both incompatible divergence and premature false convergence when local scope, correlated evidence or compressed closures are promoted system-wide; cascade is measured as propagation and timing, not assumed as an independent cause.
- H4 covers whether a bounded envelope preserves material qualification without full-state disclosure **and without qualifier saturation**.
- H5 and H6 cover whether churn, injected doubt or repeated posture reversal is detected and bounded before capacity or response margin is exhausted.

These readings add no new hypothesis and do not assert that every run must exhibit every trajectory.

## 5. KPIs and falsification protocol for H1–H6

All compared configurations use the same frozen case facts, available evidence, authority, action library, deadline and human/tool budget. Report a numerator, denominator, branch and configuration for each rate. Do not pool heterogeneous branches into an unsupported score. The operational C–H measures retained from the prior protocol are measurement instruments here, allocated under the H# they test; they are not a second set of hypotheses. The common outcome vector is:

`[false continuation, false containment, qualified-posture correctness, deadline pass, handoff integrity, observable outcome effect, total decision burden]`.

| Hypothesis | KPI set and calculation | What would falsify or limit the hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **H1** | **Local determinacy margin:** declared determined evidence/conditions relative to the threshold for the decision; report with evidence and sample coverage inside U. **Explicit-indeterminate rate:** outputs preserving INDETERMINATE/UNKNOWN where the branch oracle requires it ÷ applicable branches. **Material-break recall:** oracle-declared material breaks exposed with affected basis ÷ oracle-declared material-break branches. **Material-break precision:** exposed material breaks confirmed by oracle ÷ exposed material-break alerts. **False-continuation rate:** CONTINUE/PASS after a break requiring requalification or containment ÷ applicable break branches. **Time in HELD/unresolved state** and **human-capacity binding/escalation demand** are reported with each branch. | A candidate forces unresolved state into binary closure, continues after a material break without exposing the basis, or avoids false continuation only by indefinite HOLD/escalation that exhausts declared capacity. |
| **H2** | **Local-to-global confidence inflation:** downstream confidence/closure beyond the declared U and scope, reported against the branch oracle. **Wrong-domain or systemic-closure rate:** unsupported system-wide closure from local/insufficiently scoped determination ÷ applicable composition branches. **Residual-scope preservation:** required unresolved, scope and residual fields retained ÷ cases requiring them. **Known versus unmeasured dependencies** and **inherited-indeterminacy detection rate** are reported for each receiving decision. | Downstream consumers reach the same global closure even when U, residual, scope or unresolved conditions differ; residual is silently omitted or converted into PASS. |
| **H3** | **Source diversity and primary-source retrievability:** independent source routes and retrievable primary sources ÷ required material source routes. **Correlated-evidence error rate:** closures treating duplicated/correlated evidence as independent corroboration ÷ designated correlated-source branches. **Compression exposure:** number and criticality of downstream decisions dependent on one compressed source; propagation depth of compressed closures. **Systemic decision error** and **false confidence from recursive closure reuse** are reported against the oracle. | A lossy compressed path and a context-preserving/independent-source path show no material difference in hidden residual, false closure or coordination distortion under comparable evidence and resources; or the claimed advantage disappears once latency, disclosure and review burden are included. |
| **H4** | **Handoff integrity:** required `scope, provenance/freshness, dependency, unresolved state, capacity, authority, expiry` fields delivered and interpretable at receiver ÷ required fields. **Qualification-loss rate:** qualified fields absent, flattened or contradicted without declared requalification ÷ handoffs. **Information retained/discarded at inter-agent boundary:** required decision-relevant fields retained ÷ required fields, with **latency, bandwidth, privacy and disclosure cost**. **Targeted re-entry precision/recall:** correctly reopened assumptions ÷ reopened assumptions; correctly reopened assumptions ÷ oracle-invalidated assumptions. | The bounded envelope loses material scope, provenance or residual needed by the receiver; preserving it requires full internal-state disclosure or produces no usable decision change. |
| **H5** | **Estimated U-invalidation rate:** material changes invalidating a declared U/window ÷ relevant observation intervals. **Freshness/staleness:** age of each material source at determination and number of stale inputs relied on ÷ required material inputs. **Churn:** participant/dependency changes and window changes by domain per period. **Requalification latency** and **missed ecosystem changes from Type-2 under-observation** are reported against fixed observation/verification budget. **Containment frequency and recovery success** distinguish a bounded response from repeated unresolved escalation. | Increasing churn or shortening validity does not change residual/requalification demand under fixed budget, or the candidate hides the change through stale reuse, missed material changes or unbounded containment. |
| **H6** | **Window breadth and freshness by domain:** observation scope and source age for each decision, compared with sensitivity/consequence/reversibility. **Deadline-pass rate:** permitted posture with reachable authorized response before deadline ÷ applicable runs. **Remaining response margin:** deadline minus posture time. **Decision-relevant evidence yield:** acquisitions/reviews changing justified posture or action set ÷ total acquisitions/reviews. **Total decision burden:** compute, tokens, bandwidth, tool, communication, waiting, reviewer, privacy/disclosure and response cost per run. **Risk/sensitivity mismatch:** qualified-window assumptions inconsistent with observed downstream consequence ÷ applicable runs. **Marginal decision value:** additional decision-relevant changes ÷ incremental awareness cost. | A fixed broad or fixed narrow window achieves equal/better error, deadline and burden results under matched facts; adaptation arrives after the useful response window or consumes capacity without decision-relevant improvement. |

### 5.1 Response-safety measures required wherever H1–H6 lead to action

The following measures apply to any hypothesis test that recommends, authorizes or executes a non-null response:

- **Posture correctness:** runs whose posture is within the branch oracle’s permitted set ÷ applicable runs.
- **Authority-field completeness:** handoffs containing owner, authority, scope, freshness, dependency and expiry fields ÷ handoffs requiring them.
- **Authorized-response compliance:** non-null actions within the declared authority/action library ÷ non-null actions.
- **Reversibility/containment compliance:** applicable responses meeting their declared bound ÷ applicable responses.
- **False-positive response cost:** cost of unnecessary non-null actions ÷ valid-continuity runs.
- **PNI test, only if claimed:** covered admissible states in which `U(A,ω) ≥ U(A_null,ω)` ÷ all declared covered admissible states. A PNI claim requires 100%; any counterexample defeats that claim.

These measures do not give EA authority. They test whether a candidate has kept assessment, posture, legitimate owner, permit, action and observed effect distinct.

### 5.2 Cross-cutting Type-1/Type-2 trajectory and stress measures

Use these measures only on declared branches where the corresponding mechanism is injected or observable. They are allocated to H1–H6 as stated below and do not form a new hypothesis set.

| Measure | Calculation | Primary H# / condition link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Type-1→Type-2 forced-closure rate** | Runs in which acknowledged unresolved state is closed by timeout, default, queue pressure or approval without new qualifying evidence ÷ runs reaching the declared Type-1 boundary. | H1; T2/T3/T4. |
| **Type-2→Type-1 reopened-loop rate** | Invalidated false closures that enter repeated HOLD/search because discarded material qualification cannot be reconstructed ÷ invalidated Type-2 closures. | H1/H4; T2/T4. |
| **False-convergence rate** | Runs in which multiple actors produce the same unsupported closure from correlated, imitated or recursively reused evidence ÷ designated convergence branches. | H2/H3; T1/T2. |
| **Incompatible-posture exposure** | Duration and consequence-weighted extent of incompatible postures over the same material decision or resource-time segment. | H2/H3/H5; T1/T2/T4. |
| **Posture oscillation rate** | Reversals among continue/requalify/contain/HOLD within the decision horizon, reported with dwell time and the share occurring without new material evidence. | H1/H5/H6; T2/T3/T4. |
| **Qualifier-saturation burden** | Immaterial, duplicated, stale or expired qualifiers and rechecks ÷ all transmitted qualifiers/rechecks, with latency, reviewer load and missed-deadline effect. | H4/H6; T2/T4. |
| **Defensive-UNKNOWN effect** | Unsupported or strategically emitted UNKNOWNs that transfer responsibility or trigger HOLD/containment ÷ designated incentive-stress branches; report whether the path remains Type 1 or is later forced into Type 2. | H1/H4/H6; T2/T3/T4. |
| **Injected-doubt effect** | False HOLD/containment or posture reversal caused by poisoned freshness, dependency or uncertainty signals ÷ designated adversarial branches; report rejection/recovery time. | H1/H5/H6; T1/T3/T4. |
| **Cascade latency and reach** | Time from first material qualification loss to dependent closure reversal, plus affected downstream scopes/decisions. | H3/H5; T1/T2/T4. |

A high `UNKNOWN` rate is not by itself failure, and a low one is not by itself success. The branch oracle, materiality, incentives, scope, owner, response window and downstream effect determine whether uncertainty was correctly preserved, defensively inflated or silently suppressed.

## 6. End-to-end traceability: one scope, one test route

The preceding sections are not four independent checklists. For each declared `σ(d,t)`, the assessor selects the relevant challenge surface (S#), applies the condition(s) that make that challenge sufficiently good (T#), states the foundation hypothesis or hypotheses being tested (H#), and records the named KPI evidence. The same scope, owner, time, evidence boundary and response horizon must remain visible throughout.

A KPI pass is evidence for the stated hypothesis and condition; it is not by itself proof that a challenge has been solved. A condition is met only when its full requirement, the applicable evidence and the relevant KPIs hold together for the declared scope. Conversely, a failed KPI, missing required field, unavailable owner or expired response window is a direct reason to mark the linked condition insufficient for that run. It must not be averaged away by good results in another scope.

| **S12** Accountability, challenge and repair | T2, T4 | H4, H5 | handoff integrity; provenance/freshness and scope preservation; targeted re-entry; containment recovery; observable outcome effect | ownership of the historical record or repair process |
| **S13** Authority versus intervention history | T2, T3 | H2, H4 | authority/provenance field preservation; qualification-loss rate; authorized-response compliance; action/outcome trace | overwriting or curing source provenance |
| **S14** Evidence-to-decision assessment | T1, T2, T3, T4 | H1–H6 as applicable | the common outcome vector plus the condition-specific KPI and trajectory set above; arbitration/precedence record | a mandatory runtime architecture or automatic permission to act |

### 6.1.1 S14 aggregate reading

S14 is an aggregate assessment route, not a separate breadth score. A declared test names the material component or components it exercises: **(1)** evidence sufficiency; **(2)** Type-1 / Type-2 transition; **(3)** conflict, arbitration or precedence record; **(4)** expiry; and **(5)** re-entry and disposition update. The third component records the applicable owner or the absence of one; it does not authorize EA to arbitrate or create precedence. These components reuse the S1–S14, T1–T4, H1–H6 and KPI protocol above; they create no new requirements or KPI taxonomy.

### 6.2 How to run a complete test

1. **Freeze the scope.** Declare `σ(d,t)`: subject, proposition, receiving decision, legitimate owner, commitment state, evidence and observation boundary, materiality threshold, authority, deadline, capacity and null action. Record the selected `W(d,t)`.
2. **Select the challenge route.** Name every S# materially present in that scope and the T# conditions required by the map. If a source function is outside the candidate, name its owner and the handoff/re-entry boundary rather than silently assuming it works.
3. **State the falsifiable expectation.** Name the H# being tested and the comparison configuration. Hold facts, authority, action library, deadline and resource envelope constant across configurations.
4. **Instrument the KPIs.** For every required KPI, declare numerator, denominator, branch oracle, threshold and observable outcome. Preserve UNKNOWN, residual, scope, provenance, freshness, dependency, capacity, authority and expiry through each handoff.
5. **Declare composition and arbitration.** Where several scopes or postures meet, state material dependencies, hard constraints, legitimate veto scope/expiry, timeout/default behaviour, evidence-versus-authority role, re-entry target and anti-oscillation rule. Human approval or a timeout may authorize a response; neither is new evidence about the world model.
6. **Decide condition by condition.** A positive result supports the named H# for this run and contributes evidence to its T# condition. A negative result marks the linked condition insufficient or requires a qualified HOLD/re-entry. Report the scope-specific result; do not trade it against a different domain.

### 6.3 Worked route: material change in one decision domain

Consider `σ(d1,t)`: whether to release a production order for machine M under its current maintenance and safety regime. The subject is machine M and the order; the proposition is that the maintenance/safety basis remains adequate for release; the decision is the authorized release or hold. Its `W(d1,t)` includes the declared maintenance sources, safety regime, owner, current evidence, response deadline and available human review capacity.

The route is **S3** (regime/context), **S5** (indeterminacy and containment), **S10** (commitment/material change) and **S14** (evidence-to-decision). It therefore requires T1 to recognize a material break within the declared boundary, T2 to issue a qualified posture to the legitimate owner, T3 if a hold, release or other response is proposed, and T4 to reach that posture while it remains useful. H1 tests whether unresolved maintenance evidence is preserved rather than forced into PASS; H2 tests whether evidence valid only within the maintained window is not inflated into a global safety conclusion; H5 tests requalification under churn or shortening source validity; H6 tests whether widening the window actually improves the decision before the deadline. H3 and H4 join the route if the decision relies on a lossy cross-system handoff or a bounded preservation envelope.

For this one scope, material-break recall/precision, explicit-indeterminate rate, false-continuation rate, freshness, requalification latency, remaining response margin, human-capacity demand and authorized-response compliance are the linked evidence. If a material safety change is missed, T1 is insufficient. If a handoff drops an unresolved maintenance dependency or produces a PASS without a receiving owner, T2 is insufficient. If a release is outside the action library or a claimed safe response fails the PNI test, T3 is insufficient. If the assessment completes only after the release/hold is no longer actionable, or it consumes the declared review capacity without decision-relevant gain, T4 is insufficient.

Now consider a separate procurement decision `σ(d2,t)` with extra telemetry or additional agents. Its apparently good result cannot compensate for the missing maintenance evidence in `d1`. It may change `d1` only if a represented material dependency, fresh evidence and requalification show how it bears on the machine-release proposition. Likewise, adding more telemetry or agents to `d1` is not an improvement merely by volume: the H6 evidence-yield, burden and response-margin measures must show that the expansion improves the qualified decision instead of consuming the viable response window.

## 7. Brief differential hypothesis for EA

The universal requirements above do **not** presume EA. They ask what any sufficiently-good candidate must make testable. EA’s separate, lower-priority research question is whether a strong peer composition can meet the same requirements without an equivalent semantic layer.

The four EA-specific differential propositions are: scoped non-fungible determination; proportionate decision-window requalification; separation of epistemic condition from operating posture and independent actuation authority; and interoperable targeted re-entry. They are falsified or narrowed if a strong, equally resourced peer reaches the same decisions, residual trace, timeliness and burden without equivalent semantics. They are not an assertion that no other system can meet them, and they do not establish superiority.
