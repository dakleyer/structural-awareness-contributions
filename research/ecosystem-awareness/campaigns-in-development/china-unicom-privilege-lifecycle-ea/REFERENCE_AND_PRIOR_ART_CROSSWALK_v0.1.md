## 1. How to read this bibliography without inflating the claim

A supplied 8-page research-reference PDF provided **235 URL occurrences** across candidate publications, draft links, duplicates, current and withdrawn editions, vendor pages and unrelated results. Its leading paragraph describes a proposed standards crosswalk, but the substantive following pages are primarily a **URL inventory rather than verified one-source-to-one-claim analysis**. That inventory is a discovery aid, not 235 independent attestations or 235 validated evidence items.

This crosswalk **curates official/primary publication pages and declared version status**. An established standard in its **own scope** is a reference for a known capability; it does **not** certify any implementation of CU-HIB-01, endorse an EA design, or convert optional local proposals into mandatory #23 requirements.

**Credibility hierarchy:**
- **S (source-owner proposal):** [Ying Wang / Ye Tao Theme #23](https://github.com/FG-TIDA/themes/issues/23) and dated contributor comments. This is the closest source for which behavior the authors *propose*; no adopted ITU Recommendation status is established.
- **N (approved standard / Recommendation / final specification):** other organizations' published conformance scope. They can establish prior art or a viable interoperable baseline **only within that scope**.
- **G (institutional guidance or informational RFC):** established architecture and assessment method, not proof that an external implementation implements it.
- **I (implementation-specific technical reference):** a concrete, competent design possibility, not a source-neutral standard or measured competitor until admitted.
- **L (local research candidate):** CU-SP01–10, EA/EHD 04, R1/R2/R3 and experimental controls; documentation and hypotheses, **not adopted normative assertions**.

Primary authority is kept distinct: #23 owns privileges and PDP semantics; producer domains own current graph/criticality; actual PEP owner enforces effects; #16 owns its human-intervention path; the local EA programme proposes a qualified context/evidence handoff.

**Novelty/duplication rule:** avoid “no standards exist for contextual authorization.” The established standards above directly refute that broad premise. The more bounded open research question is whether a **versioned, decision-scoped and independently owned multi-domain basis** survives hibernation/activation and later cross-domain context change with useful explicit uncertainty and authorized reentry. **Even that narrower gap is a hypothesis to test, not a proved market-wide absence.**

### Suggested insert for the article, citation-ready

> Existing authorization and security frameworks already include environment-aware policy evaluation, policy decision/enforcement separation, continuous security-event exchange and evidence provenance (NIST SP 800-162; OASIS XACML; OpenID AuthZEN and CAEP; W3C PROV-DM). CU-HIB-01 does not claim to invent those capabilities or to demonstrate a market-wide absence. The open question is whether **the current, decision-relevant and source-owned basis of a suspended privilege** remains sufficiently established after a material **cross-domain dependency change**, with explicit unknown scope, permitted review, timely restoration and verifiable effects. Under a competent conventional implementation, parity or superior conventional performance is an admissible scientific result. Any additional EA/EHD interoperability proposal requires its own independently tested conformance case.

The exact reference URLs are the N01–N12 primary links above. The paragraph is **local argumentation**, not a quote from a standard and not a claim of FG-TIDA endorsement.

## 5. Excluded or lower-priority candidates from the supplied PDF

- **Withdrawn older NIST SP 800-162 versions:** prefer [2019 updated final](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/162/upd2/final) over [2014 page marked withdrawn](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/162/final). Multiple versions are not independent proof.
- **Drafts after an approved final exists:** prefer [AuthZEN Final 1.0](https://openid.net/specs/authorization-api-1_0.html) and [CAEP Final](https://openid.net/specs/openid-caep-1_0-final.html), not intermediate -03/-04/-05 working drafts; prefer [W3C PROV-DM Recommendation](https://www.w3.org/TR/2013/REC-prov-dm-20130430/) rather than earlier proposed Recommendation.
- **Unapproved IETF drafts:** useful prospectively where scope requires them, but not a substitute for [RFC 9334](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc9334/). RFC 9334 itself is **Informational**, not an adopted dependency-graph protocol.
- **ISO 22301, ISO/IEC 25010, NIST contingency-planning and broad AI governance:** may motivate continuity, system quality and resilience but are not specific sources for restored AI-agent privileges. Their relevance must be justified and sourced for a precise statement before adding them as central proof.
- **Software brochures, unrelated search results, scraped downloads and unofficial PDF copies:** do not elevate to a published normative or independent assessment source.
- **ITU-T SG17 Contribution 676** is [cited by the original #23 issue](https://www.itu.int/md/T25-SG17-C-0676/en); its publicly described *proposal of a work item* is not equivalent to an adopted Recommendation. No direct access to its full text or approval outcome is claimed in this crosswalk.
- **Third-party report output from a research assistant** should be treated as lead generation unless the underlying primary documents, precise version status and intended reference-to-claim linkage are checked.
