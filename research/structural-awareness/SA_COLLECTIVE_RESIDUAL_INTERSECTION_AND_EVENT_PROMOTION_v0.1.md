## Abstract and theorem scope

An event unclassifiable to each of N object-level agents may be **actually observed** at their admission boundaries. Although each agent discards it as a substantive object, its **pre-admission source** may record a qualified property explaining the rejection. Multiple agents' compatible, co-referential metaproperties can then establish a **joint task-relevant statement** about the same event, without recovering its full identity, objective score or the unenumerated exterior. A meta-interpreter already resident in an agent can promote the newly encountered event into a **task-specific usable syntactic state**, while the original object-level classifiers remain unchanged.

We prove a conditional qualified-promotion theorem, a strict observational nonfactorization theorem against a permanently discarding closed-report class, and a conditional arrival-time bound. None follows from De Morgan alone; the guarantee that a particular residual event improves the objective requires a separate sound value or feasibility implication. The process may still be physically unable to reach the common residual before an eligible encounter, and an ordinary effective programme with exactly the same pre-admission certificates and rule interpreter can implement the same function.

## 1. The precise De Morgan relation

Let \(\Omega\) be one shared **interpreted** source domain for a declared question. Let \(S_i\subseteq\Omega\) be the region admitted by object-level agent i, not canonical 00M delivered A. Let \(R_i=\Omega\setminus S_i\). Then:

\[
\boxed{\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^N S_i
=\bigcap_{i=1}^N R_i.}
\tag{1}
\]

The dual identity is \(\Omega\setminus\bigcap_i S_i=\bigcup_iR_i\). These are standard De Morgan laws, **not new physical discoveries**. The shared \(\Omega\), source meaning and referent correspondence must be valid; similar words from unrelated agents are insufficient.

**Three obstacles:** (a) nonempty \(R_i\) do not imply nonempty common \(\bigcap_iR_i\); (b) membership in that intersection does not imply high quality; (c) an observed **INDETERMINATE/abstention** does not in general prove the semantic claim \(x\notin S_i\). A qualified rejection certificate and a mere failure to classify have different logical types.

Let \(E_t\) be the physically received, source-qualified events available at admission time t, and \(\gamma_i(e)\) a qualified **metaproperty of the same event e**: a signed boundary condition, interval, evidence-scope relation or relevant rejection reason. The actual joint meta-recognition region is:

\[
H_t=\{e\in E_t:\operatorname{CompatibleSources}(e)\land
\bigwedge_{i=1}^N\gamma_i(e)\}.
\tag{2}
\]

H is not **the entire** A′; it is a possibly empty, source-qualified **observed subset** of what the object-level system could not use. Its predicates require no exhaustive enumeration of all possible unknowns.

## 2. SA-CR1 — nonfungible residual predicates yield a sufficient task property

Let \(F\) be an independently fixed task score, \(F_0\) the existing admissible result, \(C(e)\) the authorized transition cost and \(\underline F(e)\) a conservative lower score bound derived from qualified source and metaproperties. Suppose a valid proof \(\pi\) applies under the current question, time and authority:

\[
\boxed{\forall e\in H_t:\quad
F(e)\ge\underline F(e)>F_0+C(e).}
\tag{3}
\]

**Theorem SA-CR1.** If a real event e arrives in H, the programme can verify the claims, and an **already existing legal and executable adoption mechanism** is available, then admitting e strictly improves the specified net task score. The programme need not compute its exact F, determine every other property of e or identify the global optimum.

**Proof.** Equation (3) implies \(F(e)-C(e)>F_0\). An authorized action realizing e has the stipulated result. No step requires a general semantic decider or oracle. \(\square\)

**Why nonfungibility matters:** a missing property or source right cannot be compensated by many unrelated accurate properties. The conjunction supports (3) only when all necessary premises cover the **same event and question**. A collection of N residual labels alone may convey no lower bound at all. PR #45's bounded source cloud and conditional Lipschitz envelope provide an existing **local** method for computing conservative intervals when regularity, origin, validity and consistency are justified. Without those conditions, no interpolation, objective bound or arbitrary success probability follows.

## 3. SA-CR2 — a strict theorem against the information-discarding comparator

Let \(Z_C(w)\) denote the full report received from a **specified closure-only stage** that irreversibly drops unclassifiable incoming events and all distinctions about their rejection reasons, and lacks replay. Let \(Z_M(w)\) carry the same normal results **plus qualified meta-records preserved at or before admission**. Suppose two admissible worlds require different choices for a fixed task r:

\[
Z_C(w_+)=Z_C(w_-),\qquad
r(w_+)\ne r(w_-),\qquad Z_M(w_+)\ne Z_M(w_-).
\tag{4}
\]

**Theorem SA-CR2 (restricted strict information separation).** No procedure whose complete input is \(Z_C\) can answer r correctly on **both** worlds. If r also factors through the enriched \(Z_M\) under an effective source-qualified decoder and permitted actions, the metasyntactic procedure can distinguish them.

**Proof.** Equal \(Z_C\) forces an identical closed-report decision on both worlds, conflicting with different required r. The positive claim follows from the specified factorization and effective decoder. \(\square\)

This theorem captures the *Given Universe* admission-loss mechanism in a live setting. It is **not** an impossibility theorem against *every* ordinary computer: a classical machine with the same upstream raw access, meta-certificates and interpreter can simulate this effect and ties. Such a machine **does not satisfy the particular irreversibly discarding closure-only information contract**. Scientific claims of exclusive superior computing power would be incorrect.

## 4. An N-dimensional witness with no full-state identification

Consider four agents and a common mathematical coordinate model \(\Omega=[0,1]^4\). Define \(F(x)=\sum_i x_i\), technical upper bound 4, and existing task score \(F_0=2.4\). Each agent i has an object-language admitted band

\[
S_i=\{x:0.25\le x_i\le0.75\}.
\tag{5}
\]

Its **ordinary exported syntax** has only IN_A or DROP. At pre-admission it can additionally provide a source-qualified **ABOVE** assertion when \(x_i>0.75\) or **BELOW** when \(x_i<0.25\), using **only its own measurement**. Such a meta-label is a property of the residual beyond its old admitted band, not the recovery of the whole four-dimensional vector.

The **bare intersection** \(\bigcap_iR_i\) still includes both very poor and very good states: each \(R_i\) is the union of its low and high tails. Hence merely combining four DROP tags is **not** enough. But the qualified high-tail conjunction proves:

\[
\bigwedge_{i=1}^4\mathrm{ABOVE}_i(x)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad F(x)>3>2.4.
\tag{6}
\]

It also proves a **value gap to the known technical upper bound** of less than 1. This is not a guarantee of geometric closeness or governability.

Two possible worlds present the **same opaque event id** and identical ordinary output \((\mathrm{DROP})^4\). The true x vectors are \(x_+=(.90,.88,.83,.86)\) and \(x_-=(.10,.08,.12,.05)\). Their referee-only scores are 3.47 and 0.35. Their pre-admission meta signals are ABOVE×4 and BELOW×4, respectively. The same closed output cannot support a correct promotion decision in both worlds, while the richer labels can. **The metaprocess does not see those true 4D scores**; it sees only side predicates and a proof of (6).

With independently stipulated transition cost 0.1 and a preinstalled authorized adoption route, the high event permits a guaranteed net improvement greater than 0.5; the referee later scores it as 3.37 net. This is a deliberately transparent finite **research witness**, not a real-workload observation or the original PR #57 result.

## 5. Three stages: from suboptimum to pending opportunity to event-triggered promotion

**Stage 1 — restricted syntactic outcome.** All agents remain within their object categories. The reference optimum may be strictly larger than the result reached by those policies. The original PR #57 separates **technical** optimum from governable/reachable optimum and uses operational gaps, not subjective suffering.

**Stage 2 — a shared metaproperty, without a controllable route.** Source-qualified meta-conditions define a region H with the improvement certificate (3). **Mathematical nonemptiness of the coordinate region is not proof that an actual physically available event in the future stream will satisfy it.** To assert a *genuinely existing* but unreachable opportunity, a separately valid real-candidate existence premise is needed. When such a qualified premise applies, the system can represent a **second-order opportunity** whose arrival time and contacting agent remain unknown. This is not an independent cost or additional physical gap to be added to the original technical gap.

**Stage 3 — arrival, qualification and change of operational classification.** A previously unclassifiable physical event is actually received, meta-records align to **that same event**, the implication (3) is verified and the authorized meta-interpreter promotes the event into a task-specific usable input. The object-level old checks need not be rewritten; the **overall program syntax/active rule state changes through a pre-existing rule interpretation path**. A promoted candidate may remain unknown for many other questions.

**Arrival claim, properly qualified.** Open-world mathematical existence alone gives **no finite or almost-sure arrival theorem**. If an independently justified encounter process gives conditional hit probability at least \(\varepsilon>0\) on each eligible observation before the first hit, then:

\[
\Pr(\tau>k)\le(1-\varepsilon)^k.
\tag{7}
\]

This follows by multiplying the bounded conditional failure probabilities, without assuming independent arrivals. Without such an encounter/fairness premise, no such bound follows from De Morgan or L1 OW-9's different **exhaustive H-EXP** condition. In particular, **more intersected dimensions may reduce the encounter rate** rather than increase it.

## 7. Falsifiers and conclusion

The strong claim **fails or becomes conditional** if (i) the common exterior is empty; (ii) negative membership was inferred from mere UNKNOWN; (iii) claims from different events are joined; (iv) a qualified property gives no lower bound for the target; (v) the actual stream never hits the region; (vi) action authority or execution capability is absent; (vii) a source claim is false or stale; (viii) a competing program already retains the same data/proofs; or (ix) costs eliminate the gain. The originally deployed syntactic filter cannot process a residual event which it **irreversibly discarded**; a better equipped ordinary system can implement the same metalevel retention and is thereby outside the strict closure-only comparator.

**Result:** common unknown/undelivered regions can be **source-qualified into useful predicates on an actually encountered event**, not merely abstractly declared nonempty. This can preserve options the old admission rule lost, support a strict task improvement when a candidate appears, and update the effective classification **during execution** without needing to delimit or enumerate all of A′. It is a constructive instance of *Structural Awareness* that remains bounded by source truth, feasible action, arrival and cost.
