## Abstract

A finite agent execution can deliver exact local results without settling the semantic task to which its procedure refers in an open world. When a source ecosystem sends only execution traces to a receiving ecosystem, the receiver may acquire extensive data without enough information to reconstruct the **general question** that those executions were intended to address. When it instead sends a run-local result as a **globally closed set**, it exports an unsupported exhaustiveness claim. The proposed semantic carrier separates **A, the procedure and its declared task**, from **A′, its open non-determination frontier**. A/A′ is not a sum of extensional sets and does not require enumeration of unknown agents. We prove a trace-only nonfactorization result, a false-closure transfer result, and a conditional soundness statement for semantic transfer through a qualified correspondence between the source and receiver's questions. A further result identifies the **weak information dominance** of a signal preserving witnessed boundary information over a reduced signal that discards it, without giving either machine the power to compute A′. The B/C/D distinctions are treated as **partial, process-relative abstractions of the open frontier**, each retaining its own primed non-closure rather than forming a mandatory exhaustive partition. The receiver can thereby preserve **meaning and honest incompleteness** without being given a purported universal semantic oracle or a completed list of every unknown case. This contribution is presented as **implementation-neutral architectural principles**—resilience to indeterminate encounters, preservation of justified source information and weak informational dominance relative to a specifically lossy representation—not as an exclusive technology architecture. The exact comparator is a **two-stage, strictly closure-only architecture** whose exported semantics is confined to A. The new formal continuation distinguishes **in-A outcome-status recognition from the generally impossible semantic meta-classifier**, treats the composition of any K/D/checker chain as one effective procedure, and defines **epistemically coverage-complete** open reports with a single finite witnessed product plus an unenumerated open frontier. Open A/A′ has strictly greater claim expressivity for the non-closed domain in the defined comparator. Implementations independently representing that frontier can remain informationally equivalent, but are no longer closure-only under the comparator definition. Global maximality of all possible information does not follow from Gödel/Turing; the defensible target is faithful preservation of **currently qualified** information without fabricated closure.

## Architectural reading — mathematical principles, not a branded implementation

The results in this note are best understood as **architectural principles for open agentic ecosystems**, obtained by transporting and clarifying ideas from the author's broader mathematical programme, not as the design specification for one proprietary metaprotocol. The author's prior ResearchGate papers include [*Practical Incompleteness*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393799525_Practical_Incompleteness_A_Set-Theoretic_Framework_for_Concrete_Decision_Procedures), [*The Projection Mechanism* (2025)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393802750_The_Projection_Mechanism_How_Practical_Incompleteness_Infiltrates_Formal_Systems), and [*The Projected Infinite Paradox*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393884759_The_Projected_Infinite_Paradox_Unifying_Constructivism_and_Platonism); the distinct [2026 fourth revision of *The Projection Mechanism*](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1OrJ2LWz2qBv2sP8_-VNldWpctxZdwcfI/view) also develops the “Given Universe” boundary. This note **does not assume the strongest historical claims have thereby been proved**. Its formal assertions are TR-1–TR-6 and the limited consequences developed below.

**Resilience principle.** An agent's operational contract should be able to preserve a correct result, record an encountered INDETERMINATE, and carry forward a non-closed meaning without pretending that an observed finite history constitutes the whole world. A correctly implemented system can thereby avoid *specified forms* of false closure and related propagation errors, while managing resource bounds, timeouts and recovery. This is a **resilience affordance**, not a guarantee that every failure is prevented.

**Information-preservation principle.** Source-to-receiver transfer should preserve all **qualified distinctions actually available and relevant to the declared transfer task**, rather than silently projecting them onto an artificially closed list of results. The receiver may use the extra information or ignore it. The existing TR-4 weak-dominance result is precisely this *optional information refinement*.

**Implementation-neutral equivalence principle.** An independently designed system that transfers the same source evidence, open-frontier semantics and receiver qualifications may be information-isomorphic, whatever its technology or names. **It is not the strict closure-only comparator of §6.12**, because it already represents outside-A semantic obligations. A/A′ describes the **reference semantic obligations**, not an exclusive software product.

**Separate four claims:** (1) the open-world computability frontier of L1/OW-1; (2) **strict semantic claim expressivity** over the expressly defined closure-only comparator (TR-10); (3) the finite-message **weak decision-information order** of TR-4; and (4) **strict or net operational value** on a specified task. (1)–(3) have proofs under their respective hypotheses, with (3) fixing the same receiving task, permissible actions and optional extra information; a positive net result in (4) needs additional cost, rights and independent performance evidence. The [open mathematical-lineage PR #60](https://github.com/dakleyer/structural-awareness-contributions/pull/60) develops such a bounded study as **Stage A**, not as an established universal performance theorem.

## Architectural comparison at a glance

**The same algorithms; different external semantic contracts.** In the comparison formalized below, a conventional **closure-first** architecture executes K to establish an operational frame A and then D to produce \(A_1\subseteq A\), reporting only claims expressible inside A. The **open-ecosystem** architecture may run precisely the same composite \(\mathcal P=D\circ K\), with identical run results, but **does not identify K's finite scope with the complete relevant world**. It reports qualified findings from the *entire* composite and an **open A′ frontier** for the original meaning that those findings have not exhausted.

| Closure-first K → A → D → A₁ | Open ecosystem \(\mathcal P\) → \(A_r\) and A′ |
| --- | --- |
| K is treated as supplying the **full represented universe** for all exported claims. | K is one **bounded subroutine** whose results and limitations are assessed like D's. |
| D may correctly handle in-A statuses, including **observed** indeterminate cases. | All those same statuses can be retained; **nothing** has been gained by solving a new halting problem. |
| The **export language** is deliberately restricted to A. | The export language also expresses the **non-enumerated, non-closed semantic frontier** A′. |
| Demanding a total, infallible semantic/metadecidability split over an H-EXP/H-REQ class would require the **forbidden oracle**. | No exhaustive split is demanded: the result is **finite certified output plus an explicitly open residual**. |
| A correctly bounded local answer is allowed, but **does not close the outside world**. | The representation is **complete about its epistemic coverage**, not about the truth of every external case. |

This produces **two distinct mathematical comparisons**: *weak decision-information dominance inside the same observed A* (possibly equality if a closed system already preserves all observed unknowns), and *strict expressivity of claims about the open A′ frontier* against an interface that expressly forbids such claims. The latter does **not require naming any element of A′**. A system which represents an equivalent A′ frontier, by whatever technology, is by definition **not** the strict closure-only comparator.

**Notation.** The comparative working frame A and run output \(A_r\) are distinct from **§1's \(A_{\mathrm{proc}}\)** (executable procedure plus intended task) and from the adopted **00M A** (delivered functional result). The full procedure is denoted \(\mathcal P\); no proof relies on silently equating these objects. Sections 6.16–6.21 and TR-12–TR-14 give the precise arguments.

## 1. Names and mathematical objects

Use \(\mathcal E_S\) for the **source ecosystem** and \(\mathcal E_R\) for the **receiving ecosystem**. Do not call these ecosystems “A” and “B”: those labels would collide with the A/A′ mathematical pair, as well as the adopted A/B/C/D process-relative vocabulary of 00M.

In **this note only**, define:

\[
A_{\mathrm{proc}}=(\Pi,q,\rho,\sigma)
\tag{1}
\]

as **A**, the executable **procedure together with its intended semantic task**. \(\Pi\) is the instruction and its effective subroutines (“execute this N times,” “search for 17 seconds,” “find the nearest eligible neighbor”), \(q\) is the declared **semantic question**, \(\rho\) describes available time, computation, communication and verification resources, and \(\sigma\) declares the relevant predicate, metric, scope, time and interpretation. An instruction merely saying “run N times” without identifying **what it intends to determine** is not yet a complete semantic specification.

For any finite source execution sequence \(r\), distinguish:

\[
L_{S,r}:=\operatorname{Trace}_{\mathcal E_S}(A_{\mathrm{proc}},r),
\qquad
M_{S,r}:=\operatorname{QualifiedMatches}(L_{S,r}).
\tag{2}
\]

The finite trace \(L_{S,r}\) and the witnessed result set \(M_{S,r}\) are **products of executing A**. They **are not A**. A particular run's qualified output may be exactly determined, even though A's intended task refers to an open world.

Define **\(A'_S\)** as the **open semantic frontier** relative to \(\mathcal E_S\) and \(A_{\mathrm{proc}}\): the continuing applicability and generalization obligations of \(q\) **not established by the finite execution record**, together with the fact that no global semantic/inventory closure is available under the original L1/OW-1/OW-6 hypotheses. A′ is a **semantic boundary specification**, not an enumerated collection of all unknown objects, nor merely the list of observed INDETERMINATE status values. Its source-side extension cannot generally be computed as a closed inventory.

The **proposed semantic carrier** is:

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak M_S(A)=A_{\mathrm{proc}}\;\widehat{\oplus}\;A'_S
}
\tag{3}
\]

where \(\widehat{\oplus}\) means **typed coupling**, not numerical addition, ordinary union or literal set complement.

A carries the **instruction, question and interpretation**. A′ carries the essential **non-closure qualification**: the procedures and executions do not exhaust the semantic question's reach. Neither component is usefully replaced by an accumulated bag of old run data.

## 2. Theorem TR-1 — the run history is not the meaning of the procedure

Let \(W_S\) be a class of admissible possible source-world situations. A finite history is an observation map

\[
H_r:W_S\longrightarrow\mathcal H_r,
\]

and the semantic target of A is independently defined as

\[
q_S:W_S\longrightarrow Y.
\]

Assume that the open-world situation contains two admissible worlds whose **entire available run history** is indistinguishable, while the correct semantic answer differs:

\[
\exists w_0,w_1\in W_S:
\quad H_r(w_0)=H_r(w_1),
\qquad q_S(w_0)\ne q_S(w_1).
\tag{4}
\]

**Theorem TR-1 (trace-only nonfactorization).** No function of the finite run history alone recovers the intended semantic answer for both worlds:

\[
\boxed{\nexists f:\mathcal H_r\to Y
\quad\text{such that}\quad q_S=f\circ H_r.}
\tag{5}
\]

**Proof.** Equal histories force equal values from f; the target differs in (4). \(\square\)

This is **the established 00M §4 task-sufficiency/factorization condition applied to finite source executions**, not a new general Turing theorem. OW-1 and OW-6 separately prove a stronger **effective uniform frontier** for the expressive peer family. TR-1 explains why accumulating arbitrarily many **particular finite** execution records, without an appropriate semantic rule and scope, does not authorize a global claim about unseen or future contexts.

Transferring a huge record may be expensive yet leave precisely the missing distinction unresolved. The mathematics proves **semantic insufficiency under (4)**; it does not claim that code is always smaller than its logs or prove an unconditional byte/information ratio.

## 3. Proposition TR-2 — a false closed set exports a semantic error

Let the source return a finite collection of correctly **witnessed** positive cases \(M_{S,r}\). Now suppose it transmits the **stronger** statement

\[
M_{S,r}
=
\{x\in\Omega_S^{\mathrm{relevant}}:P_S(x)\}
\tag{6}
\]

as if its actual execution had certified **all and only** the matches in the open source world.

Under the open-world alternative of OW-7/OW-10, two possible source worlds can yield the **same transmitted finite record** while differing in unobserved, but relevant, matches.

**Proposition TR-2 (false-closure transfer).** In such a pair of worlds, the globally exhaustive claim (6) is false in at least one, even though every individual record in \(M_{S,r}\) is correct. A receiver that uses (6) as a premise imports an **unsupported scope/exhaustiveness commitment**.

**Proof.** The export is identical, but the correct global set differs. It cannot be equal to the same finite set in both worlds. \(\square\)

The transmitted failure is **not necessarily an exception or corrupt byte sequence**. It is the unjustified assertion that the *scope of what has been determined* coincides with the *scope of what the procedure means to determine*. Better transport reliability does not repair that confusion.

## 4. Theorem TR-3 — qualified transfer of A with A′

The required cross-ecosystem object is **not** the closed result set and **not** the unqualified run history. Transmit the semantic pair \(\mathfrak M_S(A)\), then interpret it **for the receiving ecosystem**.

A receiving ecosystem need not share the source's eligible population, metric, authority, context or available evidence. Let \(W_R\) be its admissible worlds, \(q_R:W_R\to Y\) its **independently fixed recipient question**, and let

\[
\mu:D_\mu\subseteq W_R\longrightarrow W_S
\]

be a **qualified, partial source–receiver interpretation**. For a shared answer type \(Y\), preservation of the relevant question requires:

\[
\forall v\in D_\mu,\qquad
q_R(v)=q_S(\mu(v)).
\tag{7}
\]

If answer types differ, (7) must be replaced by a correspondingly qualified value translation. Mere identical protocol labels or the name “nearest” is not evidence that (7) holds.

Let \(C_S\) be a **sound source evaluator**: it may return a correctly qualified definitive value from Y or INDETERMINATE, but never a false definitive value. Define a receiver interpretation:

\[
C_R(v)=
\begin{cases}
C_S(\mu(v)),
  & v\in D_\mu\text{ and the source/interpretation evidence is valid},\\
\mathrm{INDETERMINATE},&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{8}
\]

**Theorem TR-3 (soundness of qualified semantic transfer).** Under the source soundness assumption and (7), every **definitive** \(C_R\) output is sound for \(q_R\). Cases without a qualified correspondence remain open rather than being converted into fictitious definitive answers.

**Proof.** If \(C_R(v)=b\in Y\), then by (8) the qualified branch applies, and \(b=C_S(\mu(v))=q_S(\mu(v))=q_R(v)\). Otherwise the evaluator issues no definitive semantic claim. \(\square\)

The **semantic meaning is transferred without *false closure***, not as a promise that every substantive question becomes decidable in the receiver. The latter is exactly what L1/OW-1 forbids for the unrestricted expressive class.

It is also wrong to transplant source A′ as a literal unchanging bag of unknown objects. Instead:

\[
\boxed{
(A_{\mathrm{proc}},A'_S)
\ \xrightarrow[\text{qualified }\mu]{\text{semantic transfer}}\
(A_{\mathrm{proc},R},A'_R)
}
\tag{9}
\]

where \(A'_R\) is **requalified** for the receiving ecosystem's own scope, capabilities, agent population and unanswered target obligations. What can travel is the **semantic commitment and its open frontier**, not a completed inventory of all future unknowns.

The receiver can understand the source's declared intended question even when no \(\mu\) is available; what it cannot do without (7) is claim that it has **correctly applied or decided** that question in its different ecosystem.

## 5. The example: a nearest observed neighbor versus *the* nearest neighbor

Let \(S_r\) be the finite set of source-observed qualified neighbor candidates in execution r. The source can correctly return:

\[
\widehat x_r\in
\operatorname*{argmin}_{x\in S_r\cap\operatorname{Eligible}_S}
d_S(a,x).
\tag{10}
\]

The output is **nearest within the observed eligible population**. It does not establish the concept **the nearest eligible neighbor in the whole relevant open ecosystem**, whose predicate is:

\[
\operatorname{Nearest}_S(x)
\iff \operatorname{Eligible}_S(x)\land
\forall y\in\Omega_S^{\mathrm{relevant}}
\bigl(\operatorname{Eligible}_S(y)\Rightarrow
d_S(a,x)\le d_S(a,y)\bigr).
\tag{11}
\]

The **universal comparison** in (11) is not exhausted by finite run records under the open encounter condition. Two worlds may have identical observed nearest-neighbor results while one has a closer eligible but unobserved neighbor. Thus the source output can be locally correct while an unqualified globally nearest claim is wrong.

**A** specifies **which question is being pursued, with which metric and eligibility semantics, using which procedure and budget**. **A′** records that the **universal comparison required by the target has not been exhausted**, independently of how many successfully observed nearest neighbors were reported. Their combination is precisely what gives the receiver an interpretable distinction between an observed witness and the **general meaning** being pursued.

The receiving ecosystem \(\mathcal E_R\) may have different notions of distance, eligibility, time and authority. It must interpret those semantics and requalify the open frontier; merely transferring coordinates and a ranked list does not prove the same global nearest-neighbor relation in the recipient.

## 6. Why A′ matters for generalization and abstraction

A trace explains **what happened in a finite execution**. A procedure with a declared question explains **what operation the agent intends to perform**. The open frontier A′ explains **why the delivered history is not a certificate that the intended question has been exhausted**.

The semantically consequential information in A′ is **not a computable list of every unknown**. It is the **generalization obligation left open** by the procedure's target, particularly when the target quantifies over agents or possible contexts not yet known to the source. This is why **A′ matters when moving to a higher abstraction layer or to another ecosystem**: it prevents a legitimate observed A-result from silently becoming a global truth claim.

This establishes a precise form of **construction–meaning asymmetry**:

\[
\boxed{
\text{finite constructed output}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{globally sufficient semantic representation}
}
\tag{12}
\]

and a positive, but **conditional**, counterpart:

\[
\boxed{
\text{procedural target A + open frontier A′ + qualified correspondence}
\Rightarrow
\text{sound qualified transfer of the intended task}.
}
\tag{13}
\]

The first follows from TR-1 when (4) holds; the second is TR-3. Neither implies that semantic content is *physically created by unknown objects*, that an actual city contains infinitely many unobserved peers, or that any transfer architecture outperforms an existing competent method. The distinction between **description of meaning** and **successful determination of all instances** is an essential part of the mathematics, not a retreat from L1.

### 6.1. A machine cannot compute the complete A′, but can witness its boundary during A

Fix the procedure/semantic task \(A_{\mathrm{proc}}=(\Pi,q,\rho,\sigma)\) defined in §1. Running it yields **finite outputs and a finite execution/evidence record**. It can produce a verified match, a justified refusal, a timeout, a contradictory report, a damaged input, or an explicit INDETERMINATE. These are **run-level products of A**. A machine that executes A does not thereby enumerate, decide or reach the whole unclosed \(A'_S\).

Nevertheless, an execution may encounter a **finite witnessed manifestation of its open boundary**. The machine can recognize, for example, that a particular received authorization record cannot justify a definitive answer, or that the comparison with unseen eligible neighbors remains open. Calling this observation a **qualified indicator of A′** does not mean the machine has evaluated all of A′.

Define \(Z_{S,r}\) as a **finite qualified boundary record** for run r. It can include *observed unresolved questions, explicit limits of evidence, known B/C/D-related claims, undecided role assignments, and open-coverage or non-closure qualifiers*. In particular:

\[
Z_{S,r}\text{ is finite and observable},\qquad
Z_{S,r}\ne A'_S.
\tag{14}
\]

Even \(Z_{S,r}=\varnothing\) does not imply \(A'_S=\varnothing\). This distinction is essential: **A′ supplies the open semantic scope; \(Z_{S,r}\) supplies only the boundary evidence actually available in a run.**

The machine's ability to state an indetermination is not an ability to determine the hidden substantive truth. An unanswered request can be *recognized as unanswered*, while its eventual success remains indeterminate.

### 6.2. Two ways to transmit one execution: loss by erasure

Compare two **deliberately defined signalling policies** over identical source runs and the same source question:

\[
X(w)=
\bigl(A_{\mathrm{proc}},M_{S,r}(w)\bigr)
\tag{15}
\]

and

\[
Y(w)=
\bigl(A_{\mathrm{proc}},M_{S,r}(w),
        Z_{S,r}(w),\mathrm{OPEN}(A'_S)\bigr).
\tag{16}
\]

Here X is the **reduced A-only representation**: it keeps the procedure and established results but **drops the observed residual qualifiers**. Y is the **A/A′-aware representation**: it keeps X and additionally preserves the observed boundary record and the open semantic obligation. The symbol \(\mathrm{OPEN}(A'_S)\) is a **finite truthful non-closure declaration**, **not an encoding of every indeterminate element**.

There is a simple, computable **forgetting map**

\[
g:Y\longrightarrow X,\qquad
g(A,M,Z,\mathrm{OPEN})=(A,M).
\tag{17}
\]

Consequently \(X=g\circ Y\). This is the exact limited sense in which a source can perform an **information-losing projection** by exporting X after discarding source-qualified indeterminate signals. This use of *projection* means a forgetting/representation map; it does not assert every theorem in the separate 2025/2026 *Projection Mechanism* papers.

Not every system described colloquially as “A-only” will actually erase the boundary. The theorem compares the **specified erasing policy X** with the **retaining policy Y**. If an existing sender already preserves Z, its signal is not this X baseline, whatever its product branding.

### 6.3. Theorem TR-4 — weak informational dominance of A/A′-aware transfer

Let \(W\) be any fixed class of source-world/run situations, and let \(X:W\to\mathcal X\), \(Y:W\to\mathcal Y\) be the reduced and extended messages (15)–(16). Fix any legitimate **receiving decision task** \(t:W\to\mathcal T\), a common available action set \(\mathcal A\), and any task-specific loss \(\ell(a,t(w))\). Compare the **best attainable receiver decisions** under the **same feasible actions and decision-processing assumptions**, before charging any additional acquisition, communication or deployment cost for Y.

**Theorem TR-4 (weak informational dominance).** If \(X=g\circ Y\), then for any receiver policy \(d_X:\mathcal X\to\mathcal A\) there exists a richer-message policy \(d_Y:\mathcal Y\to\mathcal A\) yielding exactly the same actions and losses in every admissible world. Thus the optimal attainable expected or worst-case loss with Y cannot exceed that with X:

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{d_Y}\mathbb E[\ell(d_Y(Y(W)),t(W))]
\ \le\
\inf_{d_X}\mathbb E[\ell(d_X(X(W)),t(W))]
}
\tag{18}
\]

and, likewise, for the corresponding infimum worst-case risk whenever those quantities are defined.

**Proof.** For any \(d_X\), set \(d_Y=d_X\circ g\). By (17), \(d_Y(Y(w))=d_X(X(w))\) **for every w**. Hence every policy achievable using X is also achievable using Y, by ignoring its extra fields. The optimal loss under Y is weakly no larger. \(\square\)

This is a simple **deterministic garbling / information-refinement** instance of the established Blackwell comparison of experiments: a richer observation can reproduce the poorer one by deletion. The elementary proof above is sufficient for this case; no claim of discovering a new statistical theorem is made. See David Blackwell, *Equivalent Comparisons of Experiments*, *Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 24(2), 1953, 265–272, DOI [10.1214/aoms/1177729032](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177729032).

**Meaning of weak.** Equality is allowed. If the supplementary boundary signal is irrelevant to the receiving task, or the receiver already knows it, the receiver may gain **nothing**. The theorem establishes **greater or equal attainable decision information**, not that every actual enhanced algorithm automatically makes better choices, spends fewer bytes, reduces latency or enjoys lower net operating cost. These latter claims require a matched resource/communication model and measurements.

**Strict informational gain requires a witness.** If two admissible situations have \(X(w_0)=X(w_1)\) but different qualified additional signals \(Y(w_0)\ne Y(w_1)\), Y preserves a distinction lost by X. Strict *decision* improvement occurs only for a task that **depends on that distinction** and for which Y supports a better receiver action.

### 6.4. Proposition TR-5 — a finite strict-gain witness without deciding A′

Consider two source worlds with the **same procedure A** and the **same two confirmed positive receipts**. In \(w_0\), the finite encountered peer population consists exactly of those two witnessed cases. In \(w_1\), the source encountered an **additional third peer with an unresolved authorization question**, but the X channel discards the third peer's unresolved status. Both source ecosystems still have **globally OPEN population/semantic coverage**.

The reduced payload is identical:

\[
X(w_0)=X(w_1)=(A,\{\mathrm{receipt}_1,\mathrm{receipt}_2\}).
\tag{19}
\]

The extended payload preserves the distinction:

\[
Y(w_0)=(X,\mathrm{observed\ unresolved}=0,\mathrm{OPEN}),\quad
Y(w_1)=(X,\mathrm{observed\ unresolved}=1,\mathrm{OPEN}).
\tag{20}
\]

Define the **independently specified, run-scoped receiving task** \(t(w)\): *did the source finish this particular run with no substantively unresolved encountered peer?* Its answer is YES in \(w_0\) and NO in \(w_1\). For a uniform two-world prior, 0–1 loss and binary receiver actions, any X-only decision must give the **same answer** in both worlds, with a minimum expected error of \(1/2\). Y permits **zero error for this run-scoped meta-question**, because the source provided the trustworthy qualified indicator.

\[
\boxed{
R_X^*(t)=1/2,\qquad R_Y^*(t)=0 .
}
\tag{21}
\]

This is an **illustrative finite construction**, not a field measurement. Y does **not** determine whether the third peer's authorization was truly valid, whether it will eventually complete, or what all unseen peers will do. It improves the ability to **recognize that a currently known decision remains unresolved** and therefore prevents certain false local closures. This is the precise **strict differential witness** compatible with weak informational dominance.

The extra reported information may be tiny (a verified unresolved-count/indicator, subject identity and scope), while the raw execution archive could be large. The construction does not assert a universal compression ratio; it shows **task-relevant information preservation**, not merely higher byte volume.

### 6.5. B/C/D as partial abstractions of A′; B′/C′/D′ as non-closed role frontiers

The open A′ is **not exhaustively divisible into three fully decided subsets**. The current Structural Awareness [00M §1](../ecosystem-awareness/baseline/00M_ABCD_AND_MATHEMATICAL_PLAUSIBILITY_v0.8_RESEARCH_NOTE.md#abcd-canonical) supplies a **useful process-relative qualification vocabulary**, with no claim to exhaustively partition a universe.

For any positioning profile \(p=(\mathrm{actor},\mathrm{question},\mathrm{scope},\mathrm{capability},\mathrm{time})\), qualifying an **observed, suitably defined aspect x** may support one or more declarations concerning:

- **B:** characterized evaluation basis and a defensibly assessable reserve beyond the currently delivered functional result;
- **C:** grounded exploration opportunity for which that evaluation basis is not characterized;
- **D:** potentially material condition beyond this process's effective route to evaluation for its particular relevant effect;
- **UNKNOWN / UNQUALIFIED:** even the scope, evidential basis or role assignment is not sufficiently established.

These roles apply **to aspects and functional claims**, not by magically sorting every physical agent into one and only one category. A program may correctly observe and report **one B reserve**, another C lead, or a witnessed D-type evaluation barrier **within a finite run**, yet that says nothing about having listed every possible B, C or D aspect of the open ecosystem.

For each role \(j\in\{B,C,D\}\), write \(\kappa_{j,p}\) for the **partial, evidence-qualified role-assertion function**, defined only on the aspects for which the predicate, scope and proof basis are established. The local **primed notation**

\[
B'_p,\qquad C'_p,\qquad D'_p
\tag{22}
\]

denotes the **unclosed applicability and exhaustion obligation of those partial role characterizations**, respectively. The primes are **meta-level boundary qualifiers**, not newly defined canonical epistemic categories, literal complements \(\Omega\setminus B\), or collections that can be enumerated by the executing agent. They do **not** assert that a particular unseen B-, C- or D-item has been found or that every named role's extensional class is necessarily nonempty.

**Lemma TR-6 (no total role inventory from an open prefix).** Fix any role \(j\in\{B,C,D\}\). If admissible source worlds allow the **same finite role-evidence history** with and without an additional unseen relevant aspect qualifying for j, then no function of that history alone can correctly certify an exhaustive global inventory of j in both worlds.

**Proof.** Both histories are identical, but the proposition “the known j-role claims exhaust all relevant j-role aspects” has different truth values. This is OW-7's indistinguishability proof applied to j-qualified claims. \(\square\)

This is **not** a theorem saying that every B/C boundary is intrinsically undecidable: specific B/C/D assignments may be clear and correctly justified. Rather, **making the role map total and exhaustive over an open domain is an extra global decision/coverage demand**. Under a sufficiently expressive role-decision problem that encodes OW-1's target, a claimed sound total boundary oracle would contradict OW-1; that expressivity connection must be stated for the role-decision task. A program should never be forced to place **all** unqualified open material into B, C or D, or to treat an insufficiently established role as D by default.

**Operational interpretation.** B is a witnessed **reserve of exploitation that can be characterized**, not a certificate of the largest reserve that will ever exist. B′ is the unclosed boundary of **what further reserves could become characterized**. C′ and D′ play the corresponding role for their respective evaluability frontiers. The useful current B/C/D assertions can be transferred as **finite, qualified representatives of A′-related epistemic structure**, while B′/C′/D′ preserve their non-exhaustiveness.

A/B/C/D remains the **owner-adopted vocabulary of 00M**; this source's A/A′/B′/C′/D′ notation is a **candidate mathematical lens**, not an amendment of that normative semantic definition.

### 6.6. What the weak dominance result does—and does not—establish

Y can preserve qualifying evidence of encountered indeterminates that X deliberately erases; an independent downstream task may strictly benefit, as TR-5 demonstrates.

**Not established by the weak theorem:** a total program for A′; an effective enumeration of all B′/C′/D′; an exact role partition; automatic semantic compatibility between two unrelated ecosystems; a universal lower bitrate; smaller latency; inevitable improvement of every recipient action; or net cost superiority. Transmission costs, access restrictions, interpretation rights and finite processing budgets may change the engineering comparison.

**Core proposition:** an A/A′-aware agent may perform no more computation on the unknown world than an A-only agent, but it can communicate **more of what was legitimately learned—including the limits of what was learned—without compressing an open decision into an unjustified closed result**. This is the **weak informational superiority** that precedes any empirical claim of superior performance.

### 6.8. Theorem TR-7 — informational equivalence and implementation-neutral isomorphism

Fix an admissible world/task class \(W\) and two source-to-receiver information interfaces \(Y:W\to\mathcal Y\) and \(Z:W\to\mathcal Z\). Introduce the deterministic informativeness preorder

\[
Z\preceq Y
\ \Longleftrightarrow\
\exists g:\ \forall w\in W,\ Z(w)=g(Y(w)).
\tag{23}
\]

Thus \(Z\preceq Y\) means Y can **reproduce** Z without accessing further world information; it does **not** say Y has better latency or costs.

Define **informational equivalence** on W by

\[
Y\simeq_{\rm info} Z
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
Z\preceq Y\ \land\ Y\preceq Z.
\tag{24}
\]

**Theorem TR-7 (equivalence of distinctions).** For deterministic interfaces on a common domain W, the following are equivalent:

(a) \(Y\simeq_{\rm info} Z\);

(b) Y and Z induce exactly the same equivalence relation on world states,

\[
Y(w_1)=Y(w_2)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
Z(w_1)=Z(w_2)
\quad(w_1,w_2\in W).
\tag{25}
\]

**Proof.** If each signal is a function of the other, equal values of either imply equal values of the other, so the fibers agree. Conversely if the fibers agree, define \(g(Y(w))=Z(w)\) on the image of Y; equality of Y outputs ensures a well-defined Z output. Similarly define the reverse decoding function on the image of Z. \(\square\)

This is an **isomorphism of information carried on the fixed admissible world class**, not an assertion of computational equivalence or equal acquisition/decoding cost unless the decoders are also effective under the declared resource envelope. We can call two carriers **effectively information-isomorphic for a profile \(\rho\)** only when both decoders are computable, legitimately accessible and resource-feasible under that same profile, while preserving the receiving semantic target. This operational strengthening makes implementation equivalence testable. For randomized channels, the corresponding concept uses stochastic Blackwell garbling and requires different hypotheses.

**Consequence.** An implementation that already exports the procedure, run evidence, recognized uncertainty **and the open-frontier meaning** may satisfy (24) with our A/A′ carrier and be just as strong for the declared task. **Such an implementation is not in the strictly closure-only class defined in §6.12.** Its equivalence with our representation therefore does not defeat the strict semantic expressivity result TR-10 against that closed class.

TR-4 proves weak decision-information dominance over a specified **erasure baseline** \(X=g(Y)\). TR-10 additionally proves **strict semantic expressivity** against the expressly closure-only interface. TR-7 describes equivalence among carriers that actually preserve the same information, which are **not** the closure-only comparator when they communicate A′.

### 6.9. Maximality, the A′ tag and what incompleteness actually establishes

A true theorem about the **global impossibility of an infallible semantic classifier** is **not** automatically a theorem that a particular information representation is maximal. The distinction can be proved rather than just asserted.

**A′ as a constant structural fact.** If H-EXP and the unchanged global target are common knowledge, the simple flag “this task's open-universe semantic frontier exists” may have the **same value in every admissible world**. Appending a perfectly recoverable constant to an already qualified message cannot discriminate any additional pair of those worlds. In the terminology of (23)–(25), the bare constant OPEN flag may be informationally equivalent to the prior message **about the world variable W**, although it communicates an essential statement of the **task's interpretation/validity conditions** to recipients that had not been given that mathematical premise. A free decoder that can already derive the flag from the transmitted procedure and shared theory defeats any claim that the flag alone gives *strict* informational gain.

**Observed boundary evidence is different.** A finite \(Z_{S,r}\) that records whether a previously unreported actual interaction became indeterminate can distinguish worlds that were identical under the reduced message X. This is exactly why TR-5 exhibits strict value for its **independent run-scoped receiver question**. The general A′ obligation and particular *observed* manifestations of that obligation should not be confused.

**Proposition TR-8 (no globally maximal effective sound semantic resolver).** Under H-EXP, let \(C:\mathbb N\to\{0,1,\bot\}\) be any **total computable, sound** classifier for \(K_0\), where \(\bot\) means INDETERMINATE. In classical mathematics, there exists another total computable sound classifier \(C^+\) that agrees with every definitive verdict of C and has a **strict superset of definitive indices**, adding at least one correctly answered case without retracting any earlier definitive answer.

**Proof.** By OW-1, C outputs \(\bot\) at some fixed index \(e_\star\). The target truth value \(b=1_{K_0}(e_\star)\) is either 0 or 1. Define

\[
C^+(e)=
\begin{cases}
b,&e=e_\star,\\
C(e),&e\ne e_\star.
\end{cases}
\tag{26}
\]

A program can compare e with this fixed index and hard-code the fixed b, so \(C^+\) is computable, total and sound; it strictly refines C's definitive outputs at \(e_\star\). The proof is **existential**: it does not give a uniform effective procedure for finding \(e_\star\) and b from arbitrary C. \(\square\)

This proposition formalizes a point consistent with L1: **no particular total sound ordinary classifier closes the global question, yet more individual questions can sometimes be determined consistently.** Thus Gödel/Turing do **not** show that “if one adds any further valid distinction, inconsistency necessarily occurs.” Their relevant contribution is the **impossibility of a universally correct total decider over the expressive class**. Broad claims of an inconsistent universe or maximality of all information need an independent, well-typed theorem.

The useful architectural substitute for unbounded maximality is **non-erasure relative to the actually qualified source information and the declared transfer task**: represent and communicate what the sender knows **and the limits on what those reports justify**. This may be a task-sufficient/faithful reference, but does not imply that no further legitimate source information could ever be added.

**Proposition TR-9 (relative maximality given the same qualified source information).** Fix an independently defined **source-qualified information bundle** \(E:W\to\mathcal E\): the full procedure/target, all finite evidence and **recognized** unresolved qualifiers relevant to the declared transfer profile, with source scope and validity. Consider the class of messages **derivable only from that bundle**, \(Z_h=h\circ E\), with qualified decoding and no extra external observations.

If an A/A′-aware carrier \(Y\) is **faithful for this E**—there exists a legitimate recoverability map \(d\) with \(E=d\circ Y\)—then

\[
\forall Z_h=h\circ E:
\quad Z_h=(h\circ d)\circ Y,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
Z_h\preceq Y .
\tag{27}
\]

**Proof.** Substitute \(E=d\circ Y\) into \(Z_h=h\circ E\). \(\square\)

Thus such a Y is **maximal relative to messages obtainable from the same declared, qualified source information E**, under the **information-refinement preorder** and ignoring any extra processing/communication cost. A conventional carrier \(Z\) that also preserves the entire E with an effective decoder can tie; it is effectively equivalent when Y and Z are both functions of E and each reconstructs E. This is an implementation-neutral **faithfulness criterion for reference architectures**, not an assertion that the current A/A′ syntax is automatically a lossless encoder of every possible future fact.

**The qualification is essential.** If Y actually transmits only the procedure A and a constant OPEN tag while the source possesses additional relevant observed boundary evidence, Y does **not** satisfy the recoverability premise for E and cannot claim this relative maximality. The finite-source evidence must genuinely be carried (or be reconstructible under valid source access) for the theorem to apply. An independent new sensor, future peer, richer authorized source or previously inaccessible relevant condition is **outside** the fixed E and may properly provide more information without inconsistency. Therefore relative maximality of a faithful carrier and **non-maximality of a global semantic decider** (TR-8) are entirely compatible.

### 6.10. From weak to strict decision improvement — and onward to the PR #60 Stage-A problem

**Weak, already proved.** For a given reduced message X and an enriched message Y satisfying \(X=g(Y)\), every X-measurable decision can be reproduced using Y. Under the same feasible receiver actions and with uncharged information overhead, the best attainable expected loss with Y is no greater than with X (TR-4).

**Strict for an independently fixed receiving task, already witnessed.** TR-5 constructs worlds with **identical X**, but distinct Y due to an actual unresolved encounter that X discards. Under equal prior and 0–1 loss for the recipient's question “did the run encounter an unresolved case?”, the optimum risks are \(1/2\) for X and 0 for Y. This proves **strict informational decision value for that specified task**, not global or universal superiority over every implementation.

**A different stronger claim remains to establish.** For a *new mission or action*, one wants eligible supplemental observations that distinguish states previously conflated by the existing A-information, and that alter the recipient's **legitimate optimal decision**. The open [PR #60 formal core](https://github.com/dakleyer/structural-awareness-contributions/pull/60) studies: F1 (fiber sufficiency), D1 (operational congruence under a changed operation), D2 (finite refinement), D3 (mission-relevant additional queries), and D7 (costless optional information weakly reduces attainable loss). It explicitly classifies **strict practical advantage after costs** as **a hypothesis**, not an already established result.

For a correct *incremental* question-selection problem, existing A cannot be discarded. Write \(o:W\to O\) for the receiver's **already available qualified observation**, \(Q_j\) for eligible supplementary questions and \(r:W\to R\) for the independently defined target. On a finite declared evaluation class \(\widehat W\), only **conflicting pairs left indistinguishable by the existing A** require new supplementary separation:

\[
U_{r\mid o}
=\bigl\{\{w,w'\}\subseteq\widehat W:
o(w)=o(w'),\ r(w)\ne r(w')\bigr\}.
\tag{27}
\]

A sufficient selected query family H for an exact target r must separate those pairs; **including already available A** is essential. This is a bounded instance of the same factorization criterion, not a mechanism for enumerating A′ or a universal oracle.

The [open re-audit PR #59](https://github.com/dakleyer/structural-awareness-contributions/pull/59) identifies precisely this defect in PR #60's application of D3: its finite **Q-alone** pair-cover theorem is valid, but using that construction directly to choose *supplementary* observations may ignore pairs already separated by existing A, falsely declaring the task unattainable or overestimating needed Q. The D3 algebra in its original scope and the corrected integrated A+Q selection must be **distinguished**; this note does not silently alter the still-open PR.

**Comparator distinction:** the strict closure-only K-then-D class is now defined by a substantive *semantic restriction* (no outside-A claim type), not by deliberately breaking its implementation or withholding correct in-A exception handling. TR-10 gives the **strict representational advantage** over that class. For a **separate operational advantage** claim, comparisons must still fix the same decision task, resource budget, permission set, evidence and action options. An architecture that already expresses A′ may tie on decisions and cost, but **it does not belong to the stipulated closure-only comparator class**. The empirical question is whether the semantically richer representation leads to better, cheaper or more resilient actions under those declared conditions.

### 6.11. Exact four-state bridge to PR #60 and a strict-gain condition

We can pin down the stronger **strict, task-dependent** result directly from the four-state countermodel in the [PR #60 formal core, D1](https://github.com/dakleyer/structural-awareness-contributions/pull/60), instead of leaving the phrase “strong superiority” undefined.

Define \(W=\{0,1,2,3\}\), the original observation \(o(x)=x\bmod2\), and an old eligible transition \(s(x)=x+1\bmod4\). The old observation remains adequate for s: \(o(s(x))=1-o(x)\). Now add the permitted operation f:

| x | o(x) | f(x) | New target \(r(x)=o(f(x))\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 3 | 1 | 3 | 1 |

The original A-like observable cannot distinguish 0 from 2, yet the independently specified **new operation's answer** differs:

\[
o(0)=o(2)=0,\qquad r(0)=1\ne0=r(2).
\tag{28}
\]

Thus r does not factor through o; the original interface is insufficient for the **new** task, although it was correct for the old one. Let one permitted supplemental observation be \(Q(x)=\mathbf1_{\{x=2\}}\), so the enriched carrier \(Y(x)=(o(x),Q(x))\) distinguishes that conflicting pair.

For the explicitly selected **two-world receiving decision problem** \(W^\star=\{0,2\}\), a uniform prior, binary prediction actions and zero-one error loss:

\[
\boxed{
R^*_{o}=1/2,\qquad R^*_{(o,Q)}=0.
}
\tag{29}
\]

**Proof.** Both states produce the same o-observation, forcing any deterministic o-only prediction to be identical: it errs on one of two equiprobable worlds, so minimum risk is \(1/2\). Q separates the worlds, permitting the correct r-prediction in each, hence zero error. \(\square\)

This is strict **gross** decision value for the specified task and observation menu. It does **not** follow from the bare A′ tag. The signal \(Y_0(x)=(o(x),\mathrm{OPEN})\) retains the **same fiber** as o when OPEN is constant on \(W^\star\), so \(R^*_{Y_0}=R^*_{o}=1/2\) for this world-prediction task. A′ tells the receiver **not to promote an insufficient o-output to a globally adequate new-task answer**, whereas Q supplies the **additional witnessed distinction needed to predict**. These are different forms of value.

A possible extra observation/qualification cost \(c\) changes the net comparison. In this synthetic 0–1-loss accounting, \(Q\) wins if \(c<1/2\), ties if \(c=1/2\), and loses if \(c>1/2\); other permissions, action feasibility and costs have been fixed away only for this example. If another **competent receiver already has Q**, then its \(R^*=0\) ties the enriched A/A′-aware carrier **on this finite in-A task**. That task-level tie does not show a closure-only interface can represent the outside-A frontier.

**Incremental correction from PR #59.** We should only purchase Q to distinguish *different-r* pairs that **o has not already separated**, as defined by \(U_{r\mid o}\) in §6.10. Testing Q alone against all conflicting pairs can declare a needed observation unavailable or increase its calculated cost even when the original o already separates them. The bounded PR's D3 theorem for Q-alone remains valid **in its own statement**; its supplementary-query application needs this integrated correction.

**Executable bounded witness:** [four-state strict-value and strong-comparator fixture](./fixtures/A_APRIME_STRICT_VALUE_FOUR_STATE_v0.1.mjs) (16 finite assertions) checks the original/expanded signatures, o-only and (o,Q) exact risks, the inability of a constant A′ flag to substitute for Q, cost win/tie/loss, and a competent conventional Q-aware tie. These are symbolic finite test cases, not evidence of deployment or a universal theorem about richer ecosystems. To rerun from the repository root:

~~~sh
node research/structural-awareness/fixtures/A_APRIME_STRICT_VALUE_FOUR_STATE_v0.1.mjs
~~~

**What remains to be called strong superiority?** TR-5 and this example exhibit *strict usefulness for a declared receiving question versus a specified lossy information state*. They do **not** establish a universal advantage of one named architecture. A **strong operational improvement claim** requires a positive mission-specific net gain against the **best competent equally authorized comparator**, at matched resources and independently assessed decision consequences. That last step is the **unproven Stage-A empirical/differential agenda** of PR #60/#57, not a theorem supplied by L1, Gödel or TR-4.

### 6.12. Two-stage closure-only comparator

Define the specific conventional comparator as two effective stages: a closure stage K constructs an operational frame A; a subsequent operator D executes only within A and produces A₁.

\[
K(\mathcal E)=A,\qquad D(A)=A_1\subseteq A,
\qquad P=D\circ K.
\tag{30}
\]

Composing K and D into one program does not remove its computability limits (OW-5). The open-aware construction uses **the same K, D, A and A₁**. Its advantage is not a hidden improvement in executing D.

**Strict comparator definition:** the closure-only exported interface is typed solely over A. It has no language for stating anything **about a semantic frontier outside A**. It may correctly preserve all in-A error and INDETERMINATE records. It is nonetheless prohibited, by its own interface contract, from representing the open semantic residual A′.

The open-aware interface preserves exactly the same A and A₁ plus a **true meta-semantic statement about A′**. Here A is the closing stage's represented frame; it should not be confused with §1's \(A_{\rm proc}\), the entire declared procedure and task, or canonical 00M A, a delivered functional result. A′ is an **open semantic obligation**, not an enumerable inventory of unidentified physical entities.

\[
M_{\rm closed}=(A,A_1,\mathrm{qualified\ in\!-\!A\ records}),
\]

\[
M_{\rm open}=(M_{\rm closed},
\mathrm{OPEN}(A'),\mathrm{unclosed\ meaning}).
\tag{31}
\]

An implementation that already carries equivalent open-frontier statements, regardless of its branding, is **not in this strictly closure-only architectural class**. The classification is by contract, not by software age or vendor.

### 6.13. Theorem TR-10 — strict claim expressivity

Let \(\mathcal L_C\) be the closed interface language, where semantic assertions and quantifiers are restricted to A and no meta-level frontier sort exists. Let \(\mathcal L_O\) extend this interface by a correctly interpreted external-frontier declaration. Fix the expressive uniform semantic question from OW-1, under H-EXP, and a sound total evaluator with an INDETERMINATE outcome. OW-1 implies **a nonempty unresolved semantic frontier** for that evaluator. This does not mean that every real city has a further unknown physical neighbor.

**TR-10.** Under those definitions,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Claims}(\mathcal L_C)
\subsetneq \operatorname{Claims}(\mathcal L_O).}
\tag{32}
\]

**Proof.** Every in-A statement expressible by the closed interface remains expressible by its extension. OW-1 guarantees that the declaration “there exists an unresolved semantic frontier relative to this uniform procedure” is true in the given problem. The open language can state it; the closed language, by its stipulated typing rules, cannot express a proposition about an outside-A semantic referent. Hence strict inclusion. \(\square\)

The proof combines **nonemptiness supplied by OW-1** with the **representational restriction defining the comparator**. It is a theorem of **strict semantic expressivity for this architectural comparison**; it is not a universal claim about any implementation called conventional.

### 6.14. TR-11 — existence without identifying unknown elements

Two admissible situations may have the same A, the same A₁ and the same truthful frontier statement while differing in the identities or substantive answers of their unrepresented constituents. Therefore that frontier statement cannot recover the individual identities or truth values. It can **refer to the open exterior without identifying its members**. That is its intended purpose, and it does not turn the machine into an oracle.

### 6.15. Relationship to the weak and strong information claims

Forgetting the additional frontier fields recovers the closure-only message:

\[
M_{\rm closed}=\operatorname{Forget}_{A'}(M_{\rm open}).
\tag{33}
\]

Thus TR-4 still gives **weak decision-information dominance**. TR-10 adds **strict representational/semantic scope**: a class of true claim is expressible by the open interface and inexpressible by the definitionally closed interface.

These are not identical orders of information. If the assertion that A′ exists is constant throughout a comparison class and the receiver already knows the same theorem, appending that bare flag does **not necessarily distinguish more possible worlds** in the Blackwell/Shannon sense. Yet it enables the closed sender to make a true *meta-semantic* claim its own interface previously prohibited. Strict receiver-decision gain follows when the enhanced message also retains **world-varying, qualified observations** erased by the closed one (TR-5).

A different architecture that can express A′ may be information-isomorphic to the open reference (TR-7), but **it is no longer the strictly closed comparator**. No claim of identified unknown entities, free implementation benefit or globally complete A′ enumeration is required.

### 6.16. One composed computation, two architectures of representation

The contrast has **no privileged execution engine**. Let \(\mathcal E\) be the declared open source ecosystem, let K be any effective admission/scope-forming routine, D an effective downstream task routine, and \(\mathcal P=\operatorname{Compose}(K,D,V_1,\ldots,V_m)\) their **entire computable orchestration**, with optional computable verifiers \(V_j\). It makes no difference to computability whether the implementation draws two boxes, a chain, or one consolidated program.

For a chosen run r, write \(h_r=\operatorname{Trace}(\mathcal P,\mathcal E,r)\) and \(A_r=\operatorname{QualifiedObserved}(h_r)\) for **the finite, correctly qualified observations and findings from the complete composed procedure**. The scope K has admitted may be denoted \(A_{\mathrm{scope}}\); the operative result selected within that scope remains \(A_1\subseteq A_{\mathrm{scope}}\) in the selector case of §6.12. The symbols should not be silently interchanged:

| Object | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| \(\mathcal P\) | The entire effective **procedure** (K, D and any additional algorithms) |
| \(A_{\mathrm{scope}}\) | The **working universe admitted** by K for a specific operation; not a certificate of global coverage |
| \(A_1\) | A particular D output within that working scope (when D is a selecting operator) |
| \(A_r\) | The **complete finite record of qualified findings** from all stages of a concrete run r |
| \(I_r\) | The observed cases **within the run/scope** for which the substantive question remains unqualified; not necessarily all intrinsically undecidable inputs |
| \(A'_r\) | **The residual open semantic reach** of \(\mathcal P\) and its declared task beyond what \(A_r\) has established; not an identified/enumerated outside population |

The **conventional closure-first architecture** uses K to form a working scope and D to act in it, then treats that scope as **its entire representable semantic frame**. It may recognize all errors and unknowns observed within that frame; no claim of deliberately defective error handling is needed.

The **open-ecosystem architecture** subjects **the entire \(\mathcal P\)**, including K itself, to the *same* open-world epistemic status. It exports the finite qualified result and a separate open residual obligation; it does **not** use successful completion of K as a certificate that the world's boundary has been settled. K may still serve as a useful bounded subroutine, but **not as a global truth-making prerequisite**.

Write the open report as a **product plus a qualification**, rather than a fictitious binary partition of the universe:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_r^{\mathrm{open}}
=
\bigl(\mathcal P,\ A_r,\
\operatorname{OpenFrontier}(q,\mathcal P,h_r,\sigma,\rho)\bigr).
}
\tag{34}
\]

The third component is a **semantic obligation/qualification**, **not a second closed result set**, and it is not required to enumerate the members of \(A'_r\). The finite first product may contain arbitrarily many recognized output kinds, diagnostics, and encountered unresolved cases. The open boundary is represented whether or not that run happened to observe a specific indeterminate.

### 6.17. TR-12 — the closure stage's meta-oracle trap

There are **three different notions of separation**, and only conflating them makes the apparent paradox:

**(i) Reported outcome of a total bounded run.** If a particular total computable wrapper emits SUCCESS/FAILURE/UNKNOWN and terminates, a machine can run it and determine **which outcome it reported**. Classifying its own *recorded output tag* is not an oracle and does not establish the hidden semantic truth.

**(ii) Uniformly correct determination of arbitrary peer semantics.** Under H-EXP (OW-1), effectively compile a peer \(P_e\) that acknowledges the handshake and sends a completion receipt exactly if \(U_e(e)\) halts. A closure classifier \(G(e)\in\{\mathrm{YES},\mathrm{NO}\}\) that always terminates and returns the **true eventual-completion verdict** for **every** peer would compute

\[
G(e)=\mathrm{YES}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ U_e(e)\downarrow,
\tag{35}
\]

which is a decider of the diagonal halting set and is impossible.

**(iii) Uniformly correct meta-classification of which arbitrary programs have an entirely decidable response domain.** This different question is **also** undecidable on the explicitly multi-request expressive family H-REQ of OW-5b/R07-P2. An exact total checker for that program-index property would decide ordinary halting. The H-REQ premise is necessary: H-EXP alone addresses the fixed request in (ii), not decidability of every request domain.

**Theorem TR-12 (no universal semantic closure gate).** Under H-EXP, no effective closure stage K, isolated or effectively composed with D and any finite/adaptive computable verifier chain, can provide the complete error-free **semantic YES/NO separator** of (ii) over **the entire H-EXP family of admissible peer descriptions**. Under H-REQ, neither can it provide the total correct **decidable-domain meta-separator** of (iii) over **the entire stated H-REQ family**.

**Proof.** For (ii), compose any alleged K/D/verifier separator into a single effective procedure and apply (35), contradicting the halting theorem. For (iii), apply OW-5b's H-REQ program-index reduction: the ability to correctly classify whole-domain decidability from a code would again decide halting. \(\square\)

Consequently, a closure-first architecture has a **dilemma**, not an automatic crash: if K attempts *genuine global semantic closure* for this expressive task, it faces TR-12; if K produces only a bounded/qualified inventory, it has **not globally closed the open world**. D can still make many locally correct decisions inside that inventory. A bounded scanner can also correctly report the run outcome UNKNOWN without violating any theorem.

An important negative control is that “is this particular **total** wrapper's output tag UNKNOWN?” remains decidable by executing it. TR-12 concerns **true semantic determination or program-level meta-decidability**, never a prohibition on recognising a concrete previously emitted UNKNOWN label.

### 6.18. TR-13 — coverage-complete representation without a universe oracle

We can now give **information-representational completeness** an exact meaning that requires no supernatural classifier.

Let \(W\) be the admissible source-world class for the declared question q, and let \(H_r:W\to\mathcal H_r\) be the **finite observation made by the entire composed \(\mathcal P\)** in run r. A given witnessed history h defines, *intensionally*, the worlds compatible with those qualified observations:

\[
\operatorname{Compat}_{\mathcal P}(h)
=
\{w\in W:H_r(w)=h
\ \text{and all independently qualified evidence constraints hold}\}.
\tag{36}
\]

This mathematical definition is **not** an algorithm for enumerating, counting or identifying that class. Define the **open epistemic meaning** of the representation \(\mathcal R_r^{\mathrm{open}}\) to preserve those compatible alternatives rather than silently declaring their outside-of-A populations nonexistent.

**Proposition TR-13 (evidence-relative coverage completeness).** If the reported finite findings are truthful and appropriately qualified, the open report (34), interpreted by (36), does **not exclude an admissible world merely because its further q-relevant agents or semantic distinctions were unobserved**. Its representation can therefore carry all justified finite output **and** the ongoing absence of a global coverage claim, without deciding which compatible world is actual.

**Proof.** The open report asserts the certified observations h and **no** exhaustive inventory fact absent from h. Every w compatible with h and the qualified evidence satisfies those assertions, including any w with undiscovered relevant constituents. No test for membership in the complete \(W\), or list of unknown constituents, is required to publish this *intensional* representation. \(\square\)

An artificially closed export would instead add, without witness, a proposition C claiming “there are no further relevant alternatives beyond the admitted A.” Under H-OPEN/OW-7 there can be two worlds with the same h, one with additional unseen relevant constituents. This C claim excludes the second possible world despite identical evidence. The open representation **preserves** it as a possibility.

**This is completeness of the representation's epistemic coverage of what was and was not established**, **not** completeness of a proof system in Gödel's sense, completeness of substantive answers, or a decision of the real world. A representation may be *correctly complete about its limits* while its substantive uncertainty remains arbitrarily large. Because the entire \(\mathcal P\) is covered, the representation also acknowledges the uncertainty that entered through K itself, not only through D's observed inputs.

### 6.19. TR-14 — weak information dominance inside A and strict claim scope outside A

Define the closure-only payload \(X_r\) as **all qualified in-A information** actually available from the same composed run, including any supported observed UNKNOWN status. Define

\[
Y_r=(X_r,\ \operatorname{OpenFrontier}(q,\mathcal P,h_r)).
\tag{37}
\]

Then the computable forgetting map \(\pi(Y_r)=X_r\) proves

\[
X\preceq Y
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\inf_{d_Y}R(d_Y,Y)\le\inf_{d_X}R(d_X,X)
\tag{38}
\]

for a common declared receiving task, admissible actions and resource-independent optimization assumptions (TR-4).

**Inside A:** the open-aware representation is **weakly at least as informative** because it can retain every true in-A record the closure-only representation has. If the closure-only system **already preserves all observed in-A determinate and indeterminate records**, the informational comparison **inside A alone can be equality**. No strict interior advantage is claimed merely because the conventional K stage lacks a universal semantic oracle; our composed \(\mathcal P\) lacks that oracle too. When a concrete closed interface discards a *known* source uncertainty that the open interface retains, TR-5 shows how a task-specific strict gain may arise.

**Outside A:** by the **strictly closure-only external claim-language definition** of §6.12, the conventional contract cannot even refer to the open semantic frontier. This is **not the same** as claiming to identify an outside-A object or gaining a new independent observation of the world.

Thus the exact result of comparing these two architectures is:

\[
\boxed{
\underbrace{Y|_A\succeq X|_A}_{\text{weak in-A information preservation}}
\quad;\quad
\underbrace{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{closed}}
\subsetneq\mathcal L_{\mathrm{open}}}_{\text{strict outside-A claim expressivity}} .
}
\tag{39}
\]

The statement requires **no superiority of execution**, and it does not claim a **strict Shannon/Blackwell advantage** merely from appending the same globally known OPEN tag. The apparent “error about an error” occurs only if the closure stage additionally claims a total correct semantics/metaclassifier of the impossible TR-12 kind or if a downstream actor promotes an unsupported closure. **Recognizing a bounded exception is allowed and valuable in either architecture.**

### 6.23. Two programs, two output grammars, one object-level execution

Let \(\mathcal P=\operatorname{Compose}(K,D,V_1,\ldots,V_j)\) be the **identical** ordinary execution kernel. Its finite qualified run record is \(h_r\), producing justified in-A results \(a_r\). Define two external report grammars.

**Closed grammar \(L_A\).** Its interpreted types and quantifiers range **only over the K-admitted working frame A**. It can create finite value, status and operation-result expressions, including recorded local UNKNOWN/ERROR cases:

~~~text
Inside ::= Result(value)
         | ObservedStatus(status, evidence)
         | InsideApply(operator, Inside)
         | InsideCombine(Inside, Inside)
~~~

By the definition of this closed interface class, no Inside constructor can **refer to a semantic object or obligation outside A**.

**Extended grammar \(L_{A+}\).** It contains all Inside terms plus new interpreted constructors:

~~~text
Meta ::= Embed(Inside)
       | OpenFrontier(program_ref, question_ref, scope, proof_status)
       | OutsideQuestion(scope_ref, predicate_ref, quantifier)
       | ObservedBoundary(case_ref, evidence)
       | RoleClaim(B_or_C_or_D, case_ref, evidence)
       | UnqualifiedRole(case_ref, reason)
       | RoleFrontier(B_prime_or_C_prime_or_D_prime, scope_ref)
~~~

A **typed, executable reporting transformation** is now possible:

\[
P_C(r)=\operatorname{Serialize}_{L_A}(a_r),\qquad
P_M(r)=\operatorname{Serialize}_{L_{A+}}
\left(\operatorname{Reflect}(\langle\mathcal P\rangle,q,h_r)\right).
\tag{41}
\]

The **object-level instructions and run outputs are the same**, but the reporting programs differ in actual code, instruction trace, accepted output types and messages. The open-aware program is not just a philosophical commentary about \(P_C\). This claim does **not** depend on a new physical instruction set or noncomputable hardware.

The constructor OutsideQuestion builds a **question/formula**, not its truth value. A status NOT_CERTIFIED_CLOSED is a statement about unavailable global certification; a stronger PROVED_UNIFORM_FRONTIER requires the independent matched H-EXP/OW-1 theorem. Neither label identifies individual unknown elements.

### 6.24. Theorem TR-15 — an external question is not definable in the restricted closed language

Let \(w_0,w_1\) be two admissible full-world structures with **the same A-reduct**: identical objects in A, identical values of all interpreted \(L_A\) symbols, and identical observed K/D records. Their outside-A portions differ in a predicate \(\chi\). Both exteriors may be nonempty. In \(w_1\) there is an outside-A object satisfying \(\chi\); in \(w_0\) no outside-A object satisfies it. The extended language expresses:

\[
\psi_\chi=
\exists x\bigl(\operatorname{Outside}_A(x)\land\chi(x)\bigr).
\tag{42}
\]

**Theorem TR-15 (relative metasyntactic non-definability).** No \(L_A\) sentence or finite combination of its **inside-A interpreted operators** can express \(\psi_\chi\) correctly in both full worlds. The open metalanguage \(L_{A+}\) can construct \(\psi_\chi\) as a well-typed **unresolved question**.

**Proof.** By induction on the grammar, any \(L_A\) term/sentence has equal interpretation/truth in the two structures because their A-reducts agree. The formula \(\psi_\chi\) is false in \(w_0\) and true in \(w_1\). It therefore cannot be equivalent to an \(L_A\) sentence in that model pair. The OutsideQuestion constructor belongs to \(L_{A+}\), so it builds the expression, **without deciding which situation is actual**. \(\square\)

**Scope of noncomposability:** this is a **language-relative** theorem. It says finite object-language composition under the stipulated inside-A type system cannot produce a **semantically interpreted outside-A term**. An unrestricted Turing machine can of course execute code that prints the symbols of the larger language. Once a former closure-only interface is extended with interpreted OutsideQuestion, it **is no longer the same closed-language comparator**.

### 6.25. Theorem TR-16 — an actual computable metasyntactic constructor exists

For finitely encoded \(\mathcal P,q,\rho,\sigma,h_r\), define the report transformer:

\[
\operatorname{Reflect}(\mathcal P,q,h_r)=
\left[
\operatorname{Embed}(a_r),\
\operatorname{OpenFrontier}(\mathcal P,q,\rho,\sigma),\
\operatorname{OutsideQuestion}(A,q,\exists)
\right].
\tag{43}
\]

**Theorem TR-16 (effective metasyntactic constructor).** Given well-typed finite inputs and a fixed finite grammar, Reflect is a **total computable function that outputs syntax**. A correct implementation marks the outside question UNDETERMINED until some qualified evidence or domain-specific theorem settles it. It need not enumerate any member of A′ or classify all possible input types.

**Proof.** A finite procedure copies the task and run references, creates the three finite typed nodes, and serializes the result. No branch calls a universal semantic truth procedure. \(\square\)

This formalizes **existence of the different program**. Its generated statement about A′ is an *interpreted instruction/message*, not a claim that the actual hidden constituents have been identified. Two implementations may emit different numbers of code bytes, but no theorem of **minimum program-length growth** follows: ordinary universal machines can encode both programs.

### 6.26. Theorem TR-17 — a perfect semantic label-of-newness is another undecidable task

Suppose we demand that the program generate a new semantic category **exactly if** an arbitrarily encountered response behaviour is genuinely different from every existing behaviour category. This is **semantic program equivalence**, not the easy task of comparing two finite output strings.

A fixed family of **total computable functions** already defeats universal exact labeling. Let \(U_e(e)\) be an arbitrary diagonal computation and define

\[
F_e(n)=
\begin{cases}
1,& U_e(e)\text{ halts within }n\text{ simulation steps},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\qquad Z(n)=0.
\tag{44}
\]

Each \(F_e\) is computable and **total**, since it runs only n simulation steps. However,

\[
\forall n\,F_e(n)=Z(n)
\iff U_e(e)\uparrow.
\tag{45}
\]

**Theorem TR-17 (impossibility of uniform perfect semantic novelty classification).** There is no computable **total** classifier which, from arbitrary e in the above family, correctly decides whether \(F_e\) and the previously known category Z are **semantically identical functions**, or whether a genuinely new semantic behaviour has appeared.

**Proof.** The alleged classifier would decide the complement of the diagonal halting set by (45), hence would decide halting by complementing its Boolean result. Contradiction. \(\square\)

A program can nevertheless mint fresh **syntactic** labels NEW-1, NEW-2, ... or a catch-all UNCLASSIFIED. That is straightforward and does **not** mean it correctly determined semantic novelty. In particular, requiring a perfect semantic distinction between IND-1 and IND-2 at every nesting level reproduces an undecidable classification obligation. TR-17 is **new work derived here** from standard halting theory, not an original theorem from *Primalidad y Lógica*.

### 6.29. Russell and Gödel: a metaproof can expose an object-level limit without inheriting it

The required distinction is **object calculus versus metatheoretical statement about that calculus versus physical machine carrying the statements**. A meta-operation may report an object-level contradiction or impossibility without itself accepting the offending object-level axiom.

**Russell.** Let \(T_{\rm naive}\) assert unrestricted set comprehension. For \(R=\{x:x\notin x\}\) it yields

\[
R\in R\iff R\notin R.
\tag{49}
\]

This is an inconsistency **of the stipulated classical object theory**, not evidence that a separate finite proof checker, a sheet of paper, or a processor has suffered an analogous inconsistency. In classical logic, inconsistent premises can entail arbitrary sentences *inside that calculus* (explosion); the program detecting that condition need not import those premises into **its own** inference environment.

**TR-18 — finite meta-certificate and typed non-propagation.** Fix an object calculus T with an effective finite proof-checking relation \(\operatorname{Check}_T(\pi,\varphi)\). Suppose the meta-process obtains proof objects \(\pi_+,\pi_-\) such that

\[
\operatorname{Check}_T(\pi_+,\varphi)=1,\qquad
\operatorname{Check}_T(\pi_-,\neg\varphi)=1.
\tag{50}
\]

It can emit the **single meta-level certificate**

\[
\operatorname{INCONSISTENT\_OBJECT\_THEORY}
(T,\varphi,\pi_+,\pi_-).
\tag{51}
\]

**Proof.** The checked proof objects establish \(T\vdash\varphi\) and \(T\vdash\neg\varphi\), hence the defined T is syntactically inconsistent. The checker publishes the **attributed fact that T proves both sentences**, rather than asserting the contradictory pair as free-standing truths of the receiving calculus. Under the explicit typed rule that proof-from-T does **not** become proof-in-the-host, explosion is not transmitted into the host by the certificate alone. \(\square\)

This is a conditional **typing and verification** result, not a global proof that all metalanguages, monitors or processors are themselves consistent. The historical [*Formalización del Meta Sistema*](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1cKahUwPzTnMLJSZ6dO1syFl7PocXpSH2LC158tUYsCk/edit) includes the author's programme of non-explosion and positioning; the separate [R05 audit, §§4 and 14.3](https://github.com/dakleyer/structural-awareness-workbench/blob/main/02-workstreams/ecosystem-awareness/07-field-notes/REVIEW_R05_FORMALIZACION_META_SISTEMA_v0.1.md) distinguishes a meta-checker's established result from the original producer's unresolved truth, without importing the historical manuscript's stronger unverified global claims.

**Gödel.** Effective coding of formulas and finite proof verification permit a metalanguage to establish an incompleteness theorem for an effectively axiomatized, consistent, sufficiently arithmetically expressive theory. Gödel's proof is a valid *metatheoretical argument about provability*, not evidence that its metalanguage is absolutely complete or can decide arbitrary arithmetic truths. See [Stanford Encyclopedia: Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/goedel-incompleteness/) and [Russell's Paradox](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/russell-paradox/).

**Operational principle:** an effective agent can safely publish a *qualified diagnosis of a theory's limits* while preserving the distinct UNKNOWN status of the object question. The diagnosis is **A of the checking task** and need not be an answer to the original task.

### 6.30. Resource-unrestricted formal model versus bounded realizations

The abstract multi-level model can be typed as

\[
\mathfrak M=(L_o,L_m,\iota,
\operatorname{Quote},\operatorname{CheckProof},
\operatorname{OpenFrontier},\operatorname{Interpret}),
\tag{52}
\]

where \(L_o\) is the object language, \(L_m\) a declared meta-language and \(\iota\) their relevant interpretation relation. This mathematical model may quantify over arbitrarily large formal domains *in defining a question*; it has **no physical wall-clock or budget imposed by its syntax**. It does **not** thereby possess a global semantic truth function, a consistency proof for itself or a way to evaluate every external existential claim.

An embodied agent is a different object:

\[
\mathfrak I=(\mathrm{Encoder},\mathrm{Kernel},
\mathrm{Reflector},\mathrm{Verifier},
\mathrm{Channel},\rho,\kappa),
\tag{53}
\]

where \(\rho\) fixes time, memory, compute, communication and evidence budgets and \(\kappa\) fixes source validity, scope and authority. Its metalanguage operators are implemented by **ordinary finite computable subroutines** with possible resource exhaustion.

An idealized mathematician may reason about a domain not exhausted by a finite page. The real person still has finite cognitive and physical resources. Neither fact licenses the claim that the abstract model answers every question about its domains.

**Completeness of representation** here means the schema can carry a finite qualified object-result *and* its unclosed semantic obligation without claiming a complete enumeration. It is not **Gödelian deductive completeness**, a complete truth predicate or a method for computing all unknown members.

### 6.31. TR-19 — compilation into an ordinary syntactic machine

Let P be an effectively described object-level program and M an effectively described reporting/metasyntactic program that consumes P's finite output and run trace. A universal machine can simulate both, with their handoff:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Run}(C(\langle P\rangle,\langle M\rangle),x)
\simeq
\operatorname{Run}(M\circ P,x)
}
\tag{54}
\]

on inputs for which the specified computations terminate, where \(\simeq\) means equal observable outputs under the same reporting contract.

**TR-19 (compiler existence).** A computable compiler C producing the composite ordinary-machine program exists for any finite encodings of effective P and M.

**Proof.** Emit P's instructions followed by M's instructions and effective message/trace handoff, or a universal interpreter for both descriptions. The resulting ordinary machine simulates the same effective steps and emits the same output. \(\square\)

This is **an exact computational equivalence claim**, not a denial that P and M occupy *different syntactic and metasyntactic roles within the programme*. TR-15 still establishes an outside-A expression is not definable **using only the original closed language \(L_A\)**. The compiled C program implementing \(L_{A+}\) has **expanded the output language**, even though its processor runs conventional code.

A fully copied compiled artifact can reproduce the same outputs; if the **same executable and environment** are held fixed, the run resources can also match. It does not follow that the copying process generated the **design rationale, applicable semantics, proof obligations or choice of design for a new context**. Those are separate stages, not distinguished by an execution-only benchmark.

### 6.32. Design, selection, checking, compiling and running are different operations

Fix a declared context c and intended question \(q_c\). The complete workflow is

\[
\operatorname{Design}(c,q_c,\kappa_c)\leadsto
(s_c,\mathrm{obligations}_c),
\quad
\operatorname{Check}(s_c,\kappa_c)\leadsto\mathrm{status},
\]

\[
\operatorname{Compile}(s_c)\leadsto p_c,
\qquad
\operatorname{Execute}(p_c,x)\leadsto y.
\tag{55}
\]

Design **constructs or selects what should be done**, with the intended meaning and evidence obligations. Compilation translates a chosen design into executable instructions; execution applies the chosen instructions to a case. Copying a known valid \(p_c\) can have no discovery burden for that context if the design and its applicability are already supplied. **Choosing the appropriate \(p_c\)** from an ambiguous library, or creating one for a newly changed question, is another problem.

If a purportedly "syntactic-only" conventional program itself knows how to inspect a new context, generate the required metasyntactic specification and validate it, it **implements the metalevel task functionally**, whatever its source language and naming. It cannot be used as a strict closure-only comparator while simultaneously being credited with the open-aware expressive operation it was defined to prohibit.

### 6.33. The pivotal condition: A′ is not the complement inside the initialized syntax U

The semantic extension would be trivial if a purported metasyn​​tax merely computed the complement of a result **inside a domain that the initializer has already admitted**. To make the comparison precise, keep three objects separate:

- \(\Omega_q\): the **mathematically modeled, q-relevant source ecosystem**, which the agent need not be able to enumerate or fully encode. Its claimed properties require their **own semantic premises**.
- \(U_r\): the **typed syntactic scope admitted by initializer K** for run r, containing the descriptions which K can admit at that point.
- \(A_r\subseteq U_r\): the **qualified result/recognized descriptions actually produced by the composed procedure** during that run. This is the comparison's run-product sense of A, not §1's \(A_{\rm proc}\).

Let \(\operatorname{den}_r:U_r\rightharpoonup\Omega_q\) be a **possibly partial interpretation** of admitted descriptions as q-relevant actual-world constituents. Neither \(U_r\) nor \(\operatorname{den}_r\) is assumed globally complete or effectively reversible.

The **internal complement** of the run product is

\[
R_r^{\mathrm{in}}=U_r\setminus A_r .
\tag{56}
\]

This may be decidable when \(U_r\) and \(A_r\) are given explicit finite lists, but it can only concern **referents admitted into U**. It does not even have the correct type to assert an external-world statement.

The **external semantic obligation**, by contrast, is about the region **not represented by the initializer**:

\[
\Phi_r^{\mathrm{out}}(q):\quad
\exists x\in\Omega_q\
\bigl[x\notin\operatorname{den}_r(U_r)
\ \land\operatorname{Relevant}_q(x)\bigr].
\tag{57}
\]

This is a **well-typed metalanguage formula**. It is neither an algorithm for computing \(\Omega_q\setminus\operatorname{den}_r(U_r)\) nor a discovery of a particular x. In the open A′ representation, \(\Phi_r^{\rm out}\) may be transmitted as an **UNDETERMINED external question** even when its truth has not been established, or as a **PROVED external fact** when an independent premise justifies it.

**Necessary comparison boundary:** both the ordinary syntactic and the augmented metasyntactic programme still run on finite processors and finite codes. Strict superiority is **relative to the report grammar confined to U**, not to the class of all Turing-computable programmes. If a so-called conventional programme already has the same well-typed external relation and source assumptions, it has implemented the open-aware representation under another name.

### 6.34. TR-20 — the U-complement trap cannot recover an exterior statement

**Theorem TR-20 (internal complements do not express external scope).** Let \(L_U\) be the strictly U-relativized language of §6.23, extended by any finite number of operators \(h_j\) whose semantic interpretations and quantifiers depend **only on the admitted U-reduct**. This includes arbitrarily nested Boolean/set operations, in-U complements, inside-U indeterminate tags and their computable compositions. If two admissible worlds have isomorphic admitted U-reducts but different truth values for \(\Phi^{\rm out}_r(q)\), no \(L_U\) sentence or term composition is equivalent to \(\Phi^{\rm out}_r(q)\) on both worlds.

**Proof.** By induction on the syntax, all atomic in-U claims have the same interpretation in both worlds; composition and quantification over the common U preserve indistinguishability. Any inside-U complement \(U\setminus A_r\) is also the same, because U and A are the same. The external formula has different truth values by hypothesis and cannot be represented by any of those expressions. \(\square\)

**Concrete witness.** Take \(U_r=A_r=\{u_1\}\). In \(w_0\), the only relevant constituent is \(\operatorname{den}(u_1)\). In \(w_1\), the same \(u_1\) is present **plus another relevant x with no representation in U**. Both give the internal complement \(\varnothing\) and exactly the same K/D outcomes. Yet (57) is false in \(w_0\) and true in \(w_1\). **Even an apparently complete internal A leaves the outside question open.**

This proves the precise failure of the syntactic shortcut \(A'=U\setminus A\). It does **not** prove that some higher-level syntactic programme using independent source information could never construct a reference to an exterior: doing so would use **a richer interpreted grammar or an added semantic premise**, which is exactly the proposed metasyntactic change.

### 6.35. Three distinct kinds of open-world premises

To move from the *ability to ask* about the exterior to the justified assertion **that something outside U actually exists**, we must state the evidence source. The original R03 *Primalidad y Lógica* §4–5 **postulates** an ND/IND exterior property, but its finite subset-label counting does **not** by itself prove a new physical agent outside the initialization of any real robot. Similarly, OW-1 proves a **nonempty uniform semantic non-decision frontier**, which need not be a physically **unrepresented** peer outside U: undecidable semantic questions may be **encoded within U**.

Use three non-interchangeable premises:

**H-OUT — independently justified exterior:** for the *actual declared task and run scope*, an admissible semantic model/certificate establishes \(\Phi_r^{\rm out}(q)\). Then the agent may correctly send a nonempty-outside-U **existence meta-claim** without knowing the witness identity.

**H-GROWTH — unbounded diversity with bounded syntactic admission:** \(\Omega_q\) contains more distinct relevant constituents than any finite \(U_r\) can denote. If \(\operatorname{den}_r(U_r)\) is finite, this premise **implies H-OUT for that run** by cardinality. It does not imply the agent can physically reach or communicate with all those constituents.

**H-REACH — effective exploration coverage:** for each finite n there exists an admissible finite exploration stage t at which the exploration process has discovered **at least n** distinct q-relevant constituents:

\[
\forall n\in\mathbb N\
\exists t<\infty:\quad
|\operatorname{Discovered}_{\le t}|\ge n .
\tag{58}
\]

This is **strictly stronger than saying the external population is unbounded**. It describes the particular explorer's eventual reachability under a qualified exploration policy. No uniform bound on t in terms of n follows without an additional rate or detection assumption.

**A fourth, contract-specific condition H-DETECT** is needed to diagnose a failed initialization from an empty result: a valid, testable source/coverage contract guarantees K **must detect at least one relevant agent by an identified deadline \(t_0\)** under the satisfied premises of this run. Merely assuming H-GROWTH or H-REACH does **not** establish H-DETECT at an arbitrary short deadline.

These distinctions allow a model to be **mathematically open**, an agent to **know a qualified exterior-existence proposition**, and a specific exploration still to be **operationally empty**, without a logical contradiction.

### 6.36. TR-21 — the metasyn​​tactic agent can state true existence beyond its own U

**Theorem TR-21 (conditional external-existence witness without identification).** Let K and D produce a finite \(U_r\) and \(A_r\) in a source ecosystem. If a justified H-OUT certificate/axiom for that exact \((q,U_r,\Omega_q)\) has been supplied, then a metasyn​​tactic finite programme can correctly construct a statement of the form

\[
\operatorname{CERTIFIED\_EXTERIOR}
\bigl(q,\langle U_r\rangle,\mathrm{source}(H_{\rm OUT})\bigr),
\tag{59}
\]

and assert \(\Phi_r^{\rm out}(q)\) even when **no member of \(\Omega_q\setminus\operatorname{den}(U_r)\) has been individually observed, identified or admitted**.

**Proof.** The qualified premise entails the existential statement (57) for the stated interpretation. A finite programme can copy the target/scope/source reference and construct the certificate-bearing meta-expression, without evaluating the existential statement by scanning \(\Omega_q\). Source justification comes from the stated external premise, **not** from the report constructor's computational power. \(\square\)

This is a real **scope-crossing semantic operation** relative to the strictly U-only report language. It is not a new extensional-world observation produced from the same U trace, nor proof that a general robot possesses H-OUT merely because an operator named OPEN exists. Without the qualification, the programme can still emit the **well-typed external question with status UNDETERMINED**, but cannot assert its affirmative truth.

### 6.37. TR-22 — the robot that sees nobody: source-of-error localization

Suppose K's initialization observes no eligible agent:

\[
U_r=\varnothing,\qquad A_r=\varnothing .
\tag{60}
\]

There are three importantly different diagnosis levels:

1. **Only an open-world design declaration:** report NO_AGENT_OBSERVED and WORLD_COVERAGE_NOT_CERTIFIED. This is not proof that a physically eligible peer is present.
2. **An independent H-OUT or qualified actual-existence premise:** report EXISTS_RELEVANT_OUTSIDE_INITIALIZED_U and K_NOT_EXHAUSTIVE_FOR_q. K's empty result is **not a truthful full census**. It can still be correct as a **run-local observation**.
3. **An additional H-DETECT finite-time detection guarantee whose preconditions are met:** now K's empty output **violates its own coverage/detection contract**. The metaprocess may emit INIT_CONTRACT_VIOLATION, backed by the specific evidence and guarantee. It still does not identify whether the fault is a sensor failure, filter mistake, authorization problem, communications outage, bad configuration or invalid environmental assumption without more evidence.

**Theorem TR-22 (sound diagnosis of missing scope versus actual initializer failure).** An agent possessing a valid H-OUT certificate can soundly reject the inference "the relevant ecosystem contains no agents" from (60). An agent additionally possessing an applicable H-DETECT contract can soundly reject "K met its detection contract" if K returned no qualifying agent by that contract's deadline.

**Proof.** H-OUT contradicts the claimed **exhaustive absence**, not the finite observation report. H-DETECT implies a nonempty qualifying output by its deadline when its preconditions hold; (60) negates that consequent. A bounded checker can verify the declared contract application and missing output, then emit a contract-violation meta-result. \(\square\)

**No epistemic oracle:** the diagnostics establish *what the admitted premises and reported measurements imply*, not the identity, location, eventual behaviour or reachability of an unseen peer. H-REACH's "eventually discoverable" guarantee alone is compatible with zero discoveries at the present run, because its quantified finite discovery time may be later and has no supplied bound.

### 6.38. Operational metasyn​​tax beyond U without literal complement enumeration

The derived report constructor can take the form:

~~~text
REFLECT_BEYOND_INITIALIZED_U(
    object_kernel_trace, U_scope,
    question, qualified_environment_axioms,
    finite_deadline_contracts
):
    retain ALL already qualified in-U observations
    keep in-U unknowns and damaged/timeout evidence distinct
    create OUTSIDE_U_QUESTION(question, U_scope), truth = UNDETERMINED
    if verified H-OUT applies to THIS U and question:
        append CERTIFIED_EXTERIOR with source/proof reference
        assert NOT_GLOBALLY_EXHAUSTIVE(U_scope, question)
    if verified H-DETECT applies and its deadline elapsed:
        if required qualified detections are absent:
            append INIT_CONTRACT_VIOLATION
    preserve OPEN_FRONTIER for the general task
    do NOT enumerate U-complement as A-prime
    do NOT manufacture outside identities or nonlocal truth values
    return typed_meta_report
~~~

**This changes the executable programme.** It adds actual typed AST constructors, premise qualification and scope/audit checks. It does **not** merely calculate \(U_r\setminus A_r\), nor does it use a semantic external truth oracle. It can evaluate its own input's qualified provenance and output a **meta-statement** about the initializer's representation.

**Implementation warning, not a reduction of the abstract theory:** an ordinary Turing machine can compile the above operation into ordinary syntax (TR-19). The strictly closed comparator lacks the required **interpreted output types or external semantic premises**, not a mythical hardware capability. Correctly implementing the same meta-types in another conventional codebase moves it outside the strictly U-closed comparison class.

### 6.39. What "strictly stronger" now asserts — and what remains an experiment

The robust theorem combination is:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
U_r\setminus A_r\ \text{does not recover the outside-U relation (TR-20)}\\
\text{H-OUT supports a true meta-existence claim without identity (TR-21)}\\
\text{H-DETECT plus failed detection supports a bounded initializer fault claim (TR-22)}
\end{array}
}
\tag{61}
\]

There are now **two independent axes** in the comparison:

- **Expressive/representational power:** when the H-OUT statement is justified, an open-aware, H-OUT-bearing programme can assert a **true fact about a semantic exterior that a strictly U-bound language cannot even express**. The gain is not limited to U's internal complement or to the names of observed indeterminates.
- **Operational detection/correction:** the programme can **sometimes** turn this external meta-fact into a concrete coverage contradiction or a verified detection-contract violation. Whether that leads to better discovery, correction, safety, throughput or cost requires a task-specific implementable follow-up operation and evidence. It does **not** follow from expressivity alone that K can be repaired or a missing peer found.

**Falsifiers.** A counterexample to a proposed actual-world gain may show no valid H-OUT evidence; a supposed "outside" candidate already expressible in U; a source whose actual ecosystem is finite and wholly inside U; a sensor correct for its declared limited scope; H-DETECT not applicable at the stated time; an existing baseline already carrying an equivalent external claim; or resource costs of the meta-operation exceeding its task benefit. Those are **applied model/implementation scope failures**, not refutations of TR-20's two-model language theorem.

**Research status:** TR-20 is a relative-definability result, TR-21 a conditional qualified-inference construction, TR-22 a conditional audit proposition. H-OUT/H-GROWTH/H-REACH/H-DETECT must **not** be inferred universally from OW-1 or from the original R03 combinatorial label-count theorem. The distinct practical question is whether the initialization agent can obtain reliable external scope premises and exploit them economically; those are **not automatically available by running more in-U syntax**.

## 7. Relationship to the established A/B/C/D vocabulary

This note deliberately reserves \(A_{\mathrm{proc}}\) for **the procedure**, to avoid conflating it with the adopted **00M A**, which means **the result actually delivered by a functional process**. A source execution's finite results may be canonical **A** for that execution; its known evaluation basis and reserve may be **B**; a grounded route to explore uncharacterized questions may be **C**; and effects beyond effective evaluation may be **D**, subject to the canonical source's process/question/time conditions. **A′ is a pre-taxonomic description of the unclosed semantic obligation, not a declaration that all A′ is D**. UNKNOWN remains appropriate when even the role assignment lacks justification.

The source's open frontier and the receiving ecosystem's open frontier need not coincide: **ABCD roles are assigned again relative to the receiving process**, not inherited by sharing a label. This is consistent with the current [01J signalling/semantic coupling](../ecosystem-awareness/baseline/01J_ECOSYSTEM_SIGNALLING_SELECTIVE_DISCLOSURE_AND_CHOREOGRAPHED_REPOSITIONING_v0.1.md), which preserves compatibility limits and does not promote handshake success to global semantic equivalence.

## 8. Position before the Projection Mechanism

TR-1–TR-3 establish the **pre-projection transfer limit and conditional soundness**; TR-4–TR-9 add **weak information dominance**, a strict finite-gain witness, non-total B/C/D boundaries, information equivalence and relative maximality. TR-10–TR-14 compare the **same effective K/D composite** under closed versus open semantic contracts, establish the meta-oracle barrier, and formalize an **epistemically complete reporting envelope** that can represent unexhausted meaning without pretending to identify its instances. Together they build the **pre-projection architectural-principles** result: why transferring only executions is semantically insufficient, why closed-result export can convey a false generalization, and how A/A′ plus a legitimate interpretation can transmit the **intended task and its open reach** without claiming to decide everything.

The separate [2025 *Projection Mechanism*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393802750_The_Projection_Mechanism_How_Practical_Incompleteness_Infiltrates_Formal_Systems) and [2026 fourth revision](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1OrJ2LWz2qBv2sP8_-VNldWpctxZdwcfI/view) ask what happens when an unqualified representation is imported and operated upon as if it carried sufficient meaning. The *Projected Infinite Paradox* provides a historical motivation for distinguishing named finite constructions and population-level or limiting questions, but its broader ontological claims are not assumed as theorems here.

The next, distinct research question is **projection under an unqualified import of a source result**, not whether the receiver has a way to preserve the A/A′ distinction.
