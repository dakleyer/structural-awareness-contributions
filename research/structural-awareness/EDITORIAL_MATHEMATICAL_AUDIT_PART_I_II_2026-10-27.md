## 3. Formal claim classification

**Mathematically standard, proved in its stated specialization (S):** observational fiber criterion, inclusion of observation refinements, finite pair separation/weighted set cover under source eligibility and additive costs, robust worst-case monotonicity under reduction of a compatible state set, group-orbit invariant factorization, compiler/simulator existence for effective programmes.

**Conditional formal consequences (D):** the H-EXP uniform semantic-decision frontier; the two-world proof of outside-U undefinability within a genuinely restricted language; a type-neutral exterior for every finite frame **when the model satisfies ND**; qualified external-existence reports when an appropriate theorem/source premise applies; source-relative typed error non-propagation; a finite witness of strict decision-information gain and an anonymous robust minimax gain under specified valid signals.

**Finite programme construction (R/implementation existence):** properly typed metasyntactic expression constructors and qualified status/proof envelopes. "The programme can construct a question" is distinct from "the programme can answer the question." Output constructors do not make the source evidence or arbitrary mathematical premises true.

**Not proved / hypotheses (H):** prevalence of positive MU-class states in physical/open production systems; an effective global discovery guarantee for actual agents; automatic repair of failed sensors; any overall latency/energy/euros advantage; net superiority over a source-qualified strong conventional baseline; all B/C/D borders semantically decidable; actual external peer existence from ND alone; a universal correct semantic novelty classifier.

Part II's abstract applicability theorem MU-8 is an **existence of a positive structural problem class**, not a theorem that an arbitrary source or agent already has an eligible m. The size extension is only to **appropriately scoped** models with no new relevant states invalidating the original receiving task.

## 4. Explicit proof obligations before scientific promotion

A third party should verify all of the following independently:

- **Formal hypotheses:** H-EXP for the exact effective peer family and semantic target; H-REQ only where meta-decidability of an entire response language is invoked; ND for the explicitly interpreted \(\Omega\), not presumed for any real physical environment.
- **Interpretation and typing:** whether object and metalanguage clauses are well-formed, whether outside-U claims are really unavailable in the comparator grammar, whether the report's source question and finite frame are correctly specified, and whether a meta-proof asserts only *about T* rather than importing T's contradiction.
- **Receiving questions:** r must be defined **before** choosing m. Investigate whether any reported improvement is a tautology induced by coding m equal to r without an actual qualified source.
- **Minimal auxiliary observations:** check \(U_{r\mid o}\), not every r-conflicting pair regardless of source o; privileges and source eligibility must be evidence-backed.
- **Partial semantics:** verify source-to-receiver maps preserve the *specific* question and do not transfer owner rights merely because two paths compose.
- **Cost and baselines:** use the same available observations, rights, actions, budgets and independent referee; include acquisition, verification, transmission, compute, design and changed-context costs.
- **Countermodels:** a finite universe fully represented, a robot with zero peers in an infinite non-agent universe, a constant OPEN tag, already sufficient o, irrelevant m, false/expired m, wrong task or source scope, and a conventional implementation with the same certified meta-information.

## 6. Editorial result and remaining limits

- **Reading order:** Part I definitions → precise limits → finite meta constructor → logical isolation → transfer; only then Part II's decision tasks and utility theorem family.
- **Archive and reproducibility:** prior working derivations are preserved, linked and non-active. They are available for challenging a detailed proof without burdening every first-time reader with 39 chronological subsections.
- **Terminology:** consistently use **metasyntax / metasyntactic**, **object-level**, **meta-level**, **model-relative**, **qualified evidence**, **strict expressivity**, **weak information dominance** and **conditional utility**. Terms such as "oracle", "complete", "superior", "outside" and "unknown" are always scoped to a declared question and model.
- **Research boundary:** theoretical paper closure does not make the Part II cost/efficiency/real-world applicability hypotheses proved. The next mathematical step is an exact, permission-qualified problem generator and an independently referee'd task family; the next engineering step is comparative verification/latency/authority instrumentation against competent incumbents.

**Audit verdict:** Parts I and II are now **coherently separated as mathematical research outputs with an inspectable chain of proofs and evidence boundaries**. No claim is made of independent certification, universal physical exterior, or globally superior architecture. The broad utility class has **conditional strict mathematical witnesses**; positive net real-world utility remains a separately testable proposition.

These are not ten independent original breakthroughs; most are established mathematics properly transported to an authorial programme, and the [source ledger](./METASYNTACTIC_UTILITY_SOURCE_PROVENANCE_v0.1.md) identifies whose claims are whose.

**Current mathematical conclusion:** true, legitimate metasyntactic information can be **useful without individual identification** by improving a target's conditional information, reducing the verifier work required to establish an already-entailed fact, or preserving the right context for an inference. This is stronger than "we added an UNKNOWN tag" but weaker than the unproved "every implementation is useful." No new simulations, synthetic populations, CI, field measurements or social/EA architectural mappings are performed as part of this revision. Older finite fixtures remain historical and separate.

**Remaining mathematical challenges:** challenge the independent qualification of M; for Semantic Window check the estimator class and bias-vs-variance direction; for R12 distinguish prior proof existence from legal/current applicability; for R14 check support/KL direction; for R11 forbid count-to-entropy substitution; and for PR #60 fix incremental Q coverage relative to the existing o.
